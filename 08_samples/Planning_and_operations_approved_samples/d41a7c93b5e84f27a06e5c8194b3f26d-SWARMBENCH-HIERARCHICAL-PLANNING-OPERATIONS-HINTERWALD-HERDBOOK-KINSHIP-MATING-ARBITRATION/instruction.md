Our breeding committee sits on 1 March to settle this year's matings, and I am the one who has to put the papers together. A hundred living Hinterwälder across eighteen member holdings, fourteen breeding places free between them, and three review boards who will not agree with each other. Genetics wants the animals whose lines are thinnest. The vets have restrictions on a good number of those same animals. Movement has to get a bull onto a lorry, over the Swiss border in one case, and through isolation at the far end. Every year the mating that looks obvious on the kinship figures turns out to be the one somebody else has already ruled out, and every year that argument happens in the room because nobody wrote it down beforehand. Not this time. I want the disagreements on paper, attributed, with the constraint that actually decided each one.

Work in `/logs/agent`. `/input_artifacts/mating_charter.md` is what governs the round: the unknown-founder rule, how kinship and mean kinship are worked out, how a visit note is read to a standing on the round date, the eligibility order, the constraints on a mating, how a movement's certificate, transport category and isolation follow, and the vocabulary for recording a conflict. Where it parts company with general practice, go with the charter. Alongside it you have `/input_artifacts/herdbook_pedigree.csv` (the whole extract, living animals and their ancestors), `/input_artifacts/member_holdings.csv`, `/input_artifacts/weight_records.csv`, `/input_artifacts/previous_matings.csv`, `/input_artifacts/movement_rules.csv`, and the veterinary correspondence under `/input_artifacts/herd_health_notes/`, a file per holding with an index at `/input_artifacts/herd_health_notes/index.md`. `/input_artifacts/source_manifest.json` says where all of it came from and what it is not.

Fair warning on two of those. The pedigree is a real herdbook extract and it carries real herdbook problems: blank parents, a parent named that appears nowhere as a record of its own, an animal whose recorded sex sits oddly beside the parent role it fills. Do not tidy them away. The charter says how each is handled, and the handling changes who is related to whom, which changes everything downstream. The health notes, meanwhile, are simply what the vet wrote on the day. They contradict each other on purpose. A restriction gets placed, lifted, placed again; some carry an end date that has already gone by; some were written at the April and May visits, ahead of rechecks that have not happened yet. What an animal's standing is on 1 March is whatever survives the charter's rules, worked out separately for each restriction it carries. An animal can be perfectly fit to breed and still unfit to travel.

Six files, at exactly these paths and names.

`/logs/agent/kinship_register.csv`, one row per living animal, columns `animal_id,sex,age_class,holding,inbreeding_coefficient,mean_kinship,mk_rank,founder_share,breeding_status,active_restrictions,evidence_note`. Put the pipe-separated set of restrictions standing on the round date in `active_restrictions`, or `none` where there are none. For `evidence_note` give me the vet's own words, quoted exactly, from the statement that actually settles that animal's breeding standing rather than some nearby sentence about it. Leave it empty where nothing in the file bears on the question.

`/logs/agent/mating_allocations.csv`, columns `allocation_id,cow_id,bull_id,pair_kinship,expected_progeny_f,host_holding,cow_mk_rank,bull_mk_rank,displaced_alternative_id,selection_rationale`. Use the places if the herd will support it. Whatever goes in `displaced_alternative_id` has to be an animal that genuinely could have taken that slot, not just the next name down the list, and the rationale wants arguing from that allocation's own figures: name the pair, say what the pairing reads on kinship, and say what the alternative would have read instead, so a member can see the comparison that was actually made rather than take my word that one was.

`/logs/agent/movement_plan.csv`, columns `movement_id,animal_id,from_holding,to_holding,movement_reason,allocation_id,receiving_health_status,certificate_class,transport_category,isolation_days,arrival_capacity_after,evidence_note`. Every movement the round creates and nothing it does not. `movement_reason` is `SIRE_PLACEMENT` or `ISOLATION_RELIEF`. Where an animal is carrying an extended isolation, quote the vet on it in `evidence_note`; otherwise leave that empty too.

`/logs/agent/board_conflicts.csv`, columns `conflict_id,animal_id,mk_rank,genetics_position,veterinary_position,movement_position,binding_constraint,arbitrated_outcome,resolution_note`. A row for each animal the charter counts as a conflict. What I need in the three position columns is each board's view of that particular animal, its rank, its restrictions, where it stands, not a description of what the boards generally concern themselves with. Write them the way a board member would say them out loud, in sentences, with the ear tag and the figure each board is arguing from; a status code on its own tells the room nothing it cannot already see in the register. The resolution note is the part I will be read back at me, so say in a couple of sentences what actually settled it for that animal, and put the thing it turned on in there: the partner that was ruled out, the holding that had no place left, the calvings behind a rest year, the age it reached this spring.

`/logs/agent/herd_dashboard.html`, one self-contained page the members can follow off a screen: where conservation priority sits across the whole living herd, the matings the round commits, and where the conflicts fall. Draw the priority picture, do not tabulate it, and give every animal its own visible mark inside the drawing carrying `data-animal-id`. Embed the three tables behind it as JSON in `<script type="application/json">` blocks with the ids `kinship-data`, `allocation-data` and `conflict-data`. They need to match what you actually filed, because somebody always asks, and I am not reconciling two versions of the same number in front of the committee.

`/logs/agent/conservation_plan.md`, 1,800 to 3,200 words, under the headings `Round outcome`, `Conservation standing`, `Control totals`, `Board conflicts`, `Movements and isolation`, `Allocation decisions` and `Provenance and limitations`. Under Control totals give me a two-column Markdown table keyed exactly `living_animals`, `breeding_eligible`, `allocations_made`, `places_committed`, `movements_planned`, `cross_border_movements`, `animals_under_restriction` and `conflicts_recorded`. Go through the herd animal by animal where it matters, naming ear tags and setting each one beside a fact the record confirms for that same animal, rather than writing about the herd in general terms. Make the case for every mating you are putting forward. On limitations, be straight about the founder convention, the herdbook's own defects, the 1 March cutoff, and the fact that more than one round satisfies every constraint.

Those six need to tell the same story: same identifiers, same decisions, same totals.

Papers go round the members the evening before, and what goes round is whatever is sitting in `/logs/agent` under those six names. Something called `final_allocations.csv` is not the allocation table, and a numbered intermediate is not the file it came from; neither gets picked up. Keep working copies in `/workspace`. Finish the job rather than the analysis: write all six out, then list the directory and open each one to satisfy yourself it is whole. Anything missing or half-written when you stop simply does not reach the meeting, and the round does not happen, whatever the thinking behind it was worth.
