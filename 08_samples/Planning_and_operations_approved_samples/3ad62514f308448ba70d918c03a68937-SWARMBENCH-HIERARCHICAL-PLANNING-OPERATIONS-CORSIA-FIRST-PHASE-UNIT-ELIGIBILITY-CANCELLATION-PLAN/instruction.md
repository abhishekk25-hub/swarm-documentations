I buy carbon for a five-airline group and the audit committee sits on the 30th. We have 4,000,000 tonnes to cancel against our 2024 to 2026 CORSIA offsetting requirement, the brokers have put 221 blocks of credit in front of us, and I am not signing a cancellation instruction on the strength of a sales sheet. Twice now a seller has told me a block was CORSIA eligible and meant something else by it.

Work in `/logs/agent`. Keep scratch files in `/workspace`.

What governs is `/input_artifacts/sources/ICAO-CEEU-2026-04.txt`, the Council's own document listing the emissions unit programmes it has approved and, for each one, the eligibility timeframe, the eligible unit dates and the scope of eligibility with its exclusions. The PDF it was taken from is beside it as `/input_artifacts/sources/ICAO-CEEU-2026-04.pdf` if you would rather read it laid out. `/input_artifacts/sources/ICAO-TAB-SUMMARY-2026-04.txt` is the advisory body's summary of which programmes hold approval for which period, and `/input_artifacts/sources/ICAO-EUC-DOC09.txt` is the criteria the Council assesses a programme against. `/input_artifacts/sources.json` says where each of those came from and carries a checksum, so quote from the file rather than from memory of the scheme.

The blocks are in `/input_artifacts/candidate_pool.csv`, one row per block, which is a project and a vintage year and the tonnage still unretired on that vintage, with the registry record for the activity beside it. `/input_artifacts/activity_dossiers.md` carries the registry's own description of each activity and any note it has attached, which is where you will find what an activity actually does when the record's category fields are not enough to place it against the Council's exclusions. What each programme has actually labelled those activities sits separately in `/input_artifacts/programme_labelling_extract.csv`, because the label is the programme's claim about a unit and not the Council's rule about it. You will need both and they do not always agree.

Read the Council document before you read a sales sheet. Our requirement falls in the first phase, the 2024 to 2026 compliance period, and a unit labelled for the pilot phase is not a unit for ours however much the label says CORSIA. Neither is a unit the programme has not labelled for a compliance period at all. Ask the identification question first and ask it of the programme's own labelling: a block whose labelling points at another period, or says nothing about any period, has not been identified for ours, and that is a scope failure and a refusal rather than a referral. A referral is the narrower case. Where the programme has labelled a block for our phase and the Council's scope asks for something the record does not show, that block is not mine to refuse on my own authority and it is not yours either. Put it up as a referral with both sides quoted and tell me which way you would go.

Five files, at these paths.

`/logs/agent/clearance_register.csv`, one row per block in the pool, columns `block_id,project_id,registry,vintage_year,quantity_available,programme_approved,dates_test,scope_test,authorisation_test,availability_test,verdict,governing_source_id,governing_quote`. The five test columns take `PASS`, `FAIL` or `UNRESOLVED`. `verdict` is `CLEARED`, `REFERRED` or `REFUSED`. `authorisation_test` is the host country question: whether the record shows the unit authorised for use in CORSIA and the corresponding adjustment accounted for. A record that shows the authorisation absent or withdrawn is not the same thing as a record that says nothing about it, and it is the second I want recorded as `UNRESOLVED` rather than written down as a refusal. `scope_test` is the other side of it: whether the programme has identified the activity for our compliance period and whether the activity falls outside the exclusions that programme's own Scope of Eligibility carries. Some of those exclusions turn on a fact the record's category fields do not carry at all, and the dossier is where you will find it. For `governing_quote` give me the sentence from the Council document that decides that block, quoted exactly as it stands there, and put its `source_id` in `governing_source_id`. The sentence that decides a block is the one carrying the rule it failed or met, not the nearest sentence about the programme, and it comes out of the part of that document which governs our own compliance period. Where the Council words a rule around a particular programme's own units, that sentence settles that programme's blocks and nobody else's, so do not put the sentence about one registry's units behind a block another registry issued. Nor do I want one sentence carried across rows that turned on different rules, because a sentence used that way is a heading you have written over a group of blocks rather than the rule that decided any of them; where two blocks failed for different reasons I expect the two sentences those reasons come from. The document sets out each programme three times over, once for the pilot phase, once for ours and once for the second phase, above a preamble that governs none of them in particular. A sentence out of the preamble, or out of another period's section, is in the file and is not the rule that decides one of our blocks.

`/logs/agent/referrals.json`, a JSON array, one object per referred block, keys `block_id`, `question`, `programme_position`, `programme_quote`, `council_position`, `council_quote`, `recommendation`. Both quotes come out of the file they are attributed to. The recommendation says what would settle the block, naming the thing that is missing and the figures of the block it applies to.

`/logs/agent/cancellation_schedule.csv`, columns `sequence_no,block_id,project_id,registry,vintage_year,quantity_to_cancel,cumulative_tonnes`. Cleared blocks only. Take them in ascending vintage year, and within a vintage by `block_id`, until the 4,000,000 is met, splitting the last block for whatever is still outstanding rather than overshooting it. Nothing referred goes in this file.

`/logs/agent/eligibility_map.html`, one self-contained page the committee can read off a screen showing where the pool stands: every block its own visible mark inside a drawing, carrying `data-block-id`, with cleared, referred and refused readable apart at a glance. Place the marks against something real rather than on a plain grid, so that where a mark sits or how big it is follows the block's own tonnage or its vintage; the committee reads weight off the picture before it reads any number. Embed the register and the schedule behind it as JSON in `<script type="application/json">` blocks with the ids `register-data` and `schedule-data`, matching what you filed.

`/logs/agent/committee_note.md`, 1,500 to 2,600 words, under the headings `Position`, `What the Council requires`, `Pool disposition`, `Referrals`, `Cancellation schedule`, `Exposure and limitations`. Under `What the Council requires` set out the four things the Council actually conditions a first phase unit on, which are the compliance period the programme has to have identified the unit for, the eligible unit dates, the host country authorisation and the corresponding adjustment behind it, and the scope of eligibility with the exclusions it carries, and carry each of the four inside a sentence quoted from the Council document itself rather than in a summary of it in your own words, because that section is what the committee will be read against if anybody challenges the basis we cleared on. Under `Pool disposition` give me a two-column Markdown table keyed exactly `blocks_assessed`, `blocks_cleared`, `blocks_referred`, `blocks_refused`, `tonnes_cleared`, `tonnes_scheduled` and `requirement_shortfall`. Argue the referrals block by block against their own figures rather than in general terms. Where a block is refused for what the activity actually is rather than for a date or a label, say what it is in the registry's own words out of the dossier and name in the same breath the exclusion those words put it under, and give me at least four of those. On limitations be straight about what the registry record cannot tell us, what a corresponding adjustment would still have to be confirmed against, and where our own convention on crediting period start had to stand in for a date the record does not carry, and name the blocks of ours that each limitation actually bites on.

The five have to agree with each other. Same block ids, same verdicts, same tonnages, because the committee will read the note with the register open beside it.
