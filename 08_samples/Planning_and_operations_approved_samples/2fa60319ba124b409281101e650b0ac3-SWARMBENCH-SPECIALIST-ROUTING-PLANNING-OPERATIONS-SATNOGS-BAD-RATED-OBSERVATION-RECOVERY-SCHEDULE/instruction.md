A few notes also mention a superseded arrangement inside the availability section itself, so read the statement rather than pattern-matching it.

A station can declare more than one thing at a time: a site might hold a maintenance window and an elevation floor and a booking cap all at once, each in its own sentence. Capture everything a station declares, not just the first restriction you come across. A closed site is the exception -- a booking freeze covers the whole window on its own. Where a note declares nothing binding for the window, the station is unrestricted.

A word on the ratings: SatNOGS defines `bad` as satellite presence not detected in an observation artifact. It is distinct from the `failed` status and it does not prove a station outage or a root cause. `good` means presence was detected; it does not prove payload completeness or that the contact was historically scheduled as a retry. In the output schema `failure_id` is only a local recovery-case key equal to the source observation ID and asserts nothing about SatNOGS `failed` status.

Apply this review team's matching policy. A later observation is a baseline candidate for a case only when its ID appears in `candidate_observations.json`, its status is `good`, it carries the same NORAD catalog ID and transmitter UUID as the case, its observation frequency is identical, its transmitter status is `active`, its maximum altitude is at least 10 degrees, and its start is later than the case end but no more than 72 hours later. On top of that, every declaration the owning station makes applies, and a candidate is out if any one of them removes it: a station on a booking freeze can host nothing; a maintenance window removes any candidate whose own start-to-end interval overlaps it; a station minimum elevation removes any candidate peaking below that figure; a mode embargo removes any candidate on the embargoed transmitter mode. A booking cap is different -- it does not disqualify individual candidates, it limits how many contacts the released schedule may book at that station across the whole window. Where more than one declaration would remove the same candidate, record it in the impact ledger under the first that applies in the order booking freeze, maintenance window, minimum elevation, mode embargo. Use candidate start, end, station, frequency, mode and altitude exactly as recorded. A candidate may cover only one case. Select a maximum-cardinality assignment, and among assignments of that size minimise the total delay from case end to candidate start. Equivalent optimal assignments are fine. These thresholds and the objective are exercise policy, not SatNOGS operating rules.
