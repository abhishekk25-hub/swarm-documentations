# Station 4022 - SPUTNIX-VHF/UHF-Magadan

- Ground station ID: 4022
- Location: 59.589261, 150.832095 at 203 m AMSL
- Network page: https://network.satnogs.org/stations/4022/
- Note revised: 2026-07-20
- Applies to: 22-25 July 2026 recovery horizon

## Site and equipment

The station runs a twin-band vertical with a phasing harness into an Airspy R2 behind a filtered preamp, driven from a rack-mounted NUC. Uptime last quarter ran at about 93 per cent which suits the unattended operating we do here.

## Recent operating history

Volunteers rebuilt the feed arrangement in January which is worth knowing when you plan around us.

A maintenance slot floated for 24 July at 15:30Z was cancelled when the crane hire fell through, pushing the work to August and leaving the station up throughout this window.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

Scheduling here is fully automated and duplicate submissions are dropped automatically.

An unplanned outage from 2026-06-29T06:00:00Z to 2026-06-29T12:00:00Z took the site down when the rotator controller failed, closed out at the time and with no bearing on the current recovery window.

## Availability for the recovery window

One local caveat: please keep GMSK observations off this station; sample rates for it exceed what the host can sustain. Nothing else about the site changes. Our sister site 4052 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here. Just so the desk knows, an engineer is on site from 10:30 UTC on the 23rd to 13:30 UTC on the 24th and the chain will be broken; the receiver is physically disconnected while the work happens. No other limits apply here.

## Antenna and rotator detail

The feed is a hairpin match with a measured VSWR under 1.3 though it wants re-checking each spring.

## Software and configuration

We track the upstream client but hold back one minor version so a rebuild takes under an hour.

## Neighbouring coverage

Our footprint overlaps 3 neighbours to the north which matters when we go offline.

## Typical traffic

Our operators favour 2m and 70cm targets so unusual modes occasionally surprise us.

## Staffing

The station is run by 2 volunteers with the club providing cover during holidays.

## Power and connectivity

Power comes from the club's metered feed, backed by a 2 kW inverter though the changeover takes long enough to clip a recording. Connectivity is domestic fibre, which is the weak point here and transfers finish well inside the pass gap.

## Local interference

A survey in January found a persistent birdie near 386 MHz and it is documented in our station notes upstream.

## Calibration

Rotator alignment was re-surveyed in January so pointing errors should be under a degree.

## Fault handling

Anything unusual is logged and reviewed weekly with a monthly summary to the network.

## Data quality

Low passes to the south pick up interference from a nearby telemetry link though nothing has needed intervention this quarter. Baseline noise sits about 3 dB above the network median which rarely defeats the decoder outright.

## Other remarks

The Friday-morning routine maintenance we once ran between 0400Z and 1100Z ended in April and no longer applies.
