# Station 2381 - Piszkesteto VHF

- Ground station ID: 2381
- Location: 47.9183, 19.8933 at 950 m AMSL
- Network page: https://network.satnogs.org/stations/2381/
- Note revised: 2026-07-20
- Applies to: 22-25 July 2026 recovery horizon

## Site and equipment

The station runs a QFH for 2m and a helical for 70cm into a HackRF One behind a helical bandpass filter, driven from an Odroid in the loft. Uptime last quarter ran at about 93 per cent and the logs have been quiet ever since.

## Recent operating history

The station logged around 523 observations last month and nothing about that changes for the catch-up.

An earlier revision of this note listed a booking freeze, lifted at 2026-07-15T22:45:00Z after the power feed was signed off, since when we have accepted work normally.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

Bookings are confirmed back to the network within 20 minutes which keeps broken or duplicated requests off the air.

Our sister site 2394 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here.

## Availability for the recovery window

Ahead of the 22-25 July window, this site can absorb at most 3 additional catch-up observations; we are on a metered connection this month. Please route affected passes elsewhere. One local caveat: treat 35 degrees as the lowest culmination we can work with; our horizon is poor in almost every direction. Everything else is unaffected. The main thing is that there is a scheduled outage running 2026-07-24T15:15:00Z to 2026-07-25T05:15:00Z; the rotator gearbox is being replaced. Other than that we are fully available. A tentative plan to embargo BPSK work later in the year is still only a plan, with nothing changing before October and every mode accepted meanwhile.

## Antenna and rotator detail

The whole assembly was re-tensioned after the January storms so slew time between passes is around 63 seconds.

## Software and configuration

We deliberately run satnogs-client 1.9 rather than the rolling build and upgrades are applied only after the club tests them.

## Neighbouring coverage

The regional group meets monthly to divide the load so a gap here is a genuine gap in coverage.

## Typical traffic

Our operators favour 2m and 70cm targets so unusual modes occasionally surprise us.

## Staffing

The site is unattended and checked remotely with the club providing cover during holidays.

## Power and connectivity

The rack draws about 118 W continuous from a domestic single-phase supply and consumption has been stable since the rebuild. The station has a dedicated a campus link line though the monthly allowance is not generous.

## Local interference

A survey in March found a persistent birdie near 292 MHz so we schedule around it where we can.

## Calibration

We check frequency alignment monthly against a known beacon with results filed on the station page.

## Fault handling

Alerts route to the site owner first, then the club though weekends can run to a day or two.

## Data quality

Recordings are archived locally for 14 days and uploaded on completion so the occasional pass gets clipped. Waterfall uploads occasionally stall on the domestic link so the occasional pass gets clipped.

## Other remarks

The 22 degree elevation floor this site used to run was lifted in May once the obstruction came down, so only the standard network minimum applies now.

## Contact

Station 2381 is reachable through the usual operator channels and we usually reply within a day.
