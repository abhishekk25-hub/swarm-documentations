# Station 4391 - UHF-VHF Sofia City Balcony

- Ground station ID: 4391
- Location: 42.68152, 23.35784 at 608 m AMSL
- Network page: https://network.satnogs.org/stations/4391/
- Note revised: 2026-07-20
- Applies to: 22-25 July 2026 recovery horizon

## Site and equipment

The station runs a twin-band vertical with a phasing harness into a NooElec SmarTee behind a helical bandpass filter, driven from a rack-mounted NUC. Local noise improved once the solar inverter was refiltered in June and it has needed very little attention since.

## Recent operating history

This site mostly serves the southern horizon which is worth knowing when you plan around us.

A tentative plan to embargo BPSK work later in the year is still only a plan, with nothing changing before August and every mode accepted meanwhile.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

Our scheduler honours priority flags so the published queue is what actually flies.

Our sister site 4409 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here.

## Availability for the recovery window

One thing for the recovery window: the site can carry 3 make-good bookings in total; the site shares a link with the household and we must be fair to it. Shout if that causes a problem.

## Antenna and rotator detail

Azimuth travel is limited to 355 degrees by the mast stay and pointing has held true since.

## Software and configuration

We track the upstream client but hold back one minor version so behaviour is predictable between updates.

## Neighbouring coverage

There is no other coverage within about 40 km and the split has worked well so far.

## Typical traffic

Our operators favour VHF targets and results there are consistently good.

## Staffing

One operator covers this site day to day which keeps the workload manageable.

## Power and connectivity

A 1 kW supply feeds the shelter through an isolating transformer and the generator has never actually been needed. Connectivity is a campus link, which is the weak point here which occasionally drops during heavy weather.

## Local interference

Pager traffic near 227 MHz used to swamp us which mostly affects the weaker downlinks.

## Calibration

Timing is disciplined by NTP with a local stratum-1 source and nothing has moved since.

## Fault handling

Anything unusual is logged and reviewed weekly though weekends can run to a day or two.

## Data quality

Waterfall uploads occasionally stall on the domestic link so raw audio is the better source if in doubt. Frame decoding is handled downstream rather than on site and downstream products are unaffected.

## Other remarks

The Wednesday-morning routine maintenance we once ran between 0400Z and 1200Z ended in April and no longer applies.
