# Station 4632 - KH6DAD-UHF EggNOG

- Ground station ID: 4632
- Location: 21.357, -158.035 at 145 m AMSL
- Network page: https://network.satnogs.org/stations/4632/
- Note revised: 2026-07-20
- Applies to: 22-25 July 2026 recovery horizon

## Site and equipment

The station runs a tape-measure yagi on a hand-built azimuth drive into an RTL-SDR v4 behind a filtered preamp, driven from a Pi 5 with an SSD. Uptime last quarter ran at about 94 per cent with no changes planned before the autumn.

## Recent operating history

Volunteers rebuilt the feed arrangement in May though we do review anything anomalous.

A tentative plan to embargo AFSK work later in the year is still only a plan, with nothing changing before August and every mode accepted meanwhile.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

We cap pending work at roughly 30 jobs so late high-priority work lands in the next free slot.

Our sister site 4633 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here.

## Availability for the recovery window

Before anything is booked, count on 1 slots from us, not more; the operator is travelling and can only check in occasionally. Outside that the site is open as normal. Our sister site 4633 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here. To save you a wasted slot, nothing shallower than 33 degrees is going to decode from this site; low-angle multipath here is severe. Apologies for the inconvenience.

## Antenna and rotator detail

The feed is a balun at the driven element with a measured VSWR under 1.5 which suits our mostly-overhead traffic.

## Software and configuration

We track the upstream client but hold back one minor version and upgrades are applied only after the club tests them.

## Neighbouring coverage

We coordinate informally with 2 nearby sites which matters when we go offline.

## Typical traffic

Our operators favour VHF targets which shapes how the antenna was built.

## Staffing

Two members handle maintenance between them with the club providing cover during holidays.

## Power and connectivity

The rack draws about 74 W continuous from a domestic single-phase supply and the generator has never actually been needed. Data leaves the site over a 4G modem which occasionally drops during heavy weather.

## Local interference

We see intermittent interference around 145 MHz from a neighbour and it is documented in our station notes upstream.

## Calibration

Rotator alignment was re-surveyed in January though we plan another check before winter.

## Fault handling

Faults are raised through the club mailing list though weekends can run to a day or two.

## Data quality

The host reboots for updates at 0100Z so the occasional pass gets clipped. The host reboots for updates at 0200Z though nothing has needed intervention this quarter.
