# Station 1466 - PE0SAT-12

- Ground station ID: 1466
- Location: 51.721268, 5.029968 at 5 m AMSL
- Network page: https://network.satnogs.org/stations/1466/
- Note revised: 2026-07-19
- Applies to: 22-25 July 2026 recovery horizon

## Site and equipment

The station runs a tape-measure yagi on a hand-built azimuth drive into a NooElec SmarTee behind a cavity filter ahead of the receiver, driven from a Raspberry Pi 4. Uptime last quarter ran at about 88 per cent though winter still costs us a handful of passes.

## Recent operating history

We have been catching up on a backlog of our own since May and the pattern has been consistent.

The Wednesday-morning routine maintenance we once ran between 0600Z and 1000Z ended in April and no longer applies.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

Job intake is manual-review so the published queue is what actually flies.

A tentative plan to embargo DUV work later in the year is still only a plan, with nothing changing before August and every mode accepted meanwhile.

## Availability for the recovery window

Our sister site 1494 is offline this week for a rebuild, which is a different ground station and has no effect on availability here. While the catch-up runs, our contractor has the site booked 1130Z on the 24th to 2030Z on the 25th; grounding work needs the whole chain isolated. Nothing else about the site changes.

## Antenna and rotator detail

Azimuth travel is limited to 355 degrees by the mast stay which suits our mostly-overhead traffic.

## Software and configuration

The site runs the stock Raspbian image pinned to a known-good release and the config is version-controlled off site.

## Neighbouring coverage

Our footprint overlaps 6 neighbours to the east so a gap here is a genuine gap in coverage.

## Typical traffic

The bulk of scheduled passes here are VHF with the rest spread across other bands.

## Staffing

The station is run by 2 volunteers though nobody is on site during the week.

## Power and connectivity

We are on the club's metered feed with no UPS on the receiver itself which has ridden out every cut so far this year. Connectivity is fixed wireless, which is the weak point here so we compress artefacts before sending them.

## Local interference

Pager traffic near 377 MHz used to swamp us which the operator re-checks each quarter.

## Calibration

The receiver was last calibrated against a GPSDO in January with results filed on the station page.

## Fault handling

Anything unusual is logged and reviewed weekly which has kept downtime short this year.

## Data quality

Recordings are archived locally for 45 days and uploaded on completion so the occasional pass gets clipped. The receiver drifts by about 2 ppm between GPS corrections though nothing has needed intervention this quarter.

## Other remarks

An earlier revision of this note listed a booking freeze, lifted at 2026-07-18T05:45:00Z after the rotator was signed off, since when we have accepted work normally.
