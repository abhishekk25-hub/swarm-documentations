## Site and equipment

The station runs a QFH for 2m and a helical for 70cm into an Airspy Mini behind an inline SAW filter, driven from a Pi 5 with an SSD. Uptime last quarter ran at about 91 per cent so the configuration has been stable for a while.

## Recent operating history

We have been catching up on a backlog of our own since March so expect the usual throughput.

An earlier revision of this note listed a booking freeze, lifted at 2026-07-08T19:45:00Z after the feedline was signed off, since when we have accepted work normally.

## Scheduling and queue behaviour

Job intake is manual-review so the published queue is what actually flies.

The Tuesday-morning routine maintenance we once ran between 0500Z and 1400Z ended in June and no longer applies.

## Availability for the recovery window

A 4-booking weekly limit trialled during the March campaign to manage upload costs finished with it, and no booking limit is in force for this recovery window. Heads up -- the site is under a hard booking freeze for the whole recovery window; both club keyholders are travelling. We can take anything else you send us.

## Antenna and rotator detail

The feed is a balun at the driven element with a measured VSWR under 1.8 which suits our mostly-overhead traffic.

## Software and configuration

We track the upstream client but hold back one minor version and upgrades are applied only after the club tests them.

## Neighbouring coverage

The nearest other station is roughly 353 km away so a gap here is a genuine gap in coverage.

## Typical traffic

Most of our traffic is 2m and 70cm weather and cubesat work which shapes how the antenna was built.

## Staffing

Two members handle maintenance between them with the club providing cover during holidays.

## Power and connectivity

Power comes from the club's metered feed, backed by a 2 kW inverter though the changeover takes long enough to clip a recording. The site uses a 4G modem with a fixed address so we compress artefacts before sending them.

## Local interference

We see intermittent interference around 390 MHz from a neighbour and it is documented in our station notes upstream.

## Calibration

The chain was swept end to end in May which keeps Doppler correction honest.

## Fault handling

Alerts route to the site owner first, then the club which has kept downtime short this year.

## Data quality

Baseline noise sits about 2 dB above the network median so raw audio is the better source if in doubt. Decoder success here has run around 91 per cent over the past year so treat marginal passes with a little caution.
