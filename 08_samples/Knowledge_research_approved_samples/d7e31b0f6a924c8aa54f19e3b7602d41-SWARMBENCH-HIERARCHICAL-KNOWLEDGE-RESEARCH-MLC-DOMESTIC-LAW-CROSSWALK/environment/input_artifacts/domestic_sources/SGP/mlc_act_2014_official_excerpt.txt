An Act to give effect to the Maritime Labour Convention, 2006, to make
provisions generally for matters connected therewith. [1 April 2014]

PART 3 — MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS FOR SEAFARERS' EMPLOYMENT

5.—(1) A person must not cause or permit a person below 16 years of age to be
employed on board a ship.

6.—(1) A seafarer below 18 years of age must not be employed or engaged, or
work, as a ship's cook.

7.—(1) Subject to subsections (3) and (4), a person must not work as a seafarer
on a ship unless that person has been issued with a medical fitness certificate
complying with the prescribed requirements, and which is valid and is not
suspended.

9. A medical fitness certificate is valid only from the date of the medical
examination and for the period specified on the certificate, which must not
exceed the following maximum periods: (a) in respect of a person below 18 years
of age, one year; (b) in respect of a person of 18 years of age or above,
2 years.

12.—(1) A person must not operate a seafarer recruitment and placement service
in Singapore unless the person is authorised to do so by the Director.
(2) Despite any written law to the contrary, a person must not demand or
receive, directly or indirectly, from a seafarer or a person seeking employment
as a seafarer, or from a person on behalf of a prospective employee, any
remuneration for providing the prospective employee with employment.

PART 4 — CONDITIONS OF SEAFARERS' EMPLOYMENT

14.—(1) A person must not cause or permit another person without a seafarer's
employment agreement to be employed as a seafarer on a ship.
(2) Every shipowner must ensure that the seafarer's employment agreement is
read over and explained to the seafarer; ascertain that the seafarer understands
the agreement; ensure that the seafarer has been given an opportunity to
examine and seek advice on the agreement before the seafarer signs it; and
ensure that the agreement is signed both by the seafarer and by or on behalf of
the shipowner.

16.—(1) Every shipowner must ensure that every seafarer in the shipowner's
employment is given the hours of rest in accordance with this section.
(2) The minimum hours of rest are 10 hours in any 24-hour period and 77 hours
in any 7-day period.
(3) The minimum hours of rest may be divided into no more than 2 periods, one
of which must be at least 6 hours in length, and the interval between
consecutive periods of rest must not exceed 14 hours.

20.—(1) Except as otherwise provided in this Act or any other written law, the
wages due to a seafarer under a seafarer's employment agreement must be paid to
the seafarer in full on a monthly basis.
(4) The master of a ship must deliver to each seafarer employed on the ship a
monthly account of wages due to the seafarer under that agreement.

22.—(1) A seafarer is entitled to paid annual leave of at least 2.5 days per
month of continuous service with the shipowner.

PART 5 — CATERING

25. A ship to which this section applies must carry a qualified ship's cook.

PART 6 — MEDICAL CARE AND TREATMENT

28. The shipowner must ensure that seafarers have access to prompt and adequate
medical care while working on board.

34. A shipowner must maintain financial security meeting the prescribed
requirements for liabilities covered by this Act.

PART 7 — HEALTH AND SAFETY PROTECTION AND ACCIDENT PREVENTION

40. Every shipowner must, so far as is reasonably practicable, ensure the safety
and health of seafarers employed on board the ship.

PART 8 — INSPECTION AND CERTIFICATION

48. A ship of the prescribed description must have on board a valid Maritime
Labour Certificate and Declaration of Maritime Labour Compliance.

50. The Declaration of Maritime Labour Compliance is to identify the national
requirements implementing the Convention and the measures adopted to ensure
ongoing compliance.

52. The Director may issue a Maritime Labour Certificate where satisfied that
the ship complies with the applicable requirements.

53. A Maritime Labour Certificate is valid for the period stated in the
certificate, subject to the inspection and cancellation provisions of this Act.

PART 9 — COMPLAINTS

55. Every ship must maintain on-board complaint procedures available to
seafarers for the fair, effective and expeditious handling of complaints.

PART 10 — INSPECTIONS IN PORT

58. An inspector may inspect a vessel in port for compliance with the
Convention and the requirements of this Act.
