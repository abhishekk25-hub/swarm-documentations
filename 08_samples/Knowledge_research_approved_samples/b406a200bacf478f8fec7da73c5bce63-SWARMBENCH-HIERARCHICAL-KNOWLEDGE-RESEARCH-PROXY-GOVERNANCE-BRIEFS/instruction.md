A stewardship desk that votes shares across a large book cannot read a five-hundred-page proxy for every company before every annual meeting, but it does need, for each one, a clear read on how the company is governed and how it pays its leaders - because that is what the ballot is actually about. What the desk keeps is a short governance-and-pay brief per company that turns the proxy statement into a voting picture: how independent and accountable the board really is, how the executive pay program is built and whether it rewards performance or just size, what shareholders said about the pay last time and what the company did about it, whether any related-party dealings or conflicts deserve a second look, and what a share-voter should actually weigh. Repeating that "the company has a board and pays its executives" is not the job - anyone can see that. The value is a reasoned governance reading of each proxy, grounded strictly in that company's own filing. A batch of proxies just came in ahead of the meeting season, and each one needs its brief written and reasoned, not merely transcribed.

Where the inputs live: the directory /input_artifacts/proxies/ holds one UTF-8 text file per company, each named for the company's stock ticker in lower case, so the proxy for the company trading as MSFT is the file /input_artifacts/proxies/msft.txt. Do not assume which companies are in the set - list that directory and work only from the files that are actually present. Each file opens with a short header (the company, ticker, CIK, form, and filing date) and then reproduces the narrative sections of the definitive proxy statement an analyst reads: the corporate-governance discussion, the Compensation Discussion and Analysis, the say-on-pay advisory vote, and the related-person transactions. Read the governance discussion and the compensation discussion against each other before you write, because whether pay is well governed only makes sense in light of how independent and engaged the board and its committees actually are. Write the briefs only under /logs/agent/briefs/. Do not modify or add files under /input_artifacts/.

Your job is one governance-and-pay brief per company, written to /logs/agent/briefs/<ticker>.md under the same lower-case ticker as the source file - so msft.txt becomes /logs/agent/briefs/msft.md. Open the file with a level-1 heading naming the company and ticker, for example: # Microsoft Corp (MSFT) - proxy governance and pay brief. After that heading, use exactly these six sections, each under the given markdown subheading:

- "## Company and meeting" - the company and ticker, the proxy filing, and what the brief covers; enough for a voter to know exactly which filing this is.
- "## Board and governance structure" - how the board is actually composed and governed: its independence, the leadership structure (whether the chair and CEO roles are combined or separate and whether there is a lead independent director), the key committees, and any notable governance features or provisions this proxy describes.
- "## Executive compensation design" - how the executive pay program is built as described in the Compensation Discussion and Analysis: the pay philosophy, the mix of fixed and variable pay, and the specific performance metrics and equity vehicles used - not a generic "executives are paid salary and bonus".
- "## Pay-for-performance and say-on-pay" - how well pay is tied to performance on this company's own telling, and the say-on-pay advisory vote: the result reported and how the company says it responded to shareholder feedback.
- "## Related-party and conflict flags" - the related-person transactions or potential conflicts this proxy discloses and why they matter; if the proxy discloses none of note, say so explicitly rather than inventing concerns.
- "## Shareholder voting takeaway" - the one or two governance or pay issues a share-voter should actually weigh for this company when deciding how to vote - specific to this proxy, not boilerplate stewardship advice.

Ground every judgment in the proxy in front of you. The company, the board and pay facts, and the say-on-pay outcome must be right, and every reading has to rest on what this proxy actually states - nothing carried in from another company or from general reputation. A brief that would fit any large company, or that assigns this company a governance feature, a pay metric, or a related-party deal it did not disclose, is worse than none, because a desk will cast votes on it. Each brief is judged on its own against its own proxy, the way a governance lead red-lines an analyst's voting memo, so filling in the headings counts for little; the value is in a correct, company-specific reading of how the board is governed, how pay is designed, whether pay tracks performance, what conflicts exist, and what a voter should weigh.

Give each company the reading and thought it deserves. If the clock runs out before the batch is done, do not pad to look complete - a handful of briefs that are precise, reasoned, and unmistakably about their own company beat a long row of interchangeable ones, since scoring is per company and a thin or boilerplate brief earns next to nothing however many you file. Always reason from the proxy of the company you are on, never from its ticker or a half-remembered impression of the firm.
