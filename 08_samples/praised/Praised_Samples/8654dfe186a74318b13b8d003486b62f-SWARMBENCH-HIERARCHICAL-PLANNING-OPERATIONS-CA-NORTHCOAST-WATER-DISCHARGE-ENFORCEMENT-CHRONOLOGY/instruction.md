Leadership wants one person to actually read across the recent orders and lay out for the room what we have been requiring, which sites are the worst actors, what keeps coming up across the region, where a site stands out, and where our own record is messy because one order revised or superseded another. These are the official record, so every finding you bring me has to trace back to a specific order and the actual wording in it, because the Executive Officer will ask where it says that before acting on anything. Do not work from memory or a summary. For each finding, put the regulator's point in your own words and pair it with the exact wording from that order that backs it up. Keep the two separate: your sentence is the finding, the copied passage is the quote, and they should not be the same text, so do not paste or trim the quote into the finding field. A finding without a real quote from the order it came from is no use to me, so leave it out rather than guess. If an order will not open or you cannot stand a point up after a couple of tries, skip it and note the gap. A shorter honest list beats a padded one, and a made up date or quote is worse than a blank.

Everything you produce goes under /logs/agent, and the written dossier is the centerpiece. There are five things I need.

First, the chronology ledger, saved as enforcement_chronology.csv. One row per finding, and treat it as the single source of truth that everything else is built from. Each row should carry your own id for the row, the order id (use the order number like R1-2024-0044, or the file name where the order has no clean number), the discharger, the county, the waterway the discharge reached or threatened, the assessor parcel number if the order gives one, the date the order ties to that finding if there is one, a topic that must be exactly one of these nine labels, whichever fits best, and not a wording you invent: sediment discharge, erosion and grading, cannabis cultivation discharge, road or stream crossing failure, fuel or chemical release, riparian or wetland fill, monitoring and reporting violation, threatened discharge, or cleanup and remediation requirement (put the site-specific detail in your finding sentence, not in the topic), then the regulator's point in your words, then the verbatim quote, then the link to that order. Do not reuse the same quote across rows, aim for several distinct findings per order spanning more than one topic, and make sure you actually cover all the orders and counties rather than letting it thin out after the first few.

Second, the dossier itself, site_dossier.docx, the document I will circulate and present from. Open with where regional enforcement stands overall, then go site by site on what each discharger was actually directed to do and where the board found the discharge or the shortfall, then pull out the themes that show up again and again across the region, then the outliers where one or two sites clearly stand apart, then a section on the messy record where one order revised, amended or superseded another for the same site, and what that means, then what you would have us tighten in how we write and track these orders, and close with an appendix that lists every order and its link. Write it as real paragraphs that say something specific to each site, not the same line with the name swapped.

Third, the crosslink matrix, saved as crosslink_matrix.csv, one row per order, tying the discharger to the parcel numbers, the order id, the waterway, the county, and any order it supersedes, is superseded by, or is otherwise related to. This is how I check that the same site under more than one order lines up.

Fourth, the contradiction log, saved as contradiction_log.csv, one row per issue you find across the orders: an order that revises, amends or supersedes another, the same site or parcel appearing under more than one order, conflicting dates, or a reused or inconsistent order reference. Each row should carry your id for the issue, the order id it concerns, the kind of issue, a short description in your words, the related order id where there is one, and a verbatim quote and link backing the relationship.

Fifth, the charts, as PNGs drawn straight off the ledger so the counts match what is in it: one of findings by topic saved as chart_findings_by_topic.png, one of findings by county as chart_findings_by_county.png, and one of findings by waterway as chart_findings_by_waterway.png.
