Our diabetes program portfolio has grown faster than our CRA coverage and I need the quarterly risk-based monitoring plan I can defend at the clinical governance review. We have a stack of registered trials across the portfolio, one quarter of CRA monitor-hours to spend, and we cannot get out to every site this quarter, so the whole point is to monitor the right trials, in the right order, on a cadence we can stand behind when a sponsor or an auditor asks why a given study was or was not monitored. Treat this as the real deliverable, not a sketch - if we under-monitor a high-risk regulated trial or burn the quarter on low-risk studies, that is a subject-safety and a data-integrity problem and it lands on us.

Everything you need is in /input_artifacts. The portfolio itself is in /input_artifacts/trials/ - one ClinicalTrials.gov study record per trial (these are the real registry records; they vary in format, so read each one rather than assuming a fixed layout). Each record carries the trial's overall recruitment status, study type and phase, enrollment count and basis, the facility locations and their individual site-level statuses, the interventions, the eligibility, and the date the record was last updated - those are the fields the risk method runs on, and they have to be read out of the record. monitoring_rulebook.md is our adopted risk-based-monitoring method and it is binding: it defines which trials are in scope this quarter, how to reconcile the record's overall status against divergent site statuses, the risk score and the risk tiers, the monitoring hours, the visit-window cadence, the CRA capacity, and the exact order in which we schedule visits. sources_to_confirm.md is the list of authorities our quality and regulatory functions require confirmed against the official source. There is also a data_provenance_note.md.

A few things that have bitten us before. The overall recruitment status governs scope, but a trial's individual site statuses do not always agree with it - where one or more sites disagree with the overall status, the overall status governs and you log the discrepancy; do not silently re-scope a trial off one site's status. Set the out-of-scope studies aside - completed, terminated, withdrawn, suspended and not-yet-recruiting trials are not risk-scored or scheduled, but keep them on the record with the reason. And the visit-window cadence and the weekly CRA-hours cap interact: a plan that looks fine trial-by-trial can quietly push a high-risk study past the cadence it needs, or run the portfolio past the hours we actually have, once everything competes for the same weeks - so some trials will be deferred for capacity and some will be window-infeasible, and that only shows up when you schedule them together against the cap.

I also need the methodology actually confirmed and cited, not paraphrased. Work through every authority in sources_to_confirm.md - ICH E6(R2) and E6(R3) good clinical practice, the FDA risk-based-monitoring guidance and its Q&A, ICH E8(R1), the IND and IDE regulations, 21 CFR Part 11, the FDAAA 801 / ClinicalTrials.gov reporting rule, the Common Rule, the TransCelerate RBM framework, and the NLM registry documentation.
