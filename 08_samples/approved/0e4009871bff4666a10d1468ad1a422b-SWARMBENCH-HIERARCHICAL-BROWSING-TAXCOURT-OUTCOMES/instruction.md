We track how the U.S. Tax Court decides its memorandum cases, and I need you to work through a set of real opinions and record, for each one, how it came out and a few key facts about it.

Your working directory is /workspace. Nothing is saved on the machine for you, so you'll need to pull the opinions yourself over the internet. Start with the index at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/olayis/taxcourt-outcomes-corpus/main/manifest.json. It lists every opinion in the set, and each entry gives you an id (use that as the opinion_id in your output), the case caption, the docket number and filing date, a source_url for the opinion's page, and a text_url that points to the full text. Open the text_url and read the whole opinion before you write anything down about it. These run long, so read carefully.

For each opinion, record the following.

How much the taxpayer won, as one of "none", "partial", or "substantial". You have to read the reasoning to know this. Mark it "none" when the Commissioner wins every contested issue the court decides and the taxpayer walks away with nothing (a point the Commissioner concedes on his own doesn't count as the taxpayer winning). Mark it "partial" when the taxpayer wins at least one contested issue and the Commissioner wins at least one other. Mark it "substantial" when the taxpayer wins the main issue or gets the relief they came for.

How the penalties came out, as one of "no_penalties", "upheld", "rejected", or "split". Use "no_penalties" when there were no penalties or additions to tax at issue, "upheld" when every penalty at issue was sustained against the taxpayer, "rejected" when none of them were sustained, and "split" when some were and some weren't. Read the penalty discussion (sections like 6662, 6663, 6651) to decide.

Be careful with both of those, because the last line of an opinion won't tell you who won or how the penalties came out. Tax Court opinions almost always end with boilerplate like "Decision will be entered under Rule 155" or "An appropriate order and decision will be entered," and that same line shows up whether the taxpayer won, lost, or split, and whether penalties stuck or not. So don't go by that line; read how the court actually resolved each issue.

Also record, for each opinion: the principal issue the case turned on, in one line; the single sentence, copied word for word from the opinion, where the court states its holding on that main issue (a real sentence from the text, not your paraphrase and not the boilerplate disposition line); the judge who authored the opinion; the tax year or years at issue; and the total deficiency or amount in dispute as stated near the start (or "not stated" if it isn't given).

Put everything in a spreadsheet at /logs/agent/opinions.xlsx. Give it a sheet named opinions with one row per case and these columns: opinion_id, caption, taxpayer_relief, penalty_outcome, principal_issue, dispositive_sentence, authoring_judge, tax_years, amount_at_issue, source_url. Add each case to the spreadsheet as you finish reading it, so your progress is saved as you go. Add a second sheet named summary with metric and value columns; a short rollup such as the total number of opinions is enough. Then write a short brief at /logs/agent/brief.md, a few hundred words, on how the cases break down, which issues and penalties keep coming up, and any notable taxpayer wins, all grounded in what you actually read.

Work through every opinion in the index. They run long, so if you run short before you get through all of them, save the cases you completed and write the brief from those rather than losing the work. And only write down what an opinion really says, and don't record anything for an opinion you weren't able to read.
