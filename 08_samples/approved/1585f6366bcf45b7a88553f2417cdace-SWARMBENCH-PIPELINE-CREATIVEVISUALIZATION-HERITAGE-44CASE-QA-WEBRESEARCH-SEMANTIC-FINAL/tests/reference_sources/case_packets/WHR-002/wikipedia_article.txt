The '''Minaret of Jam''' ([[Pashto]]: د جام منار, [[Dari]]: منار جام) is a [[UNESCO]] [[World Heritage Site]] in western [[Afghanistan]]. It is located in a remote and nearly inaccessible region of [[Shahrak District]], [[Ghor Province]], between the Jam and [[Hari (Afghanistan)|Hari Rivers]]. The {{convert|62|m|ft|adj=on|abbr=on}}<ref>{{cite book |last=Pinder-Wilson |first=Ralph |title=Iran |year=2001 |volume=39 |page=167 |chapter=Ghaznavid and Ghūrid Minarets}}</ref> or {{convert|65|m|ft|adj=on|abbr=on}}<ref name=bbc>{{Cite news |date=2014-08-28 |title=Afghan historic minaret of Jam 'in danger of collapse' |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-28969385 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180719130444/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-28969385 |archive-date=2018-07-19 |access-date=2022-11-18 |work=BBC News |language=en-GB}}</ref> high [[minaret]] was built {{circa|1190}}, entirely of baked bricks, and is famous for its intricate brick, stucco and glazed tile decoration, which consists of alternating bands of [[Kufic]] and [[Naskh (script)|Naskhi]] [[calligraphy]], geometric patterns, and verses from the [[Qur'an]]. Since 2002, the minaret has remained on the [[list of World Heritage in Danger]], under serious threat of erosion, and has not been actively preserved.<ref name="youtube.com">{{Cite AV media |url=http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F8SREfehZ4 |title=Discover Afghanistan - The Minaret of Jam |date=Aug 15, 2013 |publisher=NATO |access-date=2022-11-18 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140607122256/http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5F8SREfehZ4 |archive-date=2014-06-07 |url-status=dead |via=YouTube}}</ref> In 2014, the [[BBC]] reported that the tower was in imminent danger of collapse.<ref name=bbc/>

In 2020, the Minaret of Jam was listed among cultural heritage sites of the [[Islamic world]] by the [[ICESCO|Islamic World Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization]] (ICESCO). According to the Afghan [[Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Afghanistan)|Ministry of Foreign Affairs]] (MoFA), the Minaret of Jam is Afghanistan's first cultural heritage site to be listed by ICESCO.<ref name="tolonews.com">{{Cite web |title=Minaret of Jam Listed as ICESOCO Heritage Site |url=https://tolonews.com/index.php/arts-culture-168869 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210120175323/https://tolonews.com/index.php/arts-culture-168869 |archive-date=2021-01-20 |access-date=2021-01-17 |website=TOLOnews |language=en}}</ref>

== Etymology ==
The word minaret is [[Arabic]] [منارة] and usually means a tower next to a mosque from which the [[muezzin]] calls the faithful to prayer. However it also means lighthouse and has other meanings. Here, it is used loosely.

== Architecture ==
The Minaret of Jam measures at 65 meters (210 feet) and is made out of baked brick, stucco, terracotta, and turquoise glazed tiles, with its exterior decorated with floral and geometric designs. Inscribed on the eastern face of  the tower is excueted in oramental angular Kufi script except for the cursive script of the architect, Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Naysaburi. On the top of the tower is a gallery Inside the structure there is a central pillar with double spiral staircases, both have 159 steps leading up to the two wooden balconies. Each of the steps consists of four courses that have additional four or five baked bricks that are arranged horizontally. The steps at the bottom are 4.59 feet (1.4) wide and ascending at the summit they are 3.28 feet (1.0) wide and are alternatively keyed into the tower's central pillar and it's exterior. Both the central pillar and the staircases terminate at the landing, followed by a series of six vaulted brick platforms above. On the outside of tower, there are inscribed texts in angular square Kufi script with text from the Quran. Also on it, there are ribbon like and strands of intersecting boidal knots that alternate with each other.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Lintz |first=Ulrike |title=Calligraphy and Architecture in the Muslim World |publisher=Edinburgh University Press |year=2013 |isbn=978-1-4744-6842-8 |pages=Gharipour, Mohammad, and İrvin Cemil Schick, editors. Calligraphy and Architecture in the Muslim World. Edinburgh University Press, 2013. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/j.ctvxcrjh7. Accessed 2 Dec. 2025}}</ref>

== Site ==
The Minaret of Jaam is probably located at the site of the [[Ghurid dynasty]]'s capital, [[Firozkoh]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Thomas |first=David |title=The ebb and flow of the Ghūrid empire |publisher=Sydney University Press |year=2018 |isbn=9781743325414 |location=[Sydney] |oclc=1003642529}}</ref>
[[File:Impressing from any angle, Minaret of Jam - panoramio.jpg|thumb|409x409px|Film photograph of the minaret and surrounding landscape]]
The circular minaret rests on an octagonal base; it had 2 wooden balconies and was topped by a lantern. The Minaret itself is made out of baked brick, stucco, terracotta, and turquoise glazed tiles, with its exterior decorated with floral and geometric designs. Inside the structure there is a central pillar with double spiral staircases, both have 159 steps leading up to the two balconies. Each step consists of four courses that have additional four or five baked bricks that are arranged horizontal. The steps at the bottom are 4.59 feet (1.4) wide and at the summit they are 3.28 feet (1.0) wide and are alternatively keyed into the tower's central pillar<ref>{{Cite book |last=Lintz |first=Ulrike |title=Calligraphy and Architecture in the Muslim World |publisher=Edinburgh University Press |year=2013 |isbn=978-1-4744-6842-8 |pages=Lintz, Ulrike-Christiane. “The Qur’anic Inscriptions of the Minaret of Jām in Afghanistan.” Calligraphy and Architecture in the Muslim World, edited by Mohammad Gharipour and İrvin Cemil Schick, Edinburgh University Press, 2013, pp. 83–102. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/j.ctvxcrjh7.9. Accessed 1 Dec. 2025 |language=English}}</ref> It's formal presentation has a striking similarity to the [[Ghazni Minarets|Ghazni minarets]] built by Masud III.<ref>{{cite book |last=Pinder-Wilson |first=Ralph |title=Iran |year=2001 |volume=39 |pages=169–170 |chapter=Ghaznavid and Ghūrid Minarets}}</ref> It is thought to have been a direct inspiration for the [[Qutub Minar]] in [[Delhi]], India.

The Minaret of Jam belongs to a group of around 60 minarets and towers built between the 11th and the 13th centuries in Central Asia, Iran and Afghanistan, including the [[Kutlug Timur Minaret]] in [[Konye-Urgench|Old Urgench]] (long considered the tallest of these still in existence).<ref name=":0" /> The minarets are thought to have been built as symbols of [[Islam]]'s victory, while other towers were simply landmarks or watchtowers. However, the main theme Koranic verses inscribed to the minaret is Mary or the Virgin mother of Jesus who is a figure also revered in Islam and there has been some evidence that the tower was perhaps commissioned by a noble woman in the Ghurid Dynasty.{{citation needed|date=June 2026}}

The archaeological landscape around Jam includes the ruins of a 'palace', fortifications, a pottery kiln and a Jewish cemetery, and has been suggested to be the remains of the lost city of [[Firozkoh|Turquoise Mountain]]. Analysis of the "robber holes" around the site, high-resolution satellite images and data from Google Maps has led to an estimate that the Ghūrid summer capital around the minaret was about 19.5 hectares in size.<ref name=":0" />

The archaeological site of Jam was successfully nominated as Afghanistan's first World Heritage site in 2002. It was also inscribed in UNESCO's [[list of World Heritage in Danger]], due to the precarious state of preservation of the minaret, and the results of looting at the site.<ref name="unesco">{{cite web |title=Minaret and Archaeological Remains of Jam |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/211 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180422105344/http://whc.unesco.org/en/list/211/ |archive-date=22 April 2018 |access-date=19 February 2011 |work=UNESCO World Heritage Center |publisher=UNESCO}}</ref>

According to archaeologists, a Jewish cemetery was also discovered 10 kilometers away from the minaret including remains from a military building, a palace and pottery jars.<ref name="tolonews.com"/>

== History ==
{{See also|History of Afghanistan}}
[[File:Jam minartet clear white ghorid empire2009.jpg|thumb|258px|Jam minaret]]
[[File:1502-Babur advancing through the mountains to Kabul.jpg|thumb|left|Timurid conqueror [[Babur]] advances through Jam and the mountains to [[Kabul]].]]

During the 12th and 13th century, the Ghurids controlled what is now [[Afghanistan]], but also parts of eastern [[Iran]], [[Central Asia]], Northern [[India]] and parts of [[Pakistan]].<ref name="unesco" />

The Arabic inscription dating the minaret is unclear – it could read 1193/4 or 1174/5 when converted to Gregorian dates. It could thus commemorate the victory of the Ghurid sultan [[Ghiyath al-Din Muhammad|Ghiyas ud-Din]] over the [[Ghaznavids]] in 1186 in [[Lahore]].<ref name="ReferenceA">{{cite book |last=Pinder-Wilson |first=Ralph |title=Iran |year=2001 |volume=39 |page=170 |chapter=Ghaznavid and Ghūrid Minarets}}</ref> However, [[Ralph Pinder-Wilson]], a British Archeologist and Director of the [[British Institute of Afghan Studies]] in the 1970s, wrote a major study of the Minarets of Jam and [[Ghazni Minarets|Ghazni]] in which he expressed his belief that the minaret was built to commemorate the victory of [[Muhammad of Ghor|Mu'izz ad-Din]], Ghiyath ud-Din's brother, over [[Prithviraj Chauhan]]. This victory allowed [[Islam]] to spread into the northern Indian subcontinent.<ref name="ReferenceA" /> Pinder-Wilson believed that the minaret was built in the style of the time, which included a tradition of early Islamic victory towers proclaiming the conquering power of Islam.<ref>{{Cite web |date=14 January 2010 |title=Warwick Ball: Ralph Pinder-Wilson and Afghanistan |url=http://backdoorbroadcasting.net/2010/01/warwick-ball-ralph-pinder-wilson-and-afghanistan/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140529160132/http://backdoorbroadcasting.net/2010/01/warwick-ball-ralph-pinder-wilson-and-afghanistan/ |archive-date=2014-05-29 |website=backdoorbroadcasting.net}}</ref>

It is assumed that the Minaret was attached to the Friday Mosque of Firozkoh, which the Ghurid chronicler [[Minhaj-i Siraj Juzjani]] states was washed away in a flash flood, some time before the Mongol sieges in the early 13th century.{{citation needed|date=May 2014}} Work at Jam by the Minaret of Jam Archaeological Project has found evidence of a large courtyard building beside the minaret, and evidence of river sediments on top of the baked-brick paving.<ref name="antiquity.ac.uk">Minaret of Jam Archaeological Project, http://antiquity.ac.uk/ProjGall/thomas/ {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060426184204/http://antiquity.ac.uk/ProjGall/thomas/ |date=2006-04-26 }}</ref>

The Ghurid Empire's glory waned after the death of Ghiyath ud-Din in 1202, as it was forced to cede territory to the [[Khwarazm]] Empire. Juzjani states that Firuzkuh was destroyed by the [[Mongols]] in 1222.<ref name="antiquity.ac.uk" />

The Minaret was little known outside of Afghanistan until [[Thomas Holdich|Sir Thomas Holdich]] reported it in 1886 while working for the Afghan Boundary Commission. It did not come to world attention, however, until 1957 through the work of the French archaeologists André Maricq<ref>{{cite report |title=La découverte de la capitale des sultans ghorides : XIIe-XIIIe s. |last1=Maricq |first1=André |last2=Wiet |first2=Gaston |year=1959 |publisher=Klincksieck |location=Paris |page=16 |series=Mémoires de la Délégation archéologique française en Afghanistan}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Pinder-Wilson |first=Ralph |title=Iran |year=2001 |volume=39 |page=166 |chapter=Ghaznavid and Ghūrid Minarets}}</ref> and [[Gaston Wiet]]. Later, Werner Herberg conducted limited surveys around the site in the 1970s, and [[Ralph Pinder-Wilson]] completed his major study of the site in the same decade, before the Soviet invasion of 1979 once again cut off outside access.{{Citation needed|date=March 2025}}

== Inscriptional content ==

*The uppermost band consists of the Muslim confession of faith, the [[shahada]]; "''I bear witness there is no god but Allah (and that) Muhammad is the messenger of Allah.''"
*Below this, are upper two bands that consists of verse 13, surat al-Saff LXI;"''Help from Allah and present victory. Give good tidings (O Muhammad) to believers. O ye who believe.''"
*The band below this consists of names and titles of ''[[Ghiyas ad-Din Ghori|Ghiyath ad-Din Muhammad bin Sam]]''
*Located below this is a band containing an amplified version of Ghiyath ad-Din Muhammad's names and titles in turquoise mosaic tiles.
*An oblong hexagon with two lines of ''naskhi'' underneath, (1)"''The work of 'Ali ibn...''", (2)undeciphered
*An inscription, "''Abu'l-Fath''", heavily damaged, due to being made of stucco.
*Interlaced bands consisting of surat Maryam XIX.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Cruickshank |first=Dan |date=2008-04-23 |title=Dan Cruickshank relives his epic journey to a threatened wonder in Afghanistan |url=http://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2008/apr/23/heritage.architecture |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221117122831/https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2008/apr/23/heritage.architecture |archive-date=2022-11-17 |access-date=2022-11-18 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref>
*Facing north is a Kufic inscription, "''On the date of the year five hundred ninety''"(equivalent of 27 December 1193 to 16 December 1194).<ref>{{cite book |last=Pinder-Wilson |first=Ralph |title=Iran |year=2001 |volume=39 |pages=168–169 |chapter=Ghaznavid and Ghūrid Minarets}}</ref>

== Conservation issues ==

=== Threats ===
The Minaret of Jam is threatened by erosion, water infiltration and floods, due to its proximity to the Hari and Jam rivers.<ref name=":1" /><ref name="unesco" /> Another threat is the earthquakes that happen frequently in the region. As a result of these issues, the tower has been deteriorating and slowly tilting.<ref name=":1">{{cite news |author=Cruickshank, Dan |date=23 April 2008 |title=Meeting with a Minaret |url=https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2008/apr/23/heritage.architecture |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170311195601/https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2008/apr/23/heritage.architecture |archive-date=11 March 2017 |access-date=12 December 2016 |work=[[The Guardian]]}}</ref>

Following his 2002 visit, British explorer and future Member of Parliament [[Rory Stewart]] reported that looters and illegal excavations had also damaged the archaeological site surrounding the minaret.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Stewart |first=Rory |author-link=Rory Stewart |title=The places in between |title-link=The Places in Between |date=2006 |publisher=Harcourt, Inc |isbn=978-0-15-603156-1 |edition=1st U.S. |location=Orlando}}</ref>

On 21 July 2018 Pajhwok News reported Taliban clashes with local forces at checkpoints near the Minaret of Jam in a 6-hour long skirmish. The militants set the forests surrounding the historic district on fire, damaging a mosque. Ghor Director of Culture and Information Fakhruddin Ariapoor expressed concern at the instability in the area, stating that some parts of the green area were damaged; and although the minaret remained intact, warned that if the central government did not pay due attention to the security of the site, the militants would destroy it.<ref>{{Cite news |last=خسرو |first=منصور |date=21 July 2018 |title=Taliban storm checkpoints near ancient Ghor minaret |url=https://pajhwok.com/2018/07/21/taliban-storm-checkpoints-near-ancient-ghor-minaret/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180721221832/https://www.pajhwok.com/en/2018/07/21/taliban-storm-checkpoints-near-ancient-ghor-minaret |archive-date=21 July 2018 |access-date=16 March 2025 |language=en}}</ref>

On January 17, 2022, an [[January 2022 Afghanistan earthquakes|earthquake struck western Afghanistan]], killing 28 people. The earthquake caused bricks to fall from the tower and it has since been at an even greater risk of collapsing.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Minaret of Jam, a UNESCO world heritage monument, in danger of 'collapse' after earthquakes |url=https://www.firstpost.com/world/minaret-of-jam-a-unesco-world-heritage-monument-in-danger-of-collapse-after-earthquakes-10303341.html/amp |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220120201506/https://www.firstpost.com/world/minaret-of-jam-a-unesco-world-heritage-monument-in-danger-of-collapse-after-earthquakes-10303341.html/amp |archive-date=2022-01-20 |access-date=2022-01-20 |website=firstpost.com}}</ref>

Threats to the integrity of the structure, including the risk of collapse, have been reported multiple times in recent years due to severe flooding, including in 2014,<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Qazizada |first1=Mohammad |last2=Qarizadah |first2=Daud |date=28 August 2014 |title=Afghan historic minaret of Jam 'in danger of collapse' |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-28969385 |access-date=2025-03-16 |work=BBC News |language=en-GB}}</ref> 2019,<ref>{{Cite web |date=26 May 2019 |title=Afghanistan: Floods threaten Jam minaret |url=https://www.dw.com/en/afghanistan-floods-threaten-communities-minaret-of-jam/a-48877812 |access-date=2025-03-16 |website=DW |language=en}}</ref> and 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Mohammad |date=22 May 2024 |title=Floods and the Destruction of History: Minaret of Jam at Risk of Collapse |url=https://8am.media/eng/floods-and-the-destruction-of-history-minaret-of-jam-at-risk-of-collapse/ |access-date=2025-03-16 |website=Hasht-e Subh |language=en-CA}}</ref>

=== Restoration efforts ===
Stabilisation work was started and carried out in the 1970s, but then lapsed. Work resumed after 2001, but was reported to have stopped again by 2008.<ref name=":1" />
[[File:Jam Qasr Zarafshan.jpg|thumb|258x258px|The Minaret of Jam and Qasr Zarafshan melt into the rugged surrounding landscape – August 2005]]
UNESCO has tried to launch assessment programs. In 2012 UNESCO outlined plans for 3D scanning, hydraulic measurements, and strengthening of support beams and walls to maintain the Minaret, and photos of the external structure have been taken to provide models for future reconstruction. Although the 3D modelling of the minaret was finally carried out for UNESCO by Iconem, political instability has led to a lack of funding and no maintenance efforts have been conducted. Furthermore, the surrounding terrain has made it difficult to make any progress of the structure as it impedes access. However, these assessments have provided a strong basis for future maintenance.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Safeguarding of World Heritage Site of the Minaret of Jam {{!}} United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization |url=http://www.unesco.org/new/en/kabul/culture/programmes-and-projects/safeguarding-of-world-heritage-site-of-the-minaret-of-jam/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181107104027/http://www.unesco.org/new/en/kabul/culture/programmes-and-projects/safeguarding-of-world-heritage-site-of-the-minaret-of-jam/ |archive-date=2018-11-07 |access-date=2018-11-07 |website=www.unesco.org |language=en}}</ref> While fieldwork remains difficult, archaeologists have analyzed satellite images and data from Google Maps to make new discoveries about the minaret and the surrounding site.<ref name=":0"/>

In December 2024, the local governor of [[Ghor Province|Ghor]] announced that consolidation works, including the construction of [[retaining wall]]s, would soon begin, following the damage done by recent floods.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Sayeedi |first=Nasrin |date=10 December 2024 |title=Work on Jam minaret retaining walls to begin soon |url=https://pajhwok.com/2024/12/10/work-on-jam-minaret-retaining-walls-to-begin-soon/ |access-date=2025-03-16 |language=en-GB}}</ref> It was reported in March 2025 that the retaining walls were completed after 50 days of work. However, some experts indicate that more drastic efforts would be needed to prevent long term deterioration.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Rahmati |first=Fidel |date=23 March 2025 |title=Completion of Gabion Wall Project for Minaret of Jam Protection in Ghor Province |url=https://www.khaama.com/completion-of-gabion-wall-project-for-minaret-of-jam-protection-in-ghor-province/ |access-date=2025-07-15 |language=en-GB}}</ref>
