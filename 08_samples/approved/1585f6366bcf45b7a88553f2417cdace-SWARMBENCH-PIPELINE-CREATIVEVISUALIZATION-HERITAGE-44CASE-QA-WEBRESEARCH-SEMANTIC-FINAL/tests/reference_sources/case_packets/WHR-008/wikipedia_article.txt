The '''Kahuzi-Biega National Park''' ({{Langx|sw|Hifadhi ya Taifa ya Kahuzi-Biega}}, {{Langx|fr|Parc national de Kahuzi-Biega}}) is a [[protected area]] near [[Bukavu]] town in eastern [[Democratic Republic of the Congo]]. It is situated near the western bank of [[Lake Kivu]] and the [[Democratic Republic of the Congo–Rwanda border|Rwandan border]].

==History==
The earliest reserve, the Zoological and Forest Reserve of Mount Kahuzi, was created on 27 July 1937 by the then Governor General of the Belgian Colonial administration.{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=68}} That reserve has been part of the Kahuzi-Biega National Park since November 1970 when it was established in 1970 by the Belgian photographer and conservationist [[Adrien Deschryver]]. Five years later, the park was extended to cover {{convert|6,000|km2|abbr=on}}.{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=70}}

The park was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980,<ref name="Unesco" /> under Criterion (x) for its unique habitat of rainforest and diversity of the mammal species, particularly [[eastern lowland gorilla]]s.<ref name="Unesco" />

In 1997 the park was put on the [[List of World Heritage in Danger]] because of the political instability of the region, an influx of refugees and increasing wildlife exploitation.<ref name=Debonnet2004>{{cite journal |author1=Debonnet, G. |author2=Hillman-Smith, K. |name-list-style=amp |year=2004 |title=Supporting protected areas in a time of political turmoil: the case of World Heritage Sites in the Democratic Republic of Congo |journal=Parks |volume=14 |issue=1 |pages=9–16}}</ref>

With an area of {{convert|6,000|km2|abbr=on}}, Kahuzi-Biega is one of the biggest [[national park]]s in the country. Set in both mountainous and lowland terrain, the park lies west of the town of Bukavu in [[South Kivu Province]].{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=68-}} The smaller, {{convert|600|km2|abbr=on}} eastern part of the park lies in the [[Mitumba Mountains]] of the [[Albertine Rift]] in the [[Great Rift Valley]]. The larger, {{convert|5400|km2|abbr=on}} western part is mainly lowland terrain stretching from [[Bukavu]] to [[Kisangani]]. It is drained by the [[Luka (river)|Luka]] and [[Lugulu (river)|Lugulu]] rivers which flow into the [[Lualaba River]].<ref name=Unesco>{{Cite web|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/137|title= Kahuzi-Biega National Park|access-date=27 October 2013|website = UNESCO World Heritage Centre |publisher= United Nations Educational, Scientific, and Cultural Organization}}</ref>  A {{convert|7.4|km|mi|abbr=on}} wide corridor connects the two parts.{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=68-}} Two dormant volcanoes are set within the park's limits and lend their names to it: [[Kahuzi]] ({{convert|3308|m|abbr=on}}) and Biéga ({{convert|2790|m|abbr=on}}).{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=68-}}

===Climate===
The park receives an average annual precipitation of {{convert|1800|mm|in|abbr=on}}. The average maximum temperature in the area is {{convert|18|C|F}} while the minimum is {{convert|10.4|C|F}}.{{sfn|Barume|2000|p=68}}

==Environment==
[[File:Kahuzi-Biega National Park (39423247372).jpg|alt=Kahuzi-Biega National ParK.|thumb|Kahuzi-Biega National Park]]
[[File:20180531 View from the top of Mt. Kahuzi in Kahuzi-Biega National Park.jpg|alt=Mt. Kahuzi in Kahuzi-Biega National Park.jpg|right|thumb|View from the top of Mount Kahuzi]]
[[File:Kahuzi-Biega National Park (39423247252).jpg|thumb|right|Waterfall in the park]]

The park has a rich diversity of flora and fauna. As of a survey conducted in 2003 by the [[Wildlife Conservation Society]], it protects roughly 349 species of birds and 136 species of mammals.<ref name=Unesco/> Over 1,178 plant species have been observed in the highland regions of the park alone.<ref name = Unesco/> Because of its varied topography and habitat types, Kahuzi-Biega National Park is also a [[biodiversity hotspot|hotspot]] for plants and [[endemism|endemic]] vertebrates.<ref name = Unesco/>

===Flora===
The park's swamps, bogs, marshland and [[riparian forest]]s on hydromorphic ground at all altitudes are rare worldwide. The western lowland sector of the park is dominated by dense Guineo-Congolian [[Tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests|wet equatorial rainforest]], part of the [[Northeastern Congolian lowland forests]] ecoregion,<ref name="dopa">[https://dopa-explorer.jrc.ec.europa.eu/wdpa/4328 "Kahuzi-Biega National Park". DOPA Explorer. Accessed 22 March 2022]</ref> with an area of transition forest between {{convert|1200|m|abbr=on}} and {{convert|1500|m|abbr=on}}.

The eastern mountainous sector includes continuous forest vegetation from {{convert|600|m}} to over {{convert|2600|m}}, and is one of the rare sites in Sub-Saharan Africa which demonstrates all stages of the low to highland transition, including six distinguishable primary vegetation types: swamp and [[peat bog]], [[swamp forest]], high-altitude rainforest, mountain rainforest, [[bamboo forest]] and [[subalpine]] [[Ericaceae|heather]].<ref name="Unesco" />

The montane rain forests are part of the [[Albertine Rift montane forests]] ecoregion.<ref name="dopa" /> Mountain and swamp forest grows between {{convert|2000|m|abbr=on}} and {{convert|2400|m|abbr=on}}, bamboo forest grows between {{convert|2350|m|abbr=on}} and {{convert|2600|m|abbr=on}}, and the summits of Mounts Kahuzi and Biéga above {{convert|2600|m}} have subalpine heather, dry savanna, and grasslands, as well as an endemic subspecies of ''[[Dendrosenecio erici-rosenii]]'' (''D. e. erici-rosenii'').<ref name="Unesco" /><ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=o-nMzgMEZlEC&pg=PA154 ''Forests For the Future: Local Strategies for Forest Protection, Economic Welfare and Social Justice''], by Paul Wolvekamp, page 154, [[Zed Books]] (January 1, 2000), {{ISBN|1856497577}}.</ref>

===Fauna===
[[File:Photo d'un gorille, prise en visite touristique au parc de Kauzi Biega au Sud-Kivu.jpg|thumb|Eastern lowland gorilla in the park]]

The national park protects a greater diversity of mammal species than any other national park in the [[Albertine Rift]].<ref name = "Unesco"/> Among the 136 species of mammals identified in the park, the eastern lowland gorilla is the most prominent. According to a 2008 status report of the DR of Congo, the park had 125 lowland gorillas, a marked reduction from the figure of 600 gorillas of the pre-1990s conflict period, and consequently the species has been inscribed on the endangered list. The park is the last refuge of this rare primate.<ref name=World>{{Cite web|url=http://www.worldheritagesite.org/sites/kahuzibiega.html|title=Kahuzi-Biega National Park| access-date=28 October 2013|publisher=World Heritage Site Organization}}</ref> According to the census survey of eastern lowland gorillas reported by the [[Wildlife Conservation Society]] in April 2011, at least 181 gorillas were recorded in the park.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.gorillas.org/eastern_lowland_census|title=Gorillas surviving against the odds in Kahuzi-Biega|date=21 April 2011|access-date=28 October 2013|publisher=Gorilla Organization|archive-date=21 September 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110921121315/http://www.gorillas.org/eastern_lowland_census|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Other primates include the [[eastern chimpanzee]], several species of ''[[Cercopithecinae]]'' (including the [[olive baboon]]) and ''[[Colobinae]]'', as well as [[Hamlyn's monkey]]. Some of the other mammals include the [[forest elephant]], [[forest buffalo]], [[hippopotamus]], [[leopard]], [[giant forest hog]], [[Bongo (antelope)|bongo]], [[needle-clawed bushbaby]], [[Maclaud's horseshoe bat]], [[Ruwenzori otter shrew]], [[Mount Kahuzi climbing mouse]] and [[Alexander's bush squirrel]].<ref name=Unesco/> Two species of [[genet (animal)|genet]] that live within the park are [[endemic]] to the Congo Basin: the [[aquatic genet]] and the [[giant forest genet]].<ref name = Unesco/>

Of the 349 bird species identified within the park, at least 42 of them are [[endemic]] to the region, including the threatened [[Albertine owlet]]. Other native bird species include the [[yellow-crested helmet shrike]],  [[Congo peafowl]], [[African green broadbill]], and [[Rockefeller's sunbird]].<ref name = Unesco/> The park has been designated an [[Important Bird Area]] (IBA) by [[BirdLife International]] because it supports significant populations of many bird species.<ref name=bli>{{cite web |url= https://datazone.birdlife.org/site/factsheet/kahuzi-biega-national-park-iba-congo-the-democratic-republic-of-the|title= Kahuzi-Biega National Park|author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2024|website= BirdLife Data Zone|publisher= BirdLife International|access-date= 2024-12-04}}</ref>

A 2020 survey of the freshwater fish in the park and its three drainage basins identified 147 species, 11 of which were found to be endemic to the [[Lowa (Kivu/Maniema)|Lowa River]] basin, and 7 of which were previously undescribed.<ref name="Kisekelwa2020">{{cite journal|last1=Kisekelwa|first1=Tchalondawa|last2=Snoeks|first2=Jos|last3=Vreven|first3=Emmanuel|title=An annotated checklist of the fish fauna of the river systems draining the Kahuzi‐Biega National Park (Upper Congo: Eastern DR Congo)|journal=Journal of Fish Biology|volume=96|issue=3|year=2020|pages=700–721|issn=0022-1112|doi=10.1111/jfb.14264}}</ref>
[[Cyprinidae]] was by far the largest family represented.

==Conservation and management==
[[File:Petro Radioing the Ranger Base Station.jpg|alt=Kahuzi-Biega National Park|thumb|Park ranger]]

The park, under the management of the [[Institut Congolais pour la Conservation de la Nature]], has a basic management and surveillance structure. However, the park's 1975 expansion, which included inhabited lowland areas, resulted in forced evacuations with about 13,000 people of the tribal community of Shi, Tembo and Rega affected and refusing to leave.<ref name="Unesco" />
Cooperation by the communities living around the park and employment of the [[Twa]] people to enforce park protection was pursued by the park authorities. In 1999, a plan was developed to protect the people and the resources of the park.{{sfn|Barume|2000|pp=72-77}}

Illegal [[artisanal mining]] of [[Coltan mining and ethics#Environmental concerns|Coltan]] happens in the park, thus damaging the deep forest cover.<ref name="Diaz-Struck and Poliszuk 2012">Diaz-Struck and Poliszuk 2012</ref>

In a survey conducted between 2015 and 2016, the national park rangers reported a low level of satisfaction with the job, highlighting low salaries, lack of support from the authorities, and poor living conditions.<ref name="Spira2019">{{cite journal|last1=Spira|first1=Charlotte|last2=Kirkby|first2=Andrew E.|last3=Plumptre|first3=Andrew J.|title=Understanding ranger motivation and job satisfaction to improve wildlife protection in Kahuzi–Biega National Park, eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo|journal=Oryx|volume=53|issue=3|year=2019|pages=460–468|issn=0030-6053|doi=10.1017/S0030605318000856|doi-access=free}}</ref>

===Human rights abuses===
In 2019, national park rangers allegedly engaged in violence against [[Twa|Batwa]] who returned to the park in 2018. This included the alleged killing of a number of residents, the burning of villages, as well as the use of sexual violence against indigenous residents.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Investigation documents murder, rape by DRC national park guards |work=Al Jazeera |year=2022 |access-date=2024-10-21 |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/4/6/investigation-documents-murder-rape-by-drc-national-park-guards}}</ref>

In 2022, the [[African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights|African Commission on Human and Peoples’ Rights]] ruled that the [[Democratic Republic of the Congo]] violated the human rights of Batwa people according to the [[African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights|African Charter]],<ref>{{Cite web |title=Fortress Conservation: Can a Congo Tribe Return to Its Forest? |url=https://e360.yale.edu/features/batwa-kahuzi-biega-national-park-drc#:~:text=The%20African%20Commission's%20ruling%20is,the%20fruits%20of%20these%20lands.%E2%80%9D |access-date=2024-11-15 |website=Yale E360 |language=en-US}}</ref> including by evicting them from ancestral territories without prior consent when the Kahuzi-Biega National Park was created.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Surma |first=Katie |date=2024-07-30 |title=International Human Rights Commission Condemns ‘Fortress Conservation’ |url=https://insideclimatenews.org/news/30072024/africa-human-rights-commission-condemns-fortress-conservation/ |access-date=2024-11-15 |website=Inside Climate News |language=en-US}}</ref> The case was filed in 2015 but the decision was made public in June 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bascomb |first=Bobby |date=2024-08-02 |title=Indigenous Batwa win human rights victory over eviction from DRC park |url=https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/indigenous-batwa-win-human-rights-victory-over-eviction-from-drc-park/ |access-date=2024-11-15 |website=Mongabay Environmental News |language=en-US}}</ref>
