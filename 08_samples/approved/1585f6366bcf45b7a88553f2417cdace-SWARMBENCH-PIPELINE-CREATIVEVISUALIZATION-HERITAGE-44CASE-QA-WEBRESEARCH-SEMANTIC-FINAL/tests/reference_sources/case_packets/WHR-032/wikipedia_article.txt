The '''Medieval Monuments in Kosovo''' ({{lang-sr-Cyrl-Latn|Средњовековни споменици на Косову|Srednjovekovni spomenici na Kosovu}}, {{Langx|sq|Monumentet Mesjetare në Kosovë}}) are a [[World Heritage Site]] consisting of four [[Serbian Orthodox Christian]] churches and [[Serbian monasteries|monasteries]] which represent the fusion of the eastern Orthodox [[Byzantine architecture|Byzantine]] and the western [[Romanesque architecture|Romanesque]] ecclesiastical architecture to form the Palaiologian Renaissance style. The construction was founded by members of [[Nemanjić dynasty]], the most important dynasty of [[Serbia in the Middle Ages]]. The sites are located in [[Kosovo]].

In 2004, [[UNESCO]] recognized the [[Visoki Dečani Monastery|Dečani Monastery]] for its outstanding universal value. Two years later, the site of patrimony was extended as a serial nomination, to include three other religious monuments: [[Patriarchate of Peć Monastery]], [[Our Lady of Ljeviš]] and [[Gračanica Monastery]].

In 2006 the property was inscribed on the [[list of World Heritage in Danger]] due to difficulties in its management and conservation stemming from the region's political instability.<ref>[https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/268 World Heritage Committee puts Medieval Monuments in Kosovo on Danger List and extends site in Andorra, ending this year’s inscriptions], ''[[UNESCO|UNESCO World Heritage Centre]]'', July 13, 2006. Accessed April 4, 2008.</ref>

==UNESCO controversy==
There is an ongoing controversy over Kosovo's bid to join UNESCO, which would result in the sites being listed as part of Kosovo and not Serbia.<ref>{{citation|url=https://www.rferl.org/a/kosovo-serbia-fights-unesco-membership/27320037.html | title=Controversy Rages Over Kosovo's UNESCO Membership | website=Radio Free Europe | date=October 22, 2015}}</ref> These monuments have come under attack, especially during the [[2004 unrest in Kosovo|ethnic violence]] in 2004, during Kosovo's [[United Nations Interim Administration Mission in Kosovo|UNMIK]] rule, when the [[Our Lady of Ljeviš]] was heavily damaged.<ref>{{cite web|title=Safeguarding of Cultural Heritage in Kosovo |url=http://www.unesco.org/new/en/venice/culture/safeguarding-cultural-heritage/capacity-building/cultural-heritage-kosovo/|publisher=[[UNESCO]]|date=2005}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Protection and Preservation of Cultural Heritage in Kosovo |url=http://unesdoc.unesco.org/images/0013/001395/139567eb.pdf|publisher=[[UNESCO]]|date=2005|pages=7, 8}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=The Violence: Ethnic Albanian Attacks on Serbs and Roma |url=https://www.hrw.org/reports/2004/kosovo0704/7.htm|publisher=[[Human Rights Watch]]|date=July 2004}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Reconstruction Implementation Commission for Serbian Orthodox Religious Sites in Kosovo|url=http://www.coe.int/t/DG4/CULTUREHERITAGE/COOPERATION/RIC/inc/eng/home.html|publisher=[[Council of Europe]]|date=2005}}</ref> In October 2015 Kosovo was recommended for membership by the UNESCO Executive Board. The bid for membership was voted at the UNESCO General Conference in November 2015.<ref>{{Cite news|url = https://www.rferl.org/a/kosovo-serbia-unesco-membership/27319064.html|title = Kosovo Moves Closer To UNESCO Membership}}</ref>

[[UNESCO]] has not accepted [[Kosovo]] as a member, the proposal failed to gain a two-thirds majority at the organization's General Conference in [[Paris]] on November 9, 2015.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://tass.ru/en/world/834899|title=UNESCO General Conference rejects membership for Kosovo}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2015/11/09/world/europe/ap-unesco-kosovo.html |title=UNESCO Rejects Membership for Kosovo in Victory for Serbia - The New York Times |website=www.nytimes.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151110032815/http://www.nytimes.com/aponline/2015/11/09/world/europe/ap-unesco-kosovo.html |archive-date=2015-11-10}}</ref> One of primary reasons for this rejection of Albanian request<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.b92.net/eng/news/politics.php?yyyy=2015&mm=09&dd=14&nav_id=95418|title=FM: Albania files request for Kosovo's UNESCO admission}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.independent.mk/articles/21960/Albania+Applied+for+Kosovo's+Admission+to+UNESCO |title=Albania Applied for Kosovo's Admission to UNESCO - Independent.mk |access-date=2015-11-09 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170529041749/http://www.independent.mk/articles/21960/Albania+Applied+for+Kosovo%27s+Admission+to+UNESCO |archive-date=2017-05-29 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.businessinsider.com/ap-kosovo-hopes-to-join-unesco-vows-to-protect-serb-heritage-2015-11 |title=Kosovo hopes to join UNESCO; vows to protect Serb heritage - Business Insider |website=www.businessinsider.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222104425/http://www.businessinsider.com/ap-kosovo-hopes-to-join-unesco-vows-to-protect-serb-heritage-2015-11 |archive-date=2015-12-22}}</ref> is [[2004 unrest in Kosovo]], when 35 [[Eastern Christianity|Orthodox]] churches were [[Desecration|desecrated]], damaged or destroyed,<ref>[http://www.coe.int/t/DG4/CULTUREHERITAGE/COOPERATION/RIC/inc/eng/docs/2005_eng.pdf RIC, RECONSTRUCTION IMPLEMENTATION COMMISSION FOR ORTHODOX RELIGIOUS SITES IN KOSOVO ACTIVITY REPORT]</ref><ref name="b92">{{cite web |url=http://www.b92.net/eng/news/politics.php?yyyy=2010&mm=03&dd=17&nav_id=65852|title=Six years since March violence in Kosovo |publisher=[[B92]] |date=17 March 2010 |accessdate=7 September 2015}}</ref> including [[Our Lady of Ljeviš]] which is a [[World Heritage Site]].<ref>For details and photos see here: http://www.spc.rs/eng/prizren_our_lady_ljevis_xiv_century_burnt_inside_photo_2232004</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Eighth anniversary of violence against KiM Serbs |url=https://orthochristian.com/52282.html |access-date=2022-09-30 |website=OrthoChristian.Com}}</ref> The church is subject to constant looting, even of its construction material, with valuable lead having repeatedly been stolen from the roof.<ref>See the link: {{cite web |url=http://theorthodoxchurch.info/blog/news/2011/04/church-roof-stolen-in-prizren-bishop-protests/ |title=News &#124;
