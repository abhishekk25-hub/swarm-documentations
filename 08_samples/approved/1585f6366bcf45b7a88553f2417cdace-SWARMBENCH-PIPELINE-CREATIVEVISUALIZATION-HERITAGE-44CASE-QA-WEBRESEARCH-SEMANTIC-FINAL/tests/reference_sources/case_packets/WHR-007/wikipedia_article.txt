'''Garamba National Park''' ({{Langx|fr|Parc national de la Garamba}}) is a [[national park]] in the north-eastern [[Democratic Republic of the Congo]] covering nearly {{Cvt|2000|sqmi|km2|order=flip}}. It is among Africa's oldest parks and was designated a [[World Heritage Site]] by [[UNESCO]] in 1980 for its protection of critical habitat for [[northern white rhinoceros]]es, [[African elephant]]s, [[hippopotamus]]es, and [[giraffe]]s.<ref name = unesco>{{cite web |url=http://whc.unesco.org/en/list/136 |title=Garamba National Park |website=UNESCO World Heritage Centre |publisher=United Nations Educational, Scientific, and Cultural Organization |access-date=13 Jun 2021}}</ref> Garamba National Park has been managed by [[African Parks]] in partnership with the [[Institut Congolais pour la Conservation de la Nature]] since 2005.

==Overview==
Garamba National Park was established in 1938 and covers an area of {{Convert|4900|km2|sqmi|abbr=on}} in northeastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is bounded by [[Gangala-na-Bodio Hunting Reserve]] on the west, south, and east, and borders [[South Sudan]] on the north and northeast. It is part of the Sudano–[[Guinea savanna woodland|Guinean savanna]] zone.<ref>{{cite journal | title=The spatial correlates of wildlife distribution around Garamba National Park, Democratic Republic of Congo | author=De Merode, E., Hillman-Smith, K., Nicholas, A., Ndey, A. and Likango, M. | journal=International Journal of Remote Sensing | year=2000 | volume=21 | issue=13–14 | pages=2665–2683 | doi=10.1080/01431160050110223| s2cid=3495760 }}</ref><ref name="NG">{{cite web |title=Go on Patrol with Elephant Guardians in New 360 Film |work=[[National Geographic (magazine)|National Geographic]]|date=2017 |url=http://news.nationalgeographic.com/2017/05/wildlife-watch-360-videos-the-protectors-garamba-poaching/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170513191337/http://news.nationalgeographic.com/2017/05/wildlife-watch-360-videos-the-protectors-garamba-poaching/|url-status=dead|archive-date=13 May 2017|access-date=3 October 2017 |first1=Jani|last1=Actman|first2=Rachael|last2=Bale|issn=0027-9358|oclc=643483454}}</ref> The park is one of Africa's oldest protected areas.<ref name="Gettleman">{{cite news |last1=Gettleman|first1=J. |title=Elephants Dying in Epic Frenzy as Ivory Fuels Wars and Profits|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2012/09/04/world/africa/africas-elephants-are-being-slaughtered-in-poaching-frenzy.html|access-date=6 October 2017|work=[[The New York Times]]|date=2012|issn=0362-4331|oclc=1645522}}</ref> It lies in the transition zone between two centres of [[endemism]]: Guinea-Congolian and Guinean-Sudanese savanna. These two biogeographic zones support a variety of wildlife, which have experienced population declines in recent decades because of [[poaching]].<ref name="Canby">{{cite magazine |last1=Canby|first1=P. |title=Shootout in Garamba |magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|date=2016|url=https://www.newyorker.com/news/news-desk/shootout-in-garamba|access-date=3 October 2017 |publisher=[[Condé Nast]] |issn=0028-792X|oclc=320541675}}</ref> ICCN rangers, augmented with soldiers of the [[Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo]], work to protect Garamba from poachers and rebel groups.<ref name=Canby/>

==History==
The national park was established in 1938.<ref name=Gettleman/><ref name=Avant/>

The park was designated a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 1980, and was included on the [[List of specialized agencies of the United Nations|specialized agency]]'s List of World Heritage in Danger from 1984–1992.<ref>{{cite web|title=World Heritage Committee: Eighth Ordinary Session|url=https://whc.unesco.org/archive/1984/sc-84-conf004-9e.pdf|publisher=UNESCO|access-date=4 October 2017|date=1984}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=World Heritage Committee: Twentieth Session|url=https://whc.unesco.org/archive/1996/whc-96-conf201-21e.pdf|publisher=UNESCO|access-date=4 October 2017|page=32}}</ref> Between 1991 and 1993, 50,000 Sudanese refugees settled outside Garamba after being displaced by the [[Sudan People's Liberation Army]], resulting in increased poaching. There were reportedly 121 conflicts between poachers and park rangers between 1993 and 1995, and more than 900 weapons recovered by rangers during 1991–1996.<ref name=Avant/> The [[International Rhino Foundation]] provided funding for patrol vehicles in 1994, and ranger salaries in 1995. In February 1996, two rhinos—one male, Bawesi, and Juliet, a pregnant female—were killed.<ref name=Avant/> Garamba was added back to the danger list in 1996 because of northern white rhinoceros poaching.<ref name="BBC">{{cite news|title=Prince William in plan to tackle wildlife trafficking|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-35814135|access-date=4 October 2017|publisher=[[BBC]]|date=15 March 2016}}</ref><ref name=Heale/> Following a number of defeats during the [[First Congo War]] and [[Second Sudanese Civil War]], most importantly [[Operation Thunderbolt (1997)|Operation Thunderbolt]], the [[Uganda]]n insurgents of [[West Nile Bank Front|WNBF]] and [[Uganda National Rescue Front|UNRF (II)]] took refuge in Garamba National Park in 1997.{{sfnp|Prunier|2004|p=377}} Organizations like the [[Frankfurt Zoological Society]], [[International Union for Conservation of Nature]], and [[World Wildlife Fund]] have also worked with local authorities to rehabilitate Garamba.<ref name=Avant/>

African Parks' anti-poaching efforts have reportedly been successful at reducing animal deaths.<ref name=Nicolon/> According to [[BBC]], Garamba's management has been financed by the [[European Union]] and private donors.<ref name=BBC/> Other contributors have included the [[United States Agency for International Development]], [[United States Fish and Wildlife Service]], Wildcat Foundation, and [[World Bank]],<ref>{{cite web|title=State of Conservation: Garamba National Park (Democratic Republic of the Congo)|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/soc/3137|publisher=UNESCO|access-date=4 December 2017}}</ref> according to African Parks.<ref>{{cite web|title=Partners: Garamba|url=https://www.african-parks.org/the-parks/garamba/partners|publisher=African Parks|access-date=4 December 2017}}</ref>

Since 2005, the protected area is considered a [[Central African lion|Lion]] Conservation Unit together with [[Domaine Chasse Bili Uere]].<ref>{{cite book |author=IUCN Cat Specialist Group |year=2006 |title= Conservation Strategy for the Lion ''Panthera leo'' in Eastern and Southern Africa |publisher=IUCN |location=Pretoria, South Africa}}</ref>

Poachers have killed at least 21 park rangers in the last decade, as of 2017, and security concerns have contributed to Garamba's struggle to establish itself as a tourist destination.<ref name=NG/> [[Joseph Kony]]'s [[Lord's Resistance Army]] have used the park for sanctuary.<ref name=Canby/><ref name=Nicolon/><ref name="Chandrasekaran">{{cite news|last1=Chandrasekaran|first1=Rajiv|title=Kony 2013: U.S. Quietly Intensifies Effort to Help African Troops Capture Infamous Warlord|url=https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-35308526.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150116101149/http://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-35308526.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=16 January 2015|access-date=9 October 2017|newspaper=The Washington Post|date=29 October 2013}}</ref> In 2009, the guerrilla group attacked Garamba's Nagero station, killing at least eight people, including two park rangers, and wounding an additional thirteen. Rebels also stole food and fuel, and destroyed several of the park's buildings.<ref name=Nicolon/> Poachers killed five rangers and three members of the Congolese armed forces in three conflicts in ==Flora and fauna==
The park's savannah grasslands support a low density of [[Acacia sensu lato|acacia]]s.<ref name=Canby/><ref name=Nicolon/> Some of the Garamba's grasses can grow as high as {{Convert|3|m|0|abbr=on}}.<ref name=NG/>

===Mammals===
Garamba is home to 138 recorded mammal species,<ref name=Avant/> including various [[antelope]],<ref name=East>{{cite book|last1=East|first1=R.|title=Antelopes: Global Survey and Regional Action Plans, Part 3 |date=1990|publisher=[[International Union for Conservation of Nature]]|page=131|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=n22ER5vZqTkC&pg=PA131|access-date=6 October 2017|isbn=9782831700168}}</ref> [[African buffalo]], [[African bush elephant]], [[hyena]]s, [[giant forest hog]], [[giraffe]]s, [[hippopotamus]], and lion.<ref name=Canby/><ref name=Nicolon/><ref name=Dasgupta/> The park hosts the country's only remaining population of giraffes, the [[Kordofan giraffe|Kordofan]] subspecies, and one of the country's largest remaining elephant populations.<ref name=Canby/><ref name="Nicolon">{{cite web|last1=Nicolon|first1=T. |title=DRC's Garamba National Park: The last giraffes of the Congo |date=2017|url=https://news.mongabay.com/2017/05/drcs-garamba-national-park-the-last-giraffes-of-the-congo/|work=[[Mongabay]]|access-date=2 October 2017}}</ref>

Garamba's elephants are considered a hybrid of the [[African bush elephant|African bush]] and [[African forest elephant|African forest]] subspecies.<ref name=Canby/> Poaching has reduced the park's elephant population in recent decades.<ref name=Gettleman/> There were around 2,800 elephants in 2011.<ref name=Gettleman/> In 2017, there were estimated to be fewer than 2,000 elephants in Garamba, a significant decline from the approximately 20,000 reported in the 1960s and 1970s.<ref name=Canby/><ref name=NG/><ref name="Dasgupta">{{cite news|last1=Dasgupta|first1=S. |title=Two wildlife rangers killed by poachers in Democratic Republic of the Congo|url=https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2017/apr/25/two-wildlife-rangers-killed-democratic-republic-congo-elephants|access-date=3 October 2017|work=[[The Guardian]] |date=2017}}</ref> 22 elephants were killed in 2012,<ref name=Gettleman/> and in 2014 poachers killed 68 of Garamba's elephants within two months.<ref name=Dasgupta/><ref name=NYDN>{{cite news|url=http://www.nydailynews.com/news/world/68-elephants-killed-poachers-africa-national-park-article-1.1829165|work=[[New York Daily News]] |date=2014 |title=Nearly 70 elephants slaughtered by poachers at national park in Africa: officials |access-date=6 October 2017}}</ref><ref name="Min">{{cite news|last1=Min |first1=A. |title=68 elephants killed in the last two months at African wildlife refuge|url=https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/68-elephants-killed-last-two-months-african-wildlife-refuge |access-date=6 October 2017 |work=[[PBS]] |date=2014}}</ref> Elephant poaching numbers declined from 98 in 2015 to three in 2022 in result to an effective law enforcement strategy.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Shining a Spotlight on Garamba|url=https://www.africanparks.org/shining-spotlight-garamba|publisher=African Parks |access-date=22 August 2025}}</ref>

Reported giraffe population sizes have varied, but show a general decline in the 1990s and 2000s. The population's recorded peak was 300 in 1976, and more than 100 were reported in 2008.<ref name=Nicolon/> There were 356 giraffes in 1993, and only 86 by 2007.<ref name="TheTimes">{{cite news|url=https://www.thetimes.com/travel/destinations/africa-travel/south-africa/congo-giraffes-near-to-extinction-3jnqmzvr7dm|access-date=6 October 2017|work=[[The Times]] |date=2016 |title=Congo giraffes near to extinction|first=J. |last=Starkey}}</ref> There were 86 in 2003 and only 38 in 2016.<ref name="Hinckley">{{cite news|last1=Hinckley|first1=S. |title=Why Is This Rare Giraffe Almost Extinct?|url=https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1G1-444577688.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180413124541/https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1G1-444577688.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=13 April 2018|access-date=9 October 2017|work=[[The Christian Science Monitor]] |date=2016}}</ref> As of 2022, there were 71 giraffes in the park, and in 2024, over 80 Kordofan giraffes were allegedly present.<ref name="Parks">{{cite web |title=Garamba National Park |url=https://www.africanparks.org/the-parks/garamba |work=African Parks |access-date=27 February 2024}}</ref> Poaching is the greatest threat to Garamba's giraffe population.<ref name=Nicolon/><ref>{{Cite news|title=KORDOFAN GIRAFFE CONSERVATION IN DRC|url=https://giraffeconservation.org/programmes/kordofan-giraffe-conservation-in-drc/|publisher=Giraffe Conservation Foundation|access-date=16 December 2020}}</ref>

The park once had the last wild population of the [[northern white rhinoceros]].<ref name=Canby/><ref name=Gettleman/><ref name=Heale>{{cite book|title=Democratic Republic of the Congo |date=2009 |publisher=[[Marshall Cavendish]]|page=67|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=9nxFwxF7nCsC&pg=PA67|access-date=4 October 2017|first1=J. |last1=Heale|first2=J. L. |last2=Yong|isbn=9780761444787}}</ref> Only fifteen northern white rhinos were reported in Garamba in the mid 1980s, prompting the park's inclusion on [[UNESCO]]'s [[List of World Heritage in Danger]].<ref name=Avant/> In 2003–2004, there were reportedly between 20 and 25 white rhinos in the park.<ref name="Sieff">{{cite news|last1=Sieff|first1=K. |title=Lumbering into Extinction|url=https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-38413550.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180413043259/https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-38413550.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=13 April 2018|access-date=9 October 2017 |work=[[The Washington Post]] |date=2015}}</ref><ref name="Bisset">{{cite news|last1=Bisset|first1=S. |title=Congolese rebel troops push rare white rhino toward extinction|url=https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-1507796.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180413124627/https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-1507796.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=13 April 2018|access-date=9 October 2017 |work=[[Chicago Sun-Times]] |date=2003}}</ref><ref name="Irish">{{cite news |title=Warning over loss of white rhino|url=https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-24743522.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180413124544/https://www.highbeam.com/doc/1P2-24743522.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=13 April 2018 |access-date=9 October 2017 |work=[[The Irish Times]] |date=2004}}</ref> In 2023, restoration efforts led to 16 white rhinos being moved from a private game reserve in South Africa to the park.<ref name="rhino">{{cite web |title=Making Moves to Save a Species |url=https://www.africanparks.org/making-moves-save-species |publisher=African Parks |access-date=27 February 2024}}</ref>

===Birds===
Some 286 bird species, including the [[secretarybird]],<ref name=Gettleman/> have been recorded in the park.<ref name="Avant">{{cite book|last1=Avant|first1=Deborah D.|title=The Market for Force: The Consequences of Privatizing Security|date=25 July 2005|publisher=[[Cambridge University Press]]|pages=205–206|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=TJ3CzP2MiZUC&pg=PA205|access-date=4 October 2017|isbn=9780521615358}}</ref> It has been designated an [[Important Bird Area]] (IBA) by [[BirdLife International]] because it supports significant populations of many bird species.<ref name=bli>{{cite web |url= https://datazone.birdlife.org/site/factsheet/garamba-national-park-iba-congo-the-democratic-republic-of-the |title= Garamba National Park|author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2024|website= BirdLife Data Zone|publisher= BirdLife International|access-date= 2024-12-04}}</ref>

===Threats===
Interactions between people and wild animals in and around the park has resulted in [[human–wildlife conflict]]. Local and South Sudanese poachers hunt for elephants, often in search of [[Ivory trade|ivory]].<ref name=Nicolon/><ref name=Dasgupta/> Garamba's wildlife are also impacted by groups such as the Huda and [[Wodaabe]] (or "Mbororo") seeking [[pasture]].<ref name=Nicolon/>
