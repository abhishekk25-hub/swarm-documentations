The '''Saint Sophia Cathedral''' ({{langx|uk|Софійський собор|Sofiiskyi sobor}} or {{lang|uk|Софія Київська}}, ''Sofiia Kyivska'') in [[Kyiv]], Ukraine, is an [[Architecture of Kyivan Rus|architectural monument of Kyivan Rus']]. The former cathedral is one of the city's best known landmarks and the first heritage site in Ukraine to be inscribed on the [[World Heritage List]] along with the [[Kyiv Pechersk Lavra|Kyiv Cave Monastery]] complex. The [[World Heritage Committee]] of UNESCO decided in June 2013 that Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, and St Sophia Cathedral along with its related monastery buildings would remain on the World Heritage List. Aside from its main building, the cathedral includes an ensemble of supporting structures such as a [[Bell Tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral|bell tower]] and the House of Metropolitan. 

In 2011 the historic site was reassigned from the jurisdiction of the [[Ministry of Regional Development (Ukraine)|Ministry of Regional Development]] of Ukraine to the [[Ministry of Culture and Information Policy|Ministry of Culture]] of Ukraine. One of the reasons for the move was that both Saint Sophia Cathedral and Kyiv Pechersk Lavra are recognized by the [[World Heritage Site|UNESCO World Heritage Program]] as one complex, while in Ukraine the two were governed by different government entities. The cathedral is a museum.

The complex of the cathedral is the main component and museum of the [[National Reserve "Sophia of Kyiv"]] which is the state institution responsible for the preservation of the cathedral complex as well as four other historic landmarks across the nation.

==History==
===Early years===
The Saint Sophia Cathedral (often referred to as St Sophia's Cathedral) was built as a [[Metropolitan_bishop#Eastern_Orthodox_Church|metropolitan cathedral]] for [[Kyiv]] during the reign of [[Yaroslav the Wise]].<ref name="Zha" /> An earlier wooden church with the same name may have existed.{{sfn|Boeck|2009|p=283}} According to the historian Volodymyr Berezhynsky, the cathedral was founded on the site of the battlefield where Yaruslav's [[Kievan Rus']] army defeated the nomadic [[Pechenegs]] in 1036 following the [[Siege of Kiev (1036)|siege of Kyiv that year]].{{sfn|Berezhynsky |1996}} 

====Foundation date====
The limited and contradictory nature of the evidence for the cathedral's origins has caused its foundation and construction dates to be contested.{{sfn|Boeck|2009|p=283}} The exact dates theorised by researchers have for many years been based on four sources—the ''[[Primary Chronicle]]'' (also known as ''The Tale of Bygone Years''), ''[[Novgorod First Chronicle|The First Chronicle of Novgorod]]'', the ''{{Ill|lt=Chronicle|Chronicle of Thietmar|pl|Kronika Thietmara}}'' of [[Thietmar of Merseburg]], and the ''[[Sermon on Law and Grace]]''. The most reliable of these sources, ''The Tale of Bygone Years'', states that the cathedral was founded in 1037, but some scholars have interpreted this as being the year of completion.<ref name="Zha">{{cite encyclopedia |last=Zharkikh |first=M.I. |author-link= |editor-last=Smoliy |editor-first=V.A. |editor-link= |encyclopedia=Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine |title=Софійський собор у Києві |trans-title=Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv |url=http://history.org.ua/LiberUA/978-966-00-1290-5/978-966-00-1290-5.pdf |chapter-url= |access-date= |language= |edition= |date=2012 |year= |publisher=Institute of the History of Ukraine of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine |series= |volume=9 |location= |id= |isbn=978-966-00-1290-5 |issn= |oclc= |doi= |pages=720{{ndash}}722 |quote= |ref=}}</ref> <!-- text at http://resource.history.org.ua/cgi-bin/eiu/history.exe?&I21DBN=EIU&P21DBN=EIU&S21STN=1&S21REF=10&S21FMT=eiu_all&C21COM=S&S21CNR=20&S21P01=0&S21P02=0&S21P03=TRN=&S21COLORTERMS=0&S21STR=Sofijskyj_sobor --> The [[chronicle]] states that in 1037, “…Yaroslavl founded a great city . . . [he] also founded the church of St. Sophia, a metropolitan [church]." The ''Novgorod Chronicle'' gives 1017 as being the foundation year of the cathedral: “In the year [1017] Yaroslav founded a great city Kyiv, and put up golden gates, and founded the church of St. Sophia." This is in comparison with the ''Primary Chronicle''{{'}}s entry for 1017: “Yaroslavl entered Kiev, and churches burned down.” A third date is provided by Thietmar of Merseburg, who wrote that Prince [[Bolesław II the Bold |Boleslaw]] was present at the cathedral in 1018.{{sfn|Boeck|2009|p=283}}

An alternative theory for the foundation date has been proposed by the Ukrainian historian [[Nadiia Nikitenko]]. According to Nikitenko, the cathedral was founded in 1011, under the reign of Yaroslav's father, [[Vladimir the Great]], the [[Grand Prince of Kyiv]]. Her hypothesis, which suggests that the cathedral was founded on 4 November 1011 and was [[consecrated]] on 11 May 1018, is based on a new interpretation of painted images and [[graffiti]] on the cathedral walls dated by Nikitenko to between 1018 and 1036.{{sfn|Nikitenko|Kornienko|2012|p=232}}

The 1011 date has been accepted by both [[UNESCO]] and the [[Ukrainian government]], which officially celebrated the 1000th anniversary of the cathedral during 2011.<ref name="Nik">{{cite web |last1=Nikitenko |first1=Nadia |title=1000&річчя Софії Київської |url=https://www.mao.kiev.ua/biblio/jscans/svitogliad/svit-2010-22-2/svit-2010-22-2-46-nikitenko.pdf |publisher=MAO NASU |access-date=16 March 2026 |location=Kyiv, Ukraine |date=2011 |trans-title=The Millennium of St. Sophia of Kyiv}}</ref><ref name="Zak1">{{cite web |title=Про відзначення 1000-річчя                    заснування Софійського собору |url=https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/682/2010#Text |publisher=Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine |access-date=20 March 2026 |language=uk |date=2010 |trans-title=On the celebration of the 1000th anniversary                  of the founding of St. Sophia Cathedral}}</ref>

===Medieval period===
[[File:Kyev Zakrvsky map 05 (cropped).png|thumb|{{Ill|Nikolai Zakrevsky|uk|Закревський Микола Васильович}}, A detail of the "Plan of Old Kyiv" from ''Description of Kyiv'' (1868)]]
The cathedral was designed as a burial place for Yaroslav the Wise and his descendants. He was {{Ill|Sarcophagus of Yaroslav the Wise |lt=interred in a marble sarcophagus|uk|Саркофаг Ярослава Мудрого}}, now located in the northern inner {{Ill|gallery (building) |lt=gallery|fr|Galerie (architecture)}}. It is probable that the [[List of metropolitans and patriarchs of Kyiv|metropolitan bishops of Kyiv]] were also buried in the cathedral (prior to the absorption of the Kyivan Rus' lands into the [[Mongol Empire]]), the earliest known example being the burial of [[Kirill II of Kiev|Cyril II]] in the 1280s.<ref name="Zha" /> The tombs of other [[Grand Princes of Kiev]] known to have been buried in the cathedral have not survived, such as those of [[Vsevolod I of Kiev|Vsevolod I]] (reigned 1078{{ndash}}1093), [[Vladimir II Monomakh]] (reigned 1113{{ndash}}1125), and [[Viacheslav I of Kiev|Viacheslav I]] (reigned 1154).{{sfn|Heydenstein|1874|pp=9{{ndash}}37}}{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=110}}

In 1169, [[Sack of Kiev (1169)|Kyiv was sacked]] after its capture by the troops of [[Andrey Bogolyubsky]] of [[Vladimir-Suzdal]], who [[looted]] the cathedral's [[relic]]s and other treasures, and in particular the [[Virgin of Vladimir|icon of the Mother of God]].{{sfn|Heydenstein|1874|pp=9{{ndash}}37}}<ref>{{cite web |title=3 червня – Володимирська (Києво-Вишгородська) ікона Божої Матері |url=https://rivne.church.ua/sviati-dnia/3-chervnya-volodymyrskoyi-kyyevo-vyshhorodskoyi-ikony-bozhoyi-materi/ |website=Rivne Eparchy |access-date=23 March 2026 |language=uk |trans-title=June 3 – Volodymyr (Kyiv-vyshorod) icon of the Mother of God}}</ref> In November 1240, [[Siege of Kiev (1240)|Kyiv was besieged by the Mongols]] under the leadership of [[Batu Khan]]. St Sophia was damaged but not destroyed by the Mongols; like other city churches, it was looted when Kyiv was plundered by the victors. The city did not recover its former importance or prosperity for centuries following the [[Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus']].{{sfn|Heydenstein|1874|pp=9{{ndash}}37}}{{sfn|Plokhy|2015|p=71}} In 1203 the city was captured by [[Roman the Great]], and the cathedral was robbed by [[Anna II of Kiev]] (the wife of [[Rurik Rostislavich]]) and their allies.{{sfn|Heydenstein|1874|pp=9{{ndash}}37}}

During the decline of Kyiv, the cathedral continued as a metropolitan church, but the building slowly became dilapidated, and the western end in particular suffered from years of neglect.<ref name="Zha" />

Most modern scholars believe that the first chronicle from [[Kyivan Rus']] emerged in the 1030s and probably originated from St Sophia. Only later did chronicle writing in Ukraine move to the [[Kyiv Pechersk Lavra]].{{sfn|Plokhy|2015|p=57}} {{Ill|LIbrary of Yaruslav the Wise|lt=The first Kyivan Rus' library|uk|Бібліотека Ярослава Мудрого}} was established in the cathedral by Yaroslav the Wise in 1037. It is known that there were over 1000 books in the collection, which was maintained by the cathedral's  [[monks]]. According to one story, the monks hid the library's most valuable treasures in a secret underground passage when the city was being attacked—the hidden location has never been discovered, despite numerous attempts.<ref name="Pal">{{cite news |last1=Palamarchuk |first1=Marichka |title=Curiosities of Kyiv: St. Sophia Cathedral |url=https://www.kyivpost.com/post/20483 |access-date=21 March 2026 |work=[[Kyiv Post]] |date=13 August 2023}}</ref>

===16th{{ndash}}19th century===
[[File:Sazhin Saint Sophia's Cathedral in Kiev.jpg|thumb|[[Mikhail Sazhin (painter)|Mikhail Sazhin]], ''Interior of St. Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv'' (1854), [[National Museum in Warsaw]]]]
From 1497 until 1577, the cathedral was left abandoned, until in that year Bogusz Gulkevych-Glibovsky renovated the cathedral at his own expense.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=457}} In 1596, following the [[Union of Brest]], ownership of the cathedral was transferred to the [[Ruthenian Uniate Church]].{{sfn|Heydenstein|1874|pp=9{{ndash}}37}} From the formation of the [[Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church]] in 1596 until the official re-acceptance of Orthodox Christianity in 1632, St. Sophia’s Cathedral was used as place of worship for Greek Catholics;<ref name="Rus1">{{cite web |title=Kiev Sophia |url=https://russiaglobal.omeka.fas.harvard.edu/exhibits/show/objects/icon/sophia |website=Russia in Global Perspective |access-date=21 March 2026}}</ref> as a result of the acceptance of the union by Metropolitan [[Michael Rohoza]], St Sophia was under the jurisdiction of the Union Church.<ref name="Zha" /> 

On 2 July 1633, Mohyla regained authority over the cathedral and founded a [[monastery]],<ref name="Zha" /> after which the cathedral became the property of the restored Orthodox [[Metropolis of Kyiv]] of the [[Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople]].{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|pp=15{{ndash}}16}} In 1786 the monastery was abolished, and its lands were confiscated.<ref name="Yar">{{cite encyclopedia |last=Yaremenko |first=Maksym Vasyliovych |editor-last= |editor-first= |encyclopedia=Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine |title=Софійський монастир у києві |trans-title=Saint Sophia Monastery in Kyiv |url=http://www.history.org.ua/?termin=Sofiivskyj_monastyr |language=uk |edition= |year= |publisher= |volume= |location= |isbn= |issn= |oclc= |pages= }}</ref> In 1697, the wooden buildings were all destroyed in a fire.<ref name="Pal">{{cite news |last1=Palamarchuk |first1=Marichka |title=Curiosities of Kyiv: St. Sophia Cathedral |url=https://www.kyivpost.com/post/20483 |access-date=21 March 2026 |work=[[Kyiv Post]] |date=13 August 2023}}</ref>

====Early rebuilding and restoration work====
During the reign of [[Petro Mohyla]] (1633{{ndash}}1647),<ref name="OhlZhu">{{cite web |last1=Ohloblyn |first1=Oleksander |last2 =Zhukovsky|first2=Arkadii  |title=Mohyla, Petro
 |url=https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CM%5CO%5CMohylaPetro.htm |website=Internet Encyclopaedia of Ukraine |publisher=Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies |access-date=26 March 2026}}</ref> restoration work was begun on the cathedral. Its appearance in the mid-17th century is known from the drawings of [[Abraham van Westerveld]] and the description of [[Paul of Aleppo]].<ref name="Zha" /> According to their contemporary accounts, the church had 70 altars, and 36 crosses installed on its cupolas.<ref name="Net" /> Mohyla had a new [[iconostasis]] built, and two side altars in the external galleries installed.<ref name="Zha" /> Mohyla involved the Italian architect Octaviano Mancini in the restoration work on the cathedral,<ref name="Rus1">{{cite web |title=Kiev Sophia |url=https://russiaglobal.omeka.fas.harvard.edu/exhibits/show/objects/icon/sophia |website=Russia in Global Perspective |access-date=21 March 2026}}</ref> which he carried out by Mancini in collaboration with the Ukrainian brothers Timish and Ivan Zinoviev, the gilder Yakym Evtykhiev, the icon painter Joachim, and carvers, carpenters, and masons from Moscow.{{sfn|Galganova|2011|p=50}} Instead of maintaining a Byzantine style of architecture for the exterior of the cathedral, Mohyla and his architects were influenced by western European ideas, transformed St Sophia to give it the outward appearance of a Baroque church.{{sfn|Plokhy|2015|p=112}} The reconstruction work was continued after Mohyla's death in 1647 by Metropolitan [[Sylvester Kosiv]], until his death in 1657.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=16}}

Between 1690 and 1707 a major renovation of St Sophia was overseen by the Metropolitan {{Ill|Varlaam Yasinsky|uk|Варлаам (Ясинський)}}, with the financial assistance of Hetman [[Ivan Mazepa]]. The cathedral's galleries were raised, four new domes were added, and the tops of the towers were rebuilt in the [[Ukrainian Baroque]] style.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=30}} In 1699, a new domed [[Bell Tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral|bell tower]] was built—the dome was gilded at Mazepa's expense.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=30}} The other new buildings in the complex were completed by 1767.<ref name="Sop1" /><ref name="Pal" /> The remains of the west gallery, the arcade, and part of the choirs were dismantled. By 1695, an outer gallery had been added, along with six new domes. The fire of 1697 led to destruction in the recently restored west end, and new work had to be undertaken to repair the damage caused.<ref name="Zha" /> 

The next major restoration to the cathedral took place between 1843 and 1853.<ref name="Zha" /> Between 1882 and 1889, a number of the Baroque pediments were removed, and a [[narthex]] was built on the site of the western gallery.<ref name="Zha" />

===20th century{{ndash}}present===
[[File:Середмістя Києва 1918.jpg|thumb|A 1918 photograph of Kyiv centred on St Sophia's Cathedral]]
In 1917, the {{Ill|Central Committee for the Protection of Monuments of Antiquity and Art in Ukraine|uk|Центральний комітет охорони пам'яток старовини і мистецтва в Україні}} began research into the cathedral.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Kharkovenko |first1=Roman |title=History of the development of registration documentation for objects of cultural heritage in Ukraine |journal=International Journal of Legal Studies |date=2017 |volume=2 |issue=2 |pages=329{{ndash}}344 |url=https://instytutinnowacji.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/IJOLS-%E2%84%96-2_2017-.pdf |location=Warsaw, Poland |issn=2543-7097}}</ref><ref name="Ang1">{{cite web |title=История собора в xx веке |url=https://angloman.com.ua/sofiiskii_sobor/%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B0-%D0%B2-xx-%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BA%D0%B5/ |publisher=Angloman |access-date=21 March 2026 |language=ru |date=3 August 2023 |trans-title=History of the cathedral in the 20th century}}</ref>
During the [[Soviet]] anti-religious campaign of the 1920s, the government called for the cathedral to be demolished and the grounds to be transformed into a park named in honour of the heroes of [[Siege of Perekop (1920)|Perekop]]. The cathedral was saved from demolition. St. Sophia was the cathedral of the [[Ukrainian Autocephalous Orthodox Church]] from 1921 until 1930.<ref name="Zha" /> In October 1921, [[Vasyl Lypkivsky]] was consecrated as the Metropolitan of Kyiv and All Ukraine of the Ukrainian Apostolic Church.<ref name="Int1">{{cite web |last1=|first1=|title=Lypkivsky, Vasyl |url=https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CL%5CY%5CLypkivskyVasyl.htm |website=Internet Encyclopaedia of Ukraine |publisher=Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies |access-date=21 March 2026}}</ref> In 1921, the [[National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine|All-Ukrainian Academy of Sciences]] created the {{Ill|Archaeological Commission of the VUAN|lt=Sophia Commission|uk|Археологічна комісія ВУАН}}, which was able to save the cathedral from being demolished.{{sfn|Bon|2020}}{{sfn|Prelovska|2017}}

The [[National Reserve "Sophia of Kyiv"]], a state architectural and historical reserve containing the cathedral and other historic buildings, was established in 1934. This enabled research and restoration work to be carried out on the cathedral and other buildings in the complex.<ref name="Une1">{{cite web |title=UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Kyiv: Saint-Sophia Cathedral and Related Monastic Buildings, Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/527/ |website=UNESCO World Heritage Conveetion |access-date=21 March 2026}}</ref> The last acting vicar of St Sophia, {{Ill|Yuri Mikhnovsky|uk|Міхновський Юрій Михайлович}}, was the incumbent from 1931 until his expulsion in 1934, after which the authorities banned services in the cathedral. Gold and silver items (including the royal gates, icons, candlesticks, vestments, and books) were confiscated, and the iconostases were dismantled and stripped of their gold.<ref name="Das">{{cite web |last1=Daschko|first1=Walter  |title=Saint Sophia Cathedral
|url=https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CS%5CA%5CSaintSophiaCathedral.htm |website=Internet Encyclopaedia of Ukraine |publisher=Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies |access-date=22 March 2026}}</ref><ref name="Int3">{{cite web |last1=|first1=|title=Mikhnovsky, Yurii
|url=https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CM%5CI%5CMikhnovskyYurii.htm|website=Internet Encyclopaedia of Ukraine |publisher=Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies |access-date=22 March 2026}}</ref> The reserve opened to visitors in the spring of 1935.<ref name="Pal" />

During the Second World War, Kyiv was occupied by the German [[Wehrmacht]] from 19 September 1941 until its liberation on 6 November 1943.<ref name="Dan">{{cite web |last1=Danilenko |first1=Vladimir |title=German Occupation of Kiev in 1941-1943: Documents of the Nazi-Controlled City Administration |url=https://www.bsb-muenchen.de/mikro/lit487.pdf |publisher=[[Bavarian State Library]] |access-date=24 March 2026 |page=2}}</ref> According to a previous director of the Sophia Museum, {{Ill|Alexei Povstenko|uk|Повстенко Олекса Іванович}}, German [[sapper]]s prevented the [[NKVD]] from blowing up the cathedral, who had [[Land mine|mined]] the building in September 1941. [[Church service]]s were resumed in October 1941.{{sfn|Zabuzhko|2011|p=19}}<ref name="Cho">{{cite web |last1=Chornyi |first1=Maxim |title=Alfred Rosenberg goes Kyiv: June 1942 |url=https://war-documentary.info/alfred-rosenberg-goes-kyiv-june-1942/ |publisher=War-Documentary |access-date=22 March 2026}}</ref> In January 1942 the occupation authorities established a short-lived museum (it closed in October 1943 as the Germans were about to retreat from Kyiv). The [[Reichsleiter Rosenberg Taskforce]] listed the cathedral's remaining valuables. The Nazis expropriated some of the remaining historical pieces, and only a small proportion were returned after the end of the war.<ref name="Cho" />

Politicians have promised during the 1980s onwards to return St Sophia to the Orthodox Church, but a final decision has been postponed. Both the [[Eastern Orthodox Church]] and the Greek-Catholic Church lay sole claim using the cathedral for worship. Catholics worshippers have not been permitted to use the cathedral; the Orthodox churches have sometimes been allowed to conduct services at different dates, and other times they have been denied access.{{cn|date=March 2026}} In 1995, riot police attacked mourners during the funeral of Patriarch [[Volodymyr Romaniuk]] of the [[Ukrainian Orthodox Church – Kyiv Patriarchate]]. The patriarch was buried in a makeshift grave after the situation was brought back under control.<ref name="Reu1">{{cite news |title=Ukraine: Riot police beat mourners at burial of the Patriarch of the Ukrainian Orthodox Church |url=https://reuters.screenocean.com/record/261903 |access-date=23 June 2023 |agency=Reuters Archive Licensing}}</ref><ref name="Pol">{{cite news |last1=Poletz |first1=Lida |title=Police beat mourners at patriarch's burial |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/police-beat-mourners-at-patriarch-s-burial-1592273.html |access-date=23 June 2023 |work=[[The Independent]] |date=19 July 1995}}</ref> Representatives of five Orthodox churches read the first joint prayer for Ukraine in the cathedral in 2006.<ref name="Per1">{{cite web |title=День Незалежності України та перша спільна молитва в Софійському соборі: усе про 24 серпня |url=https://pereiaslav.city/articles/377134/den-nezalezhnosti-ukraini-ta-persha-spilna-molitva-v-sofijskomu-sobori-use-pro-24-serpnya- |publisher=Pereyaslav.City |access-date=21 March 2026 |language=uk |trans-title=Independence Day of Ukraine and the first joint prayer in St. Sophia Cathedral: all about August 24}}</ref>

On June 10, 2025, as a result of a [[blast wave]] caused by shelling of Kyiv by the [[Russian Federation]] during the [[Russo-Ukrainian War]], part of the central apse on the eastern facade was damaged.<ref name="BBC1">{{cite news |title=Russia damaged St. Sophia Cathedral, the most valuable historical monument in Kyiv |url=https://www.bbc.com/ukrainian/articles/cdr5734x4x8o |access-date=21 March 2026 |work=BBC News Ukraine |date=10 June 2025}}</ref>

====Later restoration work====
During the German occupation of Kyiv, the state of the buildings in the reserve was documented, and [[renovation]] work was begun.<ref name="Cho" />

During the 21st century, the reserve has been involved in research with the aim of preserving the buildings. The movement of the buildings, as well as the levels of nearby groundwater, the nature of the foundations and soil compaction zones, the condition of the wall paintings, the strength of stress forces acting on the cathedral and the bell tower, and the cathedral's microclimate, have been investigated, mapped, and monitored.<ref name="Min1">{{cite web |title=Київ: Собор Cвятої Софії і прилеглі монастирські споруди, Києво-Печерська Лавра - План управління |url=http://kyiv-heritage.com/sites/default/files/%D0%A1%D0%BE%D1%84%D1%96%D1%8F%20%D1%96%20%D0%9B%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%80%D0%B0%20-%20%D0%9F%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BD%20%D0%A3%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%96%D0%BD%D0%BD%D1%8F%202014%2045%D1%81.pdf |publisher=Ministry of Culture of Ukraine |access-date=27 March 2026 |language=uk |date=2014 |trans-title=Kyiv: St. Sophia Cathedral and adjacent monastery buildings, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra - Management Plan}}</ref> In 2023, an [[invitation to tender]] was initiated for a 5-year project to restore the roof, domes, and other structural elements of St. Sophia.<ref name="Gaz2">{{cite news |title=St. Sophia of Kyiv commented on the scandal with the gilding of the cathedral's domes |url=https://gazeta.ua/articles/kiev-life/_v-sofiyi-kiyivskij-prokomentuvali-skandal-z-pozolochennyam-kupoliv-soboru/1162947 |access-date=26 March 2026 |work=Gazeta.ua |date=1 November 2023}}</ref>

====International recognition====
In 1990 the ensemble of the St. Sophia Monastery was included in the [[World Heritage Site|UNESCO World Heritage List]].<ref name="Une3">{{cite web |title=Kyiv: Saint-Sophia Cathedral and Related Monastic Buildings, Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra |url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/527/ |publisher=[[UNESCO]] World Heritage Center |access-date=24 March 2026}}</ref> In 2007, the St Sophia's Cathedral was named one of the [[Seven Wonders of Ukraine]].<ref name="Pal" /> In September 2023, Kyiv was listed as a World Heritage "in danger" because of Russia's war in Ukraine. This move by the [[United Nations]] was an effort to produce aid and protection for the site.<ref name="Une2">{{cite web |title=Ukraine: UNESCO sites of Kyiv and L’viv are inscribed on the List of World Heritage in Danger |url=https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ukraine-unesco-sites-kyiv-and-lviv-are-inscribed-list-world-heritage-danger |publisher=[[UNESCO]] |access-date=21 March 2026}}</ref>

==Architecture==
===The original cathedral===
[[File:Evan Freeman - Plan of St Sophia, Kyiv.jpg|thumb|Ground floor plan of the original 11th-century cathedral]]
St Sophia is one of the largest churches built in Europe during the 11th century, and is one of the oldest and best-preserved examples of [[Architecture in Kievan Rus'|Kyivan Rus’ architecture]]. As the architects were given a task not previously encountered—to build a huge church with spacious choirs, they deviated from the standard model for a Byzantine church. To reduce costs, they used local materials, incorporating brick cross-shaped pillar into the design instead of [[marble]] columns, and slabs of pink [[Ovruch]] [[pyrophyllite]] slate and ceramic tiles instead of marble slabs. The construction materials used were brick, stone, and [[lime mortar]].<ref name="Zha" /> Russian scholars have in the past attributed differences between St Sophia and other [[Byzantine architecture|Byzantine churches]] to the influences of [[Russian church architecture]].{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=39}} The plans of the foundations of St. Sophia  and an earlier church, the [[Church of the Tithes]] are similar.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|p=41}}

The cathedral's main dimensions are multiples of the number 12—a  standard area of {{convert|12|x|12|feet}} is used in the majority of the spaces. The inner radius of the central apse is equal to 12 feet, and the diameters of the drums of the small domes are also close to this value. If the thickness of the walls is taken into account, the length, width and height of the cathedral are all equal to {{convert|100|feet}}, a measurement used since [[Classical antiquity]].{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=101}}

====Exterior====
[[File:Софійський собор 43.jpg|thumb|A model reconstruction of the original appearance of St Sophia's Cathedral]] 
The 11th-century cathedral was a large five-[[nave]] church, with the eastern ends of the naves completed by semicircular [[apse]]s (semi-circular recesses at the east end of the main building).<ref name="Zha" /> 

The core of the cathedral (without the galleries) was a square of sides {{convert|100|feet}} in length, the same size of the domed square of the [[Hagia Sophia]].<ref name="Zha" /> The original building was topped with 13 [[dome]]s covered in [[lead]] sheets, with 12 smaller domes grouped around the largest dome, forming a stepped pyramidal shape. The central part of the cathedral was surrounded on the north, west and south sides by a two-tiered inner row of galleries, and a single-tiered outer row that was topped by a balcony, or {{lang|uk|gulbyshche}}.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=87}}

The outside of the cathedral was not originally [[plaster]]ed. The facades were decorated with recessed niches, windows had carved wooden frames. The walls were laid out of large natural stones of granite and pink quartzite that alternated with rows of flat bricks. A pink lime-cement mortar was used, and pieces differently-sized stones were included in the thick layer of the mortar.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=88}}

====Interior====
The interior of the cathedral has been preserved almost unchanged since the 11th century.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=92}} The rectangular space inside the original cathedral was divided by 12 columns into five parallel naves intersected by transverse naves, to produce what Nikitenko has described as a "hierarchically ordered cosmos". The intersection of the central nave and the [[transept]], above which the main dome rises, forms an [[equilateral]] cross.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=93}} There were five [[altar]]s occupying the aspses at the east end.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=87}} The central cross is closed from the east by the wall of the main altar, and from the north, west and south by two-tier three-span arcades (the western one has not been preserved).{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=94}}

At the corners of the western side, two stair towers were built, to lead to the [[Choir (architecture)|choir]]s.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=87}} The ancient mosaic floor, of which only a few fragments are exposed, was composed of designs that reflected Byzantine architectural forms. Under the main dome, a large square with a circle (representing the union of earth and heaven) was laid out.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|p=94}}

==Interior decoration==
St Sophia is unique in that on the interior walls are preserved the world's greatest collection of early 11th-century [[mosaic]]s and [[fresco]]es. Masterpieces include the Pantocrator, the Virgin Orans, the Communion of the Apostles, the Deisis, and the Annunciation.<ref name="Une1" /> The {{Ill|Mosaics of St. Sophia Cathedral|lt=mosaics of St Sophia|uk|Мозаїки Софійського собору}} cover a total of {{convert|260|m2}} of the walls; there are {{convert|3000|m2}} of frescos.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2022|p=12}} Mosaics highlight the main parts of the interior such as the central domed altar—the walls of the five original naves, the upper floor choirs, the two towers and the lost galleries that surrounded the cathedral, were decorated with frescoes.{{sfn|Totska|1975}} The mosaics and frescoes had been [[whitewash]]ed by 1701.  In 1884, the Russian art historian and archaeologist [[Adrian Prakhov]] rediscovered and studied the mosaics, which initiated the process of their restoration by future conservators.<ref name="Zha" />

===Mosaics===
[[File:Інтер'єр Софія (20210903).jpg|thumb|The interior of the cathedral. The central mosaic is the [[Orans of Kyiv|Virgin Orans]].]]
Although the names of the individual workers are not known, the artistic features of individual images and the methods of laying the [[smalt]] shows that a team of eight craftsmen worked on the wall mosaics in the cathedral. Each of the mosaics has an explanatory inscription in [[Medieval Greek]].<ref name="Nat3">{{cite web |title=Mosaics |url=http://sofiyskiy-sobor.polnaya.info/en/mosaics_st_sophia_cathedral.shtml |website=St Sophia Cathedral |publisher=National Historical Reserve "St. Sophia Cathedral in Kiev" |access-date=30 March 2026}}</ref>

The mosaics were made with a shining golden background. The predominant foreground colours were blue and grey-white in combination with purple. The large number of colours used is one indication that the glassmakers and smalt manufacturers of the period were highly skilled—the colours used have not been able to be replicated by modern experts. <ref name="Nat3" />

The vault of the central apse—and indeed the entire cathedral's interior—is dominated by the {{convert|6|m|ft|adj=mid|-high}} [[Orans of Kyiv|Virgin Orans]].<ref name="Nat3" />{{sfn|Totska|1975}}{{sfn|Noble|1996|p=957}}

The mosaics of the central dome and sanctuary represent people and scenes from the [[Scriptures]]. A [[Medallion (architecture)|medallion]] in the summit of the central dome bears a huge half-length image of [[Christ Pantocrator]]. Arranged around him are four [[archangels]], one of which remains intact; the other three were painted over using [[oil painting|oils]] by the Russian artist [[Mikhail Vrubel]] in 1884. Of the twelve mosaic portraits of the [[Apostles]] painted on the inside of the dome's [[Cylinder|drum]], only the upper part of the figure of [[Saint Paul]] has survived. Nearby were the [[Four Evangelists]], of whom [[Saint Mark]] alone is extant. Of the mosaic medallions on the wall arches, 15  have survived. The two piers of the eastern wall arch feature the [[Annunciation]], and the lower tier of the apse features the surviving upper parts of figures depicting the [[Church Fathers]]. The multi-figure depiction of the [[Last Supper]] spreads over a large area in the intermediate tier of the apse.{{sfn|Totska|1975}}

===Frescoes===
During restoration to the cathedral in the decade following 1843,1853, the cathedral's 11th-century frescoes were rediscovered by the Russian art historian [[Fedor Solntsev]]. After having been given official approval to proceed, they were covered over with a oil paintings.<ref name="Zha" /> The walls of the five aisles, the choir, and the two towers are covered with frescos. They were painted in a variety of colours—mainly dark red, yellow, [[Olive (color)|olive]], and white tones, painted with a blue background. Many were reserved below a layer of plaster before being revealed during the 20th century. They were created when the cathedral was built in the 11th century, with the intention of  both promoting the recently-adopted Christian [[doctrine]] and reinforcing the ideas of [[feudal]] power.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|pp=250{{ndash}}263}}

During the 1950s, more frescoes were revealed, and others dating the 18th century frescoes were repaired.<ref name="Got1">{{cite web |title=Digital documentation of inscriptions in the Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv |url=https://www.gu.se/en/research/digital-documentation-of-inscriptions-in-the-saint-sophia-cathedral-in-kyiv |publisher=[[University of Gothenburg]] |access-date=27 March 2026}}</ref>
{{multiple image | perrow=2 |direction = horizontal | total_width= 450| header = | footer = 
| image1 = The Descent of Christ into Hell - Google Art Project.jpg | alt1 = aaa | caption1 =[[Harrowing of Hell|Christ's descent into Hell]]
| image2 =The Archangel Michael - Google Art Project (LwE83yRr6 HWQQ).jpg | alt2 =bbb | caption2 = The [[Archangel Michael]]
| image3 =The Palace of Hippodrome - Google Art Project.jpg | alt3 = aaa | caption3 =The Palace of Hippodrome
| image4 = Skomoroks.jpg | alt4 =bbb | caption4 = A group of entertainers with musical instruments}}

The frescoes in the transept depict scenes relating to Christ. Four of the best preserved are of the [[Crucifixion of Jesus]], the [[Harrowing of Hell|Christ's descent into Hell]], the appearance of Christ to the Holy Women, and the Descent of the [[Holy Spirit]]. The choir features subjects as the [[Wedding at Cana]], the [[Feeding the multitude|Miracle of the Loaves and Fishes]], The [[Lord's Supper]], [[Abraham]] and the Three Angels, and the [[Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego|three youths in the fiery furnacе]].{{sfn|Totska|1975}}

The largest group of images were a unique series of [[saint]]s. Over 250 are extant; the original number was more than three times as many.<ref name="Sop1" /> The painting of the southern altar is dedicated to the [[Archangel Michael]], the [[patron saint]] of Kyiv and the Grand Princes of Kyivan Rus', whilst he frescoes of the northernmost altar depict [[Saint George]], the spiritual patron of Yaroslav the Wise.<ref name="Nat4">{{cite web |title=Frescoes |url=http://sofiyskiy-sobor.polnaya.info/en/frescos_st_sophia_cathedral.shtml |website=St Sophia Cathedral |publisher=National Historical Reserve "St. Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv" |access-date=31 March 2026}}</ref> All of [[the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste]] are depicted in a separate composition.{{sfn|Powstenko|1954|pp=112{{ndash}}113}}

The frescoes in the towers that led to the original outer galleries are of secular subjects, as they were intended to be seen by the Grand Prince and a few of the clergy. These frescoes depict scenes of the [[Byzantine bureaucracy and aristocracy|Byzantine court]] and the [[Hippodrome of Constantinople]], such as the emperor watching races in his [[Box (theatre)|box]], hunting scenarios, and scenes with warriors.<!-- <ref name="Sop1" /> --><ref name="Sop1">{{cite web |title=ЮНЕСКО |url=https://sofiia-kyivska.ua/unesco/ |publisher=Sophia Kyivska |access-date=21 March 2026 |language=uk |trans-title=UNESCO}}</ref>

On three walls of the central nave, is the lhe largest fresco in the cathedral, at {{convert|15|m}} long. It purportedly shows Yaroslav the Wise and his family during the [[consecration]] of St Sophia.<ref name="Kos">{{cite news |last1=Kostiuk |first1=Bohdana |title=Україна-Русь ХІ століття. Нове дослідження фресок Софійського собору дало несподівані результати |url=https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30250336.html |access-date=30 March 2026 |work=[[Radio Liberty]] |date=3 November 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191103061505/https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30250336.html |archive-date=3 November 2019 |location=Kyiv, Ukraine |language=uk |trans-title=Ukraine-Rus of the 11th century. New study of the frescoes of St. Sophia Cathedral yielded unexpected results}}</ref> Yaroslav was portrayed holding a model of the cathedral, along with his wife [[Ingegerd Olofsdotter|Irene]], his daughters, who later became queens of France, Norway and Hungary, and his sons, who became important political figures in the Kyivan Rus' state in the latter half of the 11th century. Only part of the composition has survived.{{sfn|Totska|1975}}

According to Nikitenko, the portraits are of the family of Prince Vladimir Svyatoslavich.{{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|pp=250{{ndash}}263}} The frescoes constitute a grand princely triumphal cycle, that illustrates the conclusion of the marriage of Vladimir and the Byzantine princess Anna in  988, and which marked the [[Christianization of Kievan Rus']] {{sfn|Nikitenko|2008|pp=263{{ndash}}307}}

Other secular frescoes show hunters, [[acrobat]]s, musicians, dancers, wrestlers, and horsemen.{{sfn|Orlenko|Ivashko|Ding|2022|p=62}} Pictures of animals, birds, and fantastic creatures frequently appear in the frescoes. Of interest are the figures of buffoons with musical instruments that include a musician with a bowed instrument,  musicians playing on [[Psaltery|psalteries]] and [[lute]]s, and [[flutist]]s. A picture of a [[pipe organ]], the only known example in Kyivan Rus' art, is depicted in the southern tower.{{sfn|Totska|1975}}

===Graffiti===
[[File:Graffiti St. Sophia's Cathedral in Kiev (Graffiti № 1330).jpg|thumb|Graffiti#1330 ("help [your] slave Stepan's sinful servant"), one of over 7,000 [[inscriptions]] and drawings in the cathedral.{{sfn|Kornienko|2010|p=272}}{{refn|1=The form of the letter "в" in the text has been used to date it to the 2nd quarter of the 11th century – 1st quarter of the 13th century.{{sfn|Kornienko|2010|p=272}}|group=note}}]]
The {{Ill|lt=graffiti inscribed on the walls of St Sophia|Graffiti of St Sophia, Kyiv|uk|Графіті Софії Київської}} was left by priests and visitors to the cathedral during its existence. More than 7,000 11th{{ndash}}18th century examples of drawings and texts have been discovered, a number indicative of a high level of literacy in Ukrainian lands during this period.<ref name="Zva">{{cite news |last1=Zvarych |first1=Olena |title=«Тут була Анна» |url=http://www.umoloda.kiev.ua/number/1950/203/69356/ |access-date=29 March 2026 |work=[[Ukraina Moloda]] |issue=169-170 |date=23 November 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131215062716/http://www.umoloda.kiev.ua/number/1950/203/69356/ |archive-date=15 December 2013 |language=uk |trans-title="Anna was here"}}</ref>

Some of the inscriptions are graffiti are written in [[Glagolitic script]].<ref name="Kag1">{{cite web |title=Glyphs of Graffiti of St. Sophia Cathedral of Kyiv |url=https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/yoctoman/graffiti-st-sophia-cathedral-kyiv |website=[[Kaggle]] |access-date=29 March 2026}}</ref> The texts contain valuable information about historic events in Kievan Rus'; examples include a record of the death of Yaroslav the Wise, the autograph of Grand Prince [[Volodymyr Monomakh]], a record of a [[peace treaty]] concluded at the end of the 11th century between princes [[Sviatopolk II of Kiev|Svyatopolk Izyaslavich]], Volodymyr Monomakh and [[Oleg I of Chernigov]], and a mention of the 12th century purchase of land by [[Maria Mstislavna of Kiev|Maria Mstislavna]], the wife of [[Vsevolod II of Kyiv]]. A series of dates was discovered on the walls that make up an [[eschatological]] calendar and testify to expectations in the first third of the 11th century of the [[Second Coming]] of [[Christ]]. In different places of the cathedral, dates on the graffiti were found that support the [[hypothesis]]  that the cathedral was built from the 1010s onwards.{{sfn|Nikitenko|Kornienko|2012|p=232}}

The majority of the inscriptions were written by Kyiv's citizens, and constitute a primary source of information about the [[Old East Slavic|Old Russian]] language, which was the vernacular language of the inhabitants of Kyivan Rus' (as opposed to [[Church Slavonic]], which was used for literacy and [[liturgical]] purposes). Analysis of the inscriptions shows that they share features with [[modern Ukrainian]].<ref name="Red">{{cite web |last1=Redchenko |first1=Oleksiy |title=Українська граматика на стінах софії київської |url=http://zustrich.quebec-ukraine.com/lib/st_sophie_graffiti.html |publisher=PIC Ukraine |access-date=29 March 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130602053204/http://zustrich.quebec-ukraine.com/lib/st_sophie_graffiti.html |archive-date=2 June 2013 |language=uk |date=2008 |trans-title=Ukrainian grammar on the walls of St. Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv}}</ref>

===Fittings and monuments===
In the 12th century, a [[baptismal font]] was built into the western outer gallery. Between 1747{{ndash}}1754 a three-tiered iconostasis was built, the lower tier of which has survived.<ref name="Zha" />

==Cathedral precinct==
[[File:"Sophia Kyivska" Reserve (Estate of St. Sophia Cathedral).svg|thumb|Map of the St Sophia Cathedral precinct and the surrounding streets]]
St Sophia's Cathedral is surrounded by a Ukrainian Baroque ensemble of 17th- and 18th-century monastic buildings.<ref name="Une1" /> The cathedral and the monastic buildings within the cathedral precinct are a secular museum, with most of its visitors being tourists.<ref name="Gui1">{{cite web |title=National Conservation Area “St. Sophia of Kyiv” |url=https://guide.kyivcity.gov.ua/en/organization/natsionalnyy-zapovidnyk-sofiya-kyyivska |website=Virtual tourism |publisher=Positivus |access-date=4 April 2026}}</ref> Together, they form one part of a national conservational area, the National Reserve "Sophia of Kyiv", that includes three other sites in the city—[[Golden Gate, Kyiv|Golden Gate]], [[St. Cyril's Church]], and [[St Andrew's Church, Kyiv|St Andrew's Church]]—as well as the [[Genoese fortress, Sudak|Genoese fortress]] in [[Crimea]].<ref name="Rus1" />

Reconstruction works undertaken at St Sophia were awarded the “European Gold Medal for the Protection of Historic Monuments” in 1987. The cathedral and its associated buildings are used both as a museum for educational purposes, and for state events.<ref name="Une1" /> In 1990, they were included in the [[UNESCO World Heritage List]].<ref name="NatR1">{{cite web |title=Історія Національного заповідника "Софія Київська" |url=http://nzsk.org.ua/ukr/history/default.aspx |website=National Reserve "Sophia Kyivska" |access-date=4 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120205040516/http://nzsk.org.ua/ukr/history/default.aspx |archive-date=5 February 2012 |language=uk |trans-title=History of the National Reserve "Sophia Kyivska"}}</ref>

The reserve has over 80 thousand items, archaeological finds, paintings, ancient church items and books, archives, architectural drawings, and a valuable collection of 13th-century gold and silver embroided liturgical robes.<ref name="Nik2">{{cite web |last1=Nikitenko |first1=Nadiya Nikolaevna |title=Софія київська, Національний Заповідник |url=http://resource.history.org.ua/cgi-bin/eiu/history.exe?Z21ID=&I21DBN=EIu&P21DBN=&S21STN=1&S21REF=10&S21FMT=eiu_all&C21COM=S&S21CNR=20&S21P01=0&S21P02=0&S21P03=TRN=&S21COLORTERMS=0&S21STR=Sofiia_Kyivska |publisher=Institute of History of Ukraine, NAS of Ukraine |access-date=7 April 2026 |language=uk |date=2016 |trans-title=Sofia Kyivska, National Reserve}}</ref>

===Bell tower===
{{main|Bell Tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral}}
The lowest of the four tiers of the Bell Tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral is dominated by the gate, above which there are [[Loophole (firearm)|loopholes]]. The second tier has an open gallery. The third tier has round windows, and the fourth tier has openings for the bell. The construction has a Baroque dome. The unconventional placement of the bell tower to the east of the cathedral is due to a conscious desire to create an urban ensemble.<ref name="Chu1">{{cite web |title=Дзвіниця софійського собору |url=http://churches.kiev.ua/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=34:2011-03-23-09-39-24&catid=4:2011-03-20-20-31-28&Itemid=12 |website=The Churches of Kyiv |access-date=1 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402102959/http://churches.kiev.ua/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=34:2011-03-23-09-39-24&catid=4:2011-03-20-20-31-28&Itemid=12 |archive-date=2 April 2015 |language=uk |date=23 March 2011 |trans-title=The bell tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral}}</ref>

====History====
{{multiple image |align=left| direction = horizontal | total_width= 440| header = | footer = | image1 = Софійський собор у 1651 році. Малюнок Абрагама ван Вестерфельда.jpg | alt1 = aaa | caption1 =The original wooden bell tower, as drawn by [[Abraham van Westerveld]] in 1651 | image2 = M. Sazhin - St Sophia Cathedral (1840s).jpg| alt2 =bbb | caption2 = [[Mikhail Sazhin (painter)|Sazhin]]'s depiction of the cathedral painted in the 1840s, with the brick bell tower in the foreground }}
In a 1651 drawing of the area in the vicinity of the cathedral by van Westerfeld, a wooden four-tiered [[bell tower]] is depicted. The drawing shows that the tower once stood near the fence to the north of the cathedral. Its first tier had a passage with a semicircular arch; the second tier had an open gallery, and a parapet of carved [[baluster]]s.<ref name="NZha">{{cite web |last1=Zharkikh |first1=N.I. |title=The architectural ensemble of St. Sophia Cathedral in Kiev Disposition of 17 cent. |url=https://www.m-zharkikh.name/uk/Heritage/StSophiaEnsemble/17Cent.html#google_vignette |publisher=N.I. Zharkikh |access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref> The bell tower was built under Peter Mohyla by an unknown architect. When the tower was destroyed in a fire in 1697, it was replaced two years later with a brick tower built to the southeast of the cathedral at the expense of Hetman Mazepa, the first stone structure to be built in the complex after the fire. The 13-ton "Mazepa" bell, cast in 1705 by {{Ill|Afanasy Petrovich|uk|Афанасій Петрович}}, still hangs on the second level. In 1706, the three-tiered brick structure was completed. Soon afterwards, an earthquake damaged the upper tiers. <ref name="Chu1" /><ref name="Nat5">{{cite web |title=Bell Tower of Saint Sophia Cathedral |url=http://sofiyskiy-sobor.polnaya.info/kolokolnya_sofiyskogo_sobora.shtml |website=Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kiev Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv |publisher=National Historical and Cultural Preserve "Sophia of Kyiv" |access-date=1 April 2026}}</ref>

When cracks appeared in the walls of the building in 1744, the two uppermost tiers were rebuilt by [[Gottfried Johann Schädel]]. Ornamentation was incorporated into the walls by the {{Ill|Stepan Stobensky|uk|Стобенський Степан}} and his son {{Ill|lt=Ivan|Ivan Stobensky|uk|Стобенський Іван}}. The bell tower was completed by a Baroque bathhouse with a spire, covered with gilded stars on a blue background. The work was completed in 1748.<ref name="Chu1" />

By the early 19th century, the bell tower had become delapidated.<ref name="Nat5" /> In 1807, lightning struck the top of the tower, and the spire was then replaced. The new spire appeared to make the tower seen flatter, so a fourth tier and a high pear-shaped dome were added by the [[diocesa]]n architect {{Ill|Pavlo Sparro|uk|Спарро Павло Іванович}}. After the addition, the height of the building—an early example of the Ukrainian Baroque style—was {{convert|76|m}}.<ref name="Chu1" />

The bell tower underwent restoration in 1953 and 1972.<ref name="Chu1" /> On 27 June 2003, after a restoration period of almost four years, the bell tower opened to visitors for the first time in 50 years.<ref name="Seg1">{{cite news |title=Впервые за 50 лет софийская колокольня открыта для посетителей |url=http://ukr.segodnya.ua/oldarchive/c2256713004f33f5c2256d55003895eb.html |access-date=1 April 2026 |work=Segodynya |date=1 July 2003 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402153127/http://ukr.segodnya.ua/oldarchive/c2256713004f33f5c2256d55003895eb.html |archive-date=2 April 2015 |language=ru |trans-title=For the first time in 50 years, the Sofia Bell Tower is open to visitors.}}</ref>

===Metropolitan’s House===
[[File:Київ, Будинок митрополита.jpg|thumb|The {{Ill|Metropolitan's House|uk|Будинок митрополита}}]]
Due to its rich architectural forms and the quality of the decor, the {{Ill|Metropolitan's House|uk|Будинок митрополита}} is among the finest examples of 18th-century Ukrainian architecture.<ref name="Nat6">{{cite web |title=Metropolitan's Residence |url=http://sofiyskiy-sobor.polnaya.info/en/metropolitans_residence_st_sophia_cathedral.shtml |website=St Sophia Cathedral |publisher=National Historical Reserve "St. Sophia Cathedral in Kiev" |access-date=2 April 2026}}</ref>

A 1695 map of Kyiv shows that a wooden residence once existed in the southwestern part of the monastery courtyard. It was lost during the fire of 1697.<ref name="Myk">{{cite web |title=Metropolitan’s house, Sophia of Kyiv |url=https://www.fue.com.ua/en/2022/10/08/metropolitans-house-sophia-of-kyiv/ |website=Foundation Ukrainian Europe |access-date=2 April 2026}}</ref> A single-storied stone residence was built between 1722 and 1730,<ref name="Nat6" /> with a second floor being built at a later date. The construction was completed with an upper floor and pediments on the eastern and western facades.<ref name="Myk" /> In the 19th century, a double-storey annex and a balcony were added to the west wall. The western façade overlooked the metropolitan's courtyard which had private gates into the city; these became redundant during the 19th century when Kyiv's street plan was revised. The courtyard was then made into a garden.<ref name="Nat6" />

The Metropolitan's House is a rectangular building with two polygonal projections on the east [[facade]]. The first two floors were built at different times, and this reflected in their architectural styles. The ground floor is typical of late 17th-century Ukrainian architecture—there are side rooms off a large main entrance hall, and semicircular windows set in [[embrasure]]s within the thick walls. Both floors have decorative [[cornice]]s. Along the exterior walls there are vertical [[pilaster]]s. On the outside, the first floor windows are decorated with plaster-ornamented pediments, and plaster wreaths and ribbons.<ref name="Nat6" />

The medieval residence contained the library and the [[scriptorium]], and acted as the administrative centre for the metropolitan. After the [[episcopal see]] moved from Kyiv in 1300, it remained the metropolitan's official residence. After the First World War, various institutions and organizations were located there, including the {{Ill|Department of Confessions of the Ukrainian People’s Republic|uk|Міністерство сповідань}} (1918{{ndash}}1919), the Ukrainian Architectural Institute (1918{{ndash}}1924), and the Academy of Architecture (1944{{ndash}}1962).<ref name="Myk" /> The building was restored between 2006 and 2009.<ref name="Rin">{{cite web |title=Restoring the Metropolitan’s House at Sophia of Kyiv National Sanctuary |url=https://akhmetovfoundation.org/en/project/restavratsiya-budynku-mytropolyta-v-natsionalnomu-zapovidnyku-sofiya-kyivska |publisher=[[Rinat Akhmetov Foundation]] |access-date=3 April 2026}}</ref>

===Refectory===
[[File:Церква трапезна 01.JPG|thumb|{{Ill|Refectory of St Sophia Cathedral|uk|Трапезна церква Софійського монастиря}}
]]

A wooden refectory and church was first mentioned in 1682. Ushakov's plan of 1695 depicts a building with a brick lower floor, a wooden upper floor, and a barrel-shaped shingle roof. The building was destroyed in the fire of 1697. Between 1722 and 1730 the refectory and church was rebuilt, using the old cellars and the walls above them. The lower floor of the {{Ill|Refectory of St Sophia Cathedral|uk|Трапезна церква Софійського монастиря}} housed the refectory with the Church of the Resurrection of St. Lazarus and a kitchen; the upper floor housed store rooms. In around 1769, the refectory was redesigned, and a pediment was added onto the western facade.<ref name="Chu2">{{cite web |title=Трапезна софійського монастиря (тепла софія) |url=http://churches.kiev.ua/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=35:2011-03-23-09-39-58&catid=4:2011-03-20-20-31-28&Itemid=12 |website=Churches of Kyiv |access-date=3 April 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150403193808/http://churches.kiev.ua/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=35:2011-03-23-09-39-58&catid=4:2011-03-20-20-31-28&Itemid=12 |archive-date=3 April 2015 |date=23 March 2011 |trans-title=Refectory of the Sofia Monastery (Warm Sofia)}}</ref>

There was no longer any need for the refectory after the monastery was dissolved, and it was redesigned to be used instead of the cathedral during the coldest months. The church, which was called 'Warm Sophia"' was consecrated on 12 November 1822. In 1872, the church new aisles were added and the tower was dismantled. The building has been restored in several stages since the 1970s. The 18th-century kitchen, stair tower, and western vestibule have been rebuilt, and the building adapted to be used as a museum.<ref name="Chu2" />

===Zaborovsky Gate===
{{multiple image | direction = horizontal | total_width= 350 | header = | footer = The Zaborovsky Gate (''left'') in the early years of the 20th century; (''right'') in 2013 (restored)
| image1 = BramaZaborovskogo20.max-1920x1080 grey.jpg|alt1 = aaa | caption1 = 
| image2 = Брама Заборовського DSC 5378.jpg |  | alt2 =bbb | caption2=}}

The 18th-century  {{Ill|Zaborovsky Gate|uk|Брама Заборовського}} was built into the boundary wall of the monastery where the wall followed the modern St. Georgievsky Lane. The gate stands opposite the western facade of the Metropolitan House.<ref name="Loc1" />

The gate was the Metropolitan's main entrance to his residence. It was built in 1746{{ndash}}1748 by Schädel, and was commissioned and financed by Metropolitan [[Raphael Zaborovsky]].<ref name="Int2" /> The original side buildings built by Zaborovsky were demolished in 1822{{ndash}}1823.<ref name="Loc1" /> 

The gate was bricked up in the 1920s, and its façade was altered.<ref name="Int2" /> The former courtyard was redesigned as a garden that remained hidden from view.<ref name="Loc1">{{cite web |title=The Forbidden Guide to Kyiv, 1930, by Fyodor Ernst |url=https://localhistory.org.ua/texts/chitanka/zaboronenii-putivnik-po-kiievu-1930-roku-fedora-ernsta/ |publisher=Local History |access-date=6 April 2026 |date=8 November 2023}}</ref>
It was restored between 1946 and 1948.<ref name="Int2">{{cite web |last1=|first1=|title=Zaborovsky Gate
|url=https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CZ%5CA%5CZaborovskyGate.htm|website=Internet Encyclopaedia of Ukraine |publisher=Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies |access-date=3 April 2026}}</ref> Writing in the 1930s, the Ukrainian art historian [[Fedir Ernst]] described the architecture of the Zaborovsky Gate as being: "decorated below with a complex Baroque system of columns and pilasters, two arches and a magnificent pediment with wavy volute lines, the gate is particularly notable for its luxurious molding, which completely fills the entire field of its facade wall with acanthus designs, theatrical masks, oval cartouches with crowns and a flaming heart., rosettes, etc.."<ref name="Loc1" />

== In culture ==
In 1885 a folk Christmas song ([[koliadka]]) telling the story of the construction of the cathedral was recorded by the Ukrainian ethnographer [[Mykhailo Zubrytskyi]] in the village of [[Mshanets]] (now part of [[Sambir Raion]], [[Lviv Oblast]]). In 1889 the song's text was published by the Ukrainian poet [[Ivan Franko]] in ''[[Kievskaia starina]]'' magazine, and was later noticed by the Ukrainian historian [[Mykhailo Hrushevskyi]]. The song can be dated to the 17th century, as it contains a precise description of the cathedral's architecture before its later reconstruction.<ref name="Net">{{cite news |last1=Netudikhatkin |first1=Igor |title=Cофійська колядка: у пошуках мелодії Різдва XVII століття |url=https://tyzhden.ua/cofijska-koliadka-u-poshukakh-melodii-rizdva-xvii-stolittia/ |access-date=16 March 2026 |work=Tyzhden |date=1 January 2024 |language=uk |trans-title=Sofia's Carol: In Search of the 17th Century Christmas Melody}}</ref>

The cathedral has served as the backdrop to [[Bouquet Kyiv Stage]], the annual outdoor arts festival<ref name="Gal">{{cite news |last=Gallaher |first=Rachel |date=4 March 2025 |title=Showing Courage in Ukraine With Handfuls of Clay |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/03/04/arts/ukraine-statues-design.html |url-access=subscription |access-date=23 June 2025 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language= |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> started by the entrepreneur [[Evgeni Utkin]]. It has drawn Ukrainian artists and musical acts such as [[DakhaBrakha]],<ref name="Kab">{{cite web |last=Kabanov |first=Olexandr |date=4 June 2024 |title=Kyiv as seen by DakhaBrakha |url=https://www.ukrainer.net/en/kyiv-as-seen-by-dakhabrakha/ |access-date=23 June 2025 |website=Ukraїner}}</ref> and the concert pianist [[Antonii Baryshevskyi]].<ref name="Bla">{{cite web |last=Blackledge |first=Richard |date=28 September 2022 |title=Ukraine culture festival comes to Oxfordshire for the first time |url=https://www.oxfordshirelive.co.uk/whats-on/music-nightlife/bouquet-kyiv-stage-ukraine-culture-7635063 |access-date=23 June 2025 |website=Oxfordshire Live |language=}}</ref>

The '''Kyiv Pechersk Lavra'''<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/527/ |title=Kyiv: Saint-Sophia Cathedral and Related Monastic Buildings, Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra |last=Centre |first=UNESCO World Heritage |website=UNESCO World Heritage Centre |language=en |access-date=2019-07-26}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://gordonua.com/ukr/news/worldnews/-pravilne-napisannja-stolitsi-ukrajini-anglijskoju-movoju-zakripleno-v-dokumenti-junesko-mzs-ukrajini-1102775.html |title=Правильне написання столиці України англійською мовою закріплено в документі ЮНЕСКО – МЗС України |trans-title=The correct spelling of the capital of Ukraine in English is enshrined in a UNESCO document - MFA of Ukraine |date=2019-07-09 |website=gordonua.com |language=uk |access-date=2019-07-26}}</ref> or '''Kyievo-Pecherska Lavra''' ({{langx|uk|Києво-Печерська лавра}}), also known as the '''Kyiv Monastery of the Caves''', is a historic [[lavra]] or large [[monastery]] of [[Eastern Christianity]] that gave its name to the [[Pecherskyi District]] where it is located in [[Kyiv]].

Since its foundation as the [[cave monastery]] in 1051, the Lavra has been a preeminent center of Eastern Christianity in Eastern Europe.<ref>Magocsi P.R. A History of Ukraine. University of Toronto Press: Toronto, 1996. p 98.</ref>

==Etymology and other names==
{{langx|uk|печера|pechera}}  means {{lit.}} ''cave'', which in turn derived from [[Proto-Slavic]] ''*реktera'' with the same meaning. {{langx|uk|лавра|[[lavra]]|link=no}} is used to describe high-ranking male monasteries for monks of the [[Eastern Orthodox Church]]. Therefore, the name of the monastery is also translated as Kyiv Cave Monastery, Kyiv Caves Monastery or the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves (from ''{{langx|uk|на печерах|link=no}}'').{{cn|date=January 2023}}

== History ==
=== Foundation and early history ===
The ''[[Primary Chronicle]]'' contains contradictory information as to when the monastery was founded: in 1051, or in 1074.{{sfn|Cross|Sherbowitz-Wetzor|1953|p=7}} [[Anthony of Kiev|Anthony]], a [[monk]] on [[Mount Athos]], originally from [[Liubech]] of the [[Principality of Chernigov]], returned to [[Kievan Rus'|Rus']] and settled in Kyiv as a [[missionary]] of monastic tradition to Kyivan Rus'. He chose a cave at the [[Berestove|Berestov Mount]] that overlooked the [[Dnipro river]] and a community of disciples soon grew. [[Knyaz|Prince]] [[Iziaslav I of Kiev|Iziaslav I of Kyiv]] (1024–1078) ceded the whole mount to the Anthonite monks who founded a monastery built by architects from [[Constantinople]].{{cn|date=January 2023}}

In 1096 the monastery was plundered by the [[Cumans]]. Later it fell victim to the [[Siege of Kiev (1240)|Mongolian invaders]], and in 1416 was burned down by forces of [[Golden Horde]] ruler [[Edigu|Edigey]], being rebuilt only in 1470.<ref name=meta/>

At the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra were buried some high-importance personalities from the period when Kyiv was a part of the [[Grand Duchy of Lithuania]]: Prince of Kyiv [[Vladimir Olgerdovich]] and his son [[Aleksandras Olelka]], the Lithuanian and Ruthenian Grand Duke [[Švitrigaila]], [[Feodor Ostrogski]], [[Uliana Olshanska]] (a second wife of the Lithuanian Grand Duke [[Vytautas the Great]]), and the Lithuanian Grand Hetman [[Konstanty Ostrogski]], known for commanding the [[Grand Ducal Lithuanian Army]] in the victorious [[Battle of Orsha]] (1514) versus the [[Principality of Moscow|Grand Principality of Moscow]] Army.<ref name="Vkpk">{{cite web |title=Kijevo Pečorų lauros vienuolyno kompleksas |url=https://vkpk.lt/u-zemelapis/kijevo-pecoru-lauros-vienuolyno-kompleksas/ |website=Valstybinė kultūros paveldo komisija |access-date=25 January 2025 |language=lt}}</ref> [[List of mayors of Kyiv|Mayors of Kyiv]], members of the [[szlachta]] and [[Cossack starshyna]], as well as church hierarchs also found their burial place in the monastery.<ref name=meta/>

===Baroque era and Russian rule===
In the 17th century, under the leadership of [[archimandrite]]s [[Yelysei Pletenetskyi|Eliseus Pletenetsky]], [[Zacharias Kopystensky]] and [[Peter Mohyla]], the monastery stood at the heart of Ukrainian national identity. [[Kyiv Caves Patericon]], which was created by Lavra's monks and soon became a popular reading around the whole [[Eastern Europe]], contributed to the emergence of the symbolic image of Kyiv as a capital of [[Eastern Orthodoxy]]. Lavra's printing house, established by Pletenetskyi in the 1620s, started the process of Kyiv's cultural revival, and the monastery's school, founded by Mohyla, introduced European educational trends of the time, leading to a radical reform of education. During the [[Baroque]] era Kyiv Pechersk Lavra flourished as a centre of arts and spirituality, and pilgrimage to Kyiv was seen by some as more preferable than visiting [[Jerusalem]].<ref name=meta>{{Cite web|title=Метаморфози Києво-Печерської лаври|date=2024-08-05|url=https://tyzhden.ua/metamorfozy-kyievo-pecherskoi-lavry/|website=[[The Ukrainian Week|Український Тиждень]]|access-date=2025-08-14}}</ref> 

According to a legend published by [[polemicist]] and preacher [[Joannicius Galiatovsky]], in 1630 the monastery was besieged by a Polish army, but the [[Virgin Mary|Holy Mother of God]] protected its monks by turning a "fiery rain" against the invaders.<ref>{{Cite magazine |script-title=uk:Cофійська колядка: у пошуках мелодії Різдва XVII століття |trans-title=Sofia Carol: In Search of a 17th-Century Christmas Melody |first1=Ігор |last1=Нетудихаткін |date=2024-01-01|url=https://tyzhden.ua/cofijska-koliadka-u-poshukakh-melodii-rizdva-xvii-stolittia/ |language=uk |magazine=Український Тиждень |trans-magazine=[[The Ukrainian Week|Ukrainian Week]] |access-date=2025-10-22}}</ref>

Despite the patronage of powerful figures, including [[Ivan Mazepa]] and [[Raphael Zaborovsky]], the [[Annexation of the Metropolis of Kyiv by the Moscow Patriarchate]] in 1685 started a process of subjugation of the monastery to [[Russian Empire|Russian]] imperial authority. In 1722, by the decree of [[Peter I of Russia]], the Metropolis of Kyiv was lowered in status to an [[archbishop]]ric, which made it equal to other subdivisions of the Russian [[Most Holy Synod|Synodal Church]]. In the following years, Russian religious traditions, [[axiology]] and language were imposed on the [[History of Christianity in Ukraine|Orthodox Church in Ukraine]].<ref name=moly/>

Under Russian rule, Pechersk Lavra became a popular place of mass pilgrimage for both the common folk and figures of authority, including the royal family. During the late 19th century numerous guides for pilgrims visiting the monastery were published in Tsarist Russia, contributing to its inclusion into the empire's symbolic space. Among prominent figures buried in Lavra's walls under the Russian rule are [[Natalia Dolgorukova]], [[Pyotr Rumyantsev]] and [[Pyotr Stolypin]].<ref name=moly>{{Cite web|title=Українська молитва в лаврських печерах|date=2025-07-25|url=https://tyzhden.ua/ukrainska-molytva-v-lavrskykh-pecherakh/|website=[[The Ukrainian Week|Український Тиждень]]|access-date=2025-08-14}}</ref><ref name=meta/>

=== Modern history ===
During the [[Ukrainian War of Independence|Ukrainian Revolution]] of the early 20th century attempts to [[Ukrainization|Ukrainize]] the Lavra failed due to political instability.<ref name=moly/> On 25 January 1918 Metropolitan [[Vladimir Bogoyavlensky|Vladimir of Kiev]] was tortured and murdered in the monastery by [[Bolshevik]] troops. Eventually, the monastery was disbanded, and in 1926 a museum was opened on its territory.<ref name=meta/> Under [[Reichskommissariat Ukraine|German occupation]] religion services in the monastery were resumed.<ref name=moly/>
On 3 November 1941 the main [[Dormition Cathedral, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra|Dormition Cathedral]] was blown up by Soviet [[NKVD]];<ref name="ki-2026">{{cite web|title=Kyiv's historic Pechersk Lavra burns as large-scale Russian strikes kill 5, injure 29 in capital|url=https://kyivindependent.com/russian-attack-june-14-2026/|first1=Dmytro|last1=Basmat|first2=Abbey|last2=Fenbert|website=The Kyiv Independent|date=2026-06-15|access-date=2026-06-15}}</ref> Soviet press would falsely accuse the Germans of committing that act. The demolition of the cathedral's ruins continued into the 1960s. After a long period of reconstruction, on 24 August 2000 the reconstructed Dormition Cathedral was solemnly reopened.<ref name=meta/>

Starting from the end of the [[Second World War]], the monastery resumed its activities as part of the Russian Orthodox Church. Over 100 monks lived on Lavra's premises until its next closure by the authorities in 1961.<ref name=mon/>

In 1988 activities of Kyiv Pechersk Lavra were renewed as part of celebrations dedicated to the 1000th anniversary of the [[Christianization of Kievan Rus']]. During the early 1990s the monastery was headed by metropolitan [[Filaret Denysenko|Filaret of Kyiv]], whose residence was located on its premises. However, in 1992 ownership over the Lavra was transferred to the [[Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)]] with the support of [[Kyiv City State Administration|Kyiv's political leadership]].<ref name=moly/> Under the management of the Moscow Patriarchate Lavra became an epicentre of several scandals connected with its leadership's love for expensive cars and other attributes of wealth, as well as its monks' connections to Russian [[Federal Security Service|FSB]], veneration of Tsar [[Nicholas II of Russia|Nicholas II]] and spread of [[anti-Ukrainian sentiment|anti-Ukrainian]] propaganda.<ref name=meta/>

Together with the [[Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv|Saint Sophia Cathedral]], the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra has been inscribed as a [[UNESCO]] [[World Heritage Site]] since 1990.<ref name="Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, St. Sophia Cathedral remain on UNESCO's World Heritage List">https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/general/157947.html/ Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, St. Sophia Cathedral remain on UNESCO's World Heritage List], [[Interfax-Ukraine]] (20 June 2013)</ref>{{#tag:ref|Late 2010 a monitoring mission of [[UNESCO]] was visiting the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra to check on situation of the site. At the time the [[Ministry of Culture (Ukraine)|Minister of Culture]] [[Mykhailo Kulynyak]] stated the historic site along with the [[Saint Sophia's Cathedral, Kyiv|Saint Sophia Cathedral]] was not threatened by the "black list" of the organisation.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://mincult.kmu.gov.ua/mincult/uk/publish/article/238820 |title="Софії Київській та Києво-Печерській лаврі "чорний список" ЮНЕСКО не загрожує" – Міністр культури Михайло Кулиняк |trans-title="Sophia of Kyiv and Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra are not threatened by the UNESCO "black list" - Minister of Culture Mykhailo Kulinyak |access-date=23 June 2017 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150711080914/http://mincult.kmu.gov.ua/mincult/uk/publish/article/238820 |archive-date=11 July 2015 |df=dmy-all}}</ref> The [[World Heritage Committee]] of UNESCO decided in June 2013 that Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, and St. Sofia Cathedral and related monastery buildings would remain on the [[World Heritage List]].<ref name="Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, St. Sophia Cathedral remain on UNESCO's World Heritage List"/>|group=nb}} The monastery complex is considered a separate national historic-cultural preserve (sanctuary), the national status to which was granted on 13 March 1996.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://zakon1.rada.gov.ua/cgi-bin/laws/main.cgi?nreg=181/96&p=1260343170231885 |title=Про надання статусу національного Києво-Печерському держав... - від 13.03.1996 № 181/96 |trans-title=On granting the status of national Kyiv-Pechersk State... - dated 03.13.1996 No. 181/96 |website=zakon1.rada.gov.ua |access-date=23 June 2017}}</ref> The Lavra is not only located in another part of the city, but is part of a different national sanctuary than Saint Sophia Cathedral. While being a cultural attraction, the monastery is once again active, with over 100 monks in residence.{{citation needed|date=May 2017}} It was named one of the [[Seven Wonders of Ukraine]] on 21 August 2007.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

==== 2026 drone attacks ====
On 24 January 2026, a Russian drone attack shattered windows and damaged two buildings of the complex, marking the first time it sustained war-related damage since [[World War II]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ukraine war latest: Russian air attack damages Kyiv's holiest site |url=https://kyivindependent.com/ukraine-war-latest-update-2026-01-26/ |author=Kollen Post |date=2026-01-26 |access-date=2026-01-26|website=The Kyiv Independent}}</ref>

On 14 June 2026, a Russian drone attack caused significant damage to the complex, as part of a targeted attack that also damaged other cultural elements in Kyiv.<ref name="Guardian video">{{cite news |title=Cathedral burns at Unesco-listed Kyiv Pechersk Lavra after Russian attack |work=The Guardian |date=15 June 2026 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/video/2026/jun/15/dormiton-cathedral-burns-unesco-kyiv-pechersk-lavra-russian-attack |access-date=15 June 2026}}</ref><ref name="Beaumont">{{cite news |last=Beaumont |first=Peter |title=Kyiv monastery set on fire in night of Russian attacks across Ukraine |work=The Guardian |date=15 June 2026 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jun/15/kyiv-pechersk-lavra-monastery-on-fire-russian-attacks-ukraine |access-date=15 June 2026}}</ref> The damage, likely from [[Geran-2]] drones ("the Russian version of the Iranian Shahed-type kamikaze drone"<ref name="WaPoKyivMonastery2026"/>), included destruction of a significant portion of the roof, damage to the main dome and cupola, and significant fires within the structure of the [[Dormition Cathedral, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra|Dormition Cathedral]].<ref name="ki-2026"/> This was the first time the structure was targeted in an armed conflict since World War II. The [[Security Service of Ukraine]] published photos of debris from the Geran-2 drone.<ref name="WaPoKyivMonastery2026">{{cite news |last1=Stern |first1=David L. |last2=Galouchka |first2=Anastacia |title=Russian attack kills at least five and damages historic monastery in Kyiv |work=The Washington Post |date=15 June 2026 |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/06/15/russian-attack-kyiv-damages-historic-monastery-kills-least-5/ |access-date=15 June 2026}}</ref> By 15 June, the remains of two Shahed-type drones had been found at the UNESCO World Heritage site by state security officials, suggesting at least two drones targeted the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra complex around 04:55 a.m. Kyiv time.<ref name="Beaumont"/> Russia denied responsibility and blamed a US-made Patriot air-defence missile,<ref name="Beaumont"/><ref name="NOS"/> but the Kremlin spokesperson offered no evidence of this claim,<ref name="NOS"/> and no explanation why the remains of Russian-made Shahed-type Geran-2 drones were found at the site instead.<ref name="Beaumont"/>

Prime Minister [[Yulia Svyrydenko]] of Ukraine posted a photo of the burning monastery and wrote: "A brutal assault on our people ‌and our heritage. This is the true face of Russia’s Orthodox values. We ask for prayers for the salvation of the shrine from destruction. Another Russian crime against humanity, against history, against Christianity."<ref name="Beaumont"/> Ukraine's President [[Volodymyr Zelensky]] called it "one of Russia’s most serious crimes ​against Christian culture to date",<ref name="Beaumont"/><ref name="NOS"/> adding: "It is vital that the world does not remain silent in the face of this latest act of Russian barbarism."<ref name="NOS"/> The attack on the Lavra was widely condemned by European leaders, with [[European Commission]] President [[Ursula von der Leyen]] calling it "pointless violence": 'Russia has once again shown that it is interested only in violence and destruction.'<ref name="NOS">{{cite web |title=Europa spreekt schande van Russische aanval op historisch klooster in Kyiv |trans-title=Europe condemns Russia’s attack on a historic monastery in Kyiv |website=[[Nederlandse Omroep Stichting|NOS]] |date=15 June 2026 |url=https://nos.nl/artikel/2618687-europa-spreekt-schande-van-russische-aanval-op-historisch-klooster-in-kyiv |language=nl |access-date=15 June 2026}}</ref> French President [[Emmanuel Macron]] stated that "nothing justifies this attack on our universal heritage,"<ref name="NOS"/> with France's European and Foreign Minister [[Jean-Noël Barrot]] adding: "It is as if [[Notre-Dame de Paris|Notre-Dame]] or the [[Basilica of Saint-Denis|Cathedral of Saint-Denis]] were to be bombed in France."<ref name="NOS"/><ref name="Beaumont"/> EU Foreign Chief [[Kaja Kallas]] issued a statement qualifying the attacks on the monastery, just like attacks on Ukrainian civilians, as [[war crime]]s, announcing another round of sanctions against Russia.<ref name="NOS"/> The [[Romanian ==Buildings and structures==
[[File:Perchersk Lavra, Kyiv Panorama.jpg|thumb|Pechersk Lavra, Kyiv, in 1889, [https://www.nga.gov/research/library/imagecollections/features/watsons_russia.html Department of Image Collections], [[National Gallery of Art]] Library, Washington, D.C.]]
The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra contains numerous architectural monuments, ranging from [[bell tower]]s to cathedrals to cave systems and to strong stone [[Lavra fortification|fortification walls]]. The main attractions of the Lavra include the [[Great Lavra Belltower]], and the [[Dormition Cathedral, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra|Dormition Cathedral]], destroyed in fighting the Germans [[World War II]], and fully reconstructed in the 1990s after the fall of Soviet Union by Ukraine.

Other churches and cathedrals of the Lavra include: the [[Refectory Church (Pechersk Lavra)|Refectory Church]], the Church of All Saints, the [[Church of the Saviour at Berestove]], the Church of the Exaltation of Cross, the Church of the Trinity, the Church of the Nativity of the Virgin, the Church of the Conception of St. Anne, and the Church of the Life-Giving Spring. The Lavra also contains many other constructions, including: the St. Nicholas Monastery, the Kyiv Theological Academy and Seminary, and the Debosquette Wall.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===Great Lavra Belltower===
{{main|Great Lavra Belltower}}

The Great Lavra Belltower is one of the most notable features of the Kyiv skyline and among the main attractions of the Lavra. 96.5 meters in height, it was the tallest free-standing belltower at the time of its construction in 1731–1745, and was designed by the architect [[Gottfried Johann Schädel|Johann Gottfried Schädel]]. It is a [[Classical architecture|Classical style]] construction and consists of [[storey|tier]]s, surmounted by a [[gilding|gilded]] [[dome]].{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===Dormition Cathedral===
[[File:Собор Успенський2.jpg|thumb|The restored Dormition Cathedral]]{{Main|Dormition Cathedral, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra}}
Built in the 11th century, the main church of the monastery was destroyed during the [[World War II]], a couple of months after the Nazi Germany troops occupied the city of [[Kyiv]], during which the Soviet Union conducted the controversial [[1941 Khreshchatyk explosions]]. Withdrawing Soviet troops practiced the tactics of [[scorched earth]] and blew up all the Kyiv bridges over [[Dnieper]] as well as the main Khreshchatyk street and Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=2016-11-03 |title=1941: уничтожение Успенского собора в Лавре |url=https://www.bbc.com/ukrainian/ukraine_in_russian/2016/11/161103_ru_s_tsalyk_uspensky_cathedral |access-date=2023-06-02 |website=BBC News Україна |language=ru-UA}}</ref> The destruction of the cathedral followed a pattern of Soviet disregard for cultural heritage, as they previously blew up the ancient [[St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery]] nearby in the 1930s.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Гогун |first=Александр |date=2021-09-20 |title=Вандалы-орденоносцы. Как Красная армия взрывала Киев |language=ru |work=Радио Свобода |url=https://www.svoboda.org/a/kak-krasnaya-armiya-vzryvala-kiev/31467844.html |access-date=2023-06-02}}</ref>

In 1928, the monastery was converted into an anti-religious museum park by the Soviet authorities and after their return no efforts were provided to restore the church. The temple was finally restored in 1995 after Ukraine obtained its independence and the construction was accomplished in two years. The new Dormition Church was consecrated in 2000.<ref name=":1" />

===Gate Church of the Trinity===
{{main|Gate Church of the Trinity, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra}}

The Gate Church of the Trinity is located atop the Holy Gates, which houses the entrance to the monastery. According to a legend, this church was founded by the [[Chernihiv]] [[Rulers of Kievan Rus'|Prince]] [[Sviatoslav II of Kiev|Sviatoslav II]]. It was built atop an [[Architecture of Kievan Rus'|ancient stone church]] which used to stand in its place.
After the fire of 1718, the church was rebuilt, its revered facades and interior walls enriched with ornate stucco work made by craftsman V. Stefaovych.
In the 18th century, a new gilded pear-shaped dome was built, the facade and exterior walls were decorated with stucco-moulded plant ornaments and a vestibule built of stone attached to the north end. In the early 20th century, the fronts and the walls flanking the entrance were painted by icon painters under the guidance of V. Sonin. The interior of the Gate Trinity Church contains murals by the early 18th century painter Alimpy Galik.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===Refectory chambers with Church of the Saints Anthony and Theodosius===
{{main|Refectory Church, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra}}
[[File:Kijów zabytkowy (307).JPG|thumb|Refectory Church]]
The refectory chambers with the Church of the Saints Anthony and Theodosius is the third in a series of temples. The original temple was built in the 12th century and no drawings or visual depictions of it remain. The second temple was built at the time of the [[Cossack Hetmanate]] and was disassembled by the Russian authorities in the 19th century. It was replaced with the current temple, often referred to as the Refectory Church of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===The All Saints Church===
The All Saints Church, erected in 1696–1698, is a fine specimen of Ukrainian baroque architecture. Characteristic of the church facades are rich architectural embellishments. In 1905, students of the Lavra art school painted the interior walls of the church. The carved wooden iconostasis is multi-tiered and was made for the All Saints church in the early 18th century.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===Church of the Saviour at Berestove===
{{main|Church of the Saviour at Berestove}}
[[File:Церква Спаса-на-Берестові, м. Київ.jpg|thumb|Side view of the [[Church of the Saviour at Berestove]] seen with its [[campanile]], designed by architect Andrei Melenskyi in the [[Classical architecture|Classical style]].]]

The Church of the Saviour at Berestove is located to the North of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. It was constructed in the village of Berestove around the start of the 11th century during the reign of Prince [[Vladimir II Monomakh|Vladimir Monomakh]]. It later served as the mausoleum of the Monomakh dynasty, also including [[Yuri Dolgoruki]], the founder of Moscow. Despite being outside the Lavra fortifications, the Church of the Saviour at Berestove is part of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra complex.{{cn|date=January 2023}}
{{clearleft}}

===Caves===
{{main|Near Caves}}

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra caverns are a system of narrow underground corridors (about 1-1½ metres wide and 2-2½ metres high), along with numerous living quarters and underground chapels. In 1051, the monk [[Anthony of Kiev|Anthony]] settled in an old cave in a hill near the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. This cave received additions including corridors and a church, and is now the [[Far Caves]]. In 1057, Anthony moved to a cave near the Upper Lavra, now called the [[Near Caves]].{{cn|date=January 2023}}

Foreign travellers in the 16th–17th centuries wrote that the catacombs of the Lavra stretched for hundreds of kilometres, reaching as far as Moscow and [[Velikiy Novgorod|Novgorod]],<ref name="Touring Kyiv">{{cite book | first=Ruta | last=Malikenaite | year=2003 | title=Guidebook: Touring Kyiv | publisher=Baltia Druk | location=Kyiv | isbn=966-96041-3-3}}</ref> spreading awareness of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.{{cn|date=January 2023}}

===Library===
The notable library of the Lavra was burned during the reign of [[Peter the Great]]. On the night of April 21-22, 1718, Orthodox monks — tsarist agents — set fire to the premises of the Lavra, where its library and archive with unique documents and books from the historical past of Ukraine were located.

In 1988, after the restoration of the monastery's activities, library work was resumed. The funds began to be replenished with those publications that the Lavra monks and parishioners managed to save. New books began to be purchased, and some of the books that began to be published by the Lavra printing house restored in 1995 were transferred to the library.

Over 20 years of activity after the revival of the monastery, more than 10 thousand volumes were collected. In 2008, the library was moved to premises that allow the best placement and organization of library funds. Accounting and cataloging of the Lavra library funds were digitized.

==Museum==
[[File:Orthodox pilgrim.jpg|thumb|Eastern Orthodox pilgrim in Kyiv Pechersk Lavra – women must cover their head when entering the lavra.]]

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra is one of the largest museums in Kyiv. The exposition is the actual ensemble of the Upper (Near Caves) and Lower ([[Far Caves]]) Lavra territories, which house many architectural relics of the past. The collection within the churches and caves includes articles of precious metal, prints, higher clergy portraits and rare church hierarchy photographs.<ref name="Kyiv Sightseeing Guide"/> The main exposition contains articles from 16th to early 20th centuries, which include [[chalice (cup)|chalice]]s, [[crucifix]]es, and textiles from 16th–19th centuries, with needlework and [[embroidery]] of Ukrainian masters. The remainder of the collection consists of pieces from the Lavra's Printing House and the Lavra's Icon Painting Workshop.<ref name="Kyiv Sightseeing Guide">{{cite book | year=2001 | title=Kyiv Sightseeing Guide | publisher=Centre d'Europe | location=Kyiv/Lviv | isbn=966-7022-29-3}}</ref>

The museum provides tours of the [[catacombs]], which contain remains of Eastern Orthodox saints or their relics. The Caves are of geological interest because they are excavated into [[loess]] ground. They form one of the most extensive occurrences of loess caves in the world.{{citation needed|date=January 2020}}
