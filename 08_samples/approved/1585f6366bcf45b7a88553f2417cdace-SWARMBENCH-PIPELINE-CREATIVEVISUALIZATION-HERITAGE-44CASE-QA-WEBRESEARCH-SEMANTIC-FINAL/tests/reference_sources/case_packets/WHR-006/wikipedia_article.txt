'''Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve''' ({{Langx|fr|Réserve naturelle intégrale du Mont Nimba}}) is a protected area and [[UNESCO]] [[World Heritage Site]] located in both [[Guinea]] and [[Ivory Coast|Côte d'Ivoire]], extending over a total of area of {{cvt|175.4|km2|acre}}, with {{cvt|125.4|km2|acre}} in Guinea, and {{cvt|50|km2|acre}} in Côte d'Ivoire. The reserve covers significant portions of the [[Nimba Range]], a geographically unique area with unusually rich [[flora]] and [[fauna]], including exceptional numbers of single-site [[Endemism|endemic]] [[species]], such as [[Nimbaphrynoides]] (a genus of [[Viviparity|viviparous]] toads), the [[Nimba otter shrew]], and multiple species of [[horseshoe bat]]s.<ref name = "unesco">{{Cite web|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/155/|title=Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve |website = UNESCO World Heritage Convention|language=en|access-date=10 December 2022}}</ref> Its highest peak is [[Mount Richard-Molard]] at 1,752&nbsp;m (5,750&nbsp;ft), which is the highest peak of both countries.

==History==
The strict nature reserve was established in 1943 by Order No. 4190 SE/F in Côte d'Ivoire and in 1944 by decree in Guinea. The Guinean part was accepted as a biosphere reserve in 1980. Both reserves were combined to form one [[World Heritage Site]] in 1981 (Guinea) and 1982 (Côte d'Ivoire) because of its outstanding biodiversity and unique mosaic of tropical habitats.<ref name="unesco" /> [[Iron ore|Iron-ore]] exploration for mining in the most vulnerable montane zone started in 1992, and the reserve has been listed as a [[World Heritage Site]] in danger since then.<ref name = "unesco"/> For the sake of an improved protection, a conservation management centre is being built by [[Guinean Parks Foundation]]. The project was approved by the Guinean Government and [[CEGENS]] in 2016.

== Geography ==
The [[Nimba Range]] is a narrow ridge extending approximately 40&nbsp;km long, with an orientation of northeast–southwest; it forms a part of the southern extent of the [[Guinea Highlands]]. Its highest peak is [[Mount Richard-Molard]] at 1,752&nbsp;m (5,750&nbsp;ft). Other peaks include Grand Rochers at 1694&nbsp;m (5558&nbsp;ft), Mont Sempéré at 1682&nbsp;m (5518&nbsp;ft), Mont Piérré Richaud at 1670&nbsp;m (5479&nbsp;ft), Mont Tô at 1675&nbsp;m (5495&nbsp;ft), and Mont LeClerc 1577&nbsp;m (5174&nbsp;ft). All of them are located in the Guinean part of the reserve. There are about fifty springs, including the origins of the [[Cavalla River|Cavally]], [[Cestos River|Cestos]], and [[Sassandra River]]s. Mining of top-quality iron-ore poses the major threat to the unique geomorphology and wildlife.

== Climate ==
The [[Nimba Range]] has a sub-equatorial montane climate. Temperature changes extremely with altitude, with a daytime maximum ranging between 24&nbsp;°C and 33&nbsp;°C, and the nightly minimum  can fall below 10&nbsp;°C. Some parts of the reserve receive significantly less precipitation, due to [[Rain shadow effect|rain-shadow effect]] of the high ridge. In general, southern slopes are moister than the leeward northern ones, which are affected by dry [[Harmattan]] wind from the [[Sahara]]. The Nimba Range, as well as the reserve, has exceptional microclimatic diversity.<ref name = "unesco"/>

== Ecology ==
[[File:Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve-108442.jpg|thumb|Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve]]
Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve lies within the [[Guinean Forests of West Africa]] [[Biodiversity hotspot|Biodiversity Hotspot]]. It harbours an especially rich flora and fauna, and it is the home of more than 2000 [[vascular plant]] species, 680 [[vertebrate]] species, 132 of which are [[mammal]]s, and to more than 2,500 [[invertebrate]] species.<ref name = "unesco"/> The reserve is a subject of biological surveys, because there are still large numbers of unknown species. Vertebrates endemic to the reserve include the [[Western Nimba toad|Nimba viviparous toad]], [[Lamotte's roundleaf bat]], and [[Nimba otter shrew]]. Other rare and endangered animals found within the reserve include the [[West African lion]], [[pygmy hippopotamus]], [[zebra duiker]], and a population of [[western chimpanzee]] that use stones for tools.<ref name = "unesco"/> The reserve has been designated an [[Important Bird Area]] (IBA) by [[BirdLife International]] because it supports significant populations of many bird species.<ref name=bli>{{cite web |url= https://datazone.birdlife.org/site/factsheet/mount-nimba-strict-nature-reserve-iba-c%C3%B4te-divoire |title= Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve|author=<!--Not stated--> |date=2024|website= BirdLife Data Zone|publisher= BirdLife International|access-date= 2024-11-10}}</ref>

Terrestrial [[ecoregions]] within the reserve include [[Western Guinean lowland forests|Western Guinean lowland forest]], [[Guinean montane forests|Guinean montane forest]], [[Guinean forest-savanna mosaic]], and [[West Sudanian savanna]]. The [[Nimba Range]] is a part of a distinct [[Ecoregion|freshwater ecoregion]] with a high portion of endemic aquatic species.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.feow.org/ecoregions/details/513|title=Freshwater Ecoregions Of the World|website=www.feow.org|access-date=2016-12-19}}</ref>

Terrestrial vegetation varies with altitude and cardinal orientation.
* The mountain is isolated from other [[sky island]]s of [[Guinée forestière|Forest Guinea]] by the lowland [[West Sudanian savanna]] and [[Guinean forest-savanna mosaic|Guinean forest-savannah mosaic]]. Savanna habitats are broken by [[gallery forest]]s and closed canopy forest islands.
* The lowland [[foothills]] are covered by the [[Western Guinean lowland forests|Western Guinean Lowland Forest]] up to 600 meters. The characteristic forest type is semi-deciduous high forest with a main canopy and an emergent layer. There are 40-70 tree species found in a single hectare, and up to 500 forest tree species recorded in the area.
* [[Guinean montane forests|Guinean Montane Forests]] are located between 600 and 1200–1400 meters. At lower levels, semi-deciduous high forests extend up to 900 meters; they are poorer in species compared to lowlands. Short dry forests can be found in the eastern end of the ridge. From 900 meters altitude, where the mountain is covered by clouds almost most of the year, mossy forests are present, which can be divided into three types: Parinari exclesa forests (1), summit forests (2), and tree fern forests in the valleys where groundwater is abundant (3). Above the tree zone, there is a transition of Protea bushland to grassland.
* Isolated high-altitude grassland extends from 1200 to 1400 meters to the highest peaks. There, the most famous endemic vertebrates are the [[Western Nimba toad|Nimba viviparous toad]] (''Nimbaphrynoides occidentalis''), and the [[Lamotte's roundleaf bat]] (''Hipposideros lamottei''), the latter recorded from only one mining site. Endemic plants include among others ''[[Asplenium schnellii]]'', ''[[Osbeckia porteresii]]'' and ''[[Blaeria nimbana]]''.

== Tourism ==
Mount Nimba lies in a remote region along the tri-national border of Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire, and Liberia, far from their capital cities. In fact, the Liberian third of the ridge is seriously degraded, thanks to the former intense mining, while the Guinean part has remained ecologically intact and natural.
