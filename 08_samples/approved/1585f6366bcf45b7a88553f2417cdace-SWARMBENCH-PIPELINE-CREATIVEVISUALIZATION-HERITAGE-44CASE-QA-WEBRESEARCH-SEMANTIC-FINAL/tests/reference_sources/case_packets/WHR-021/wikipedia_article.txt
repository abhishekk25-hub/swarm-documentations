The '''archaeological site of Sabratha''' is an excavated [[Numidia]]n and later [[Roman Empire|Roman]] city situed near present-day [[Sabratha]], [[Libya]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Archaeological site of Sabratha (Libya) {{!}} African World Heritage Sites|url=https://www.africanworldheritagesites.org/cultural-places/frontiers-of-the-roman-empire/sabratha.html|access-date=2021-09-19|website=www.africanworldheritagesites.org}}</ref>

It was a [[Phoenicia]]n trading-post that served as an outlet for the products of the African hinterland, and later part of the short-lived [[Numidia|Numidian]] Kingdom of [[Massinissa]] before being [[Roman Empire|Romanized]] and rebuilt in the 2nd and 3rd centuries A.D.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Archaeological Site of Sabratha|url=https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/184/|access-date=2021-09-18|website=UNESCO World Heritage Centre|language=en}}</ref>

== History ==
Sabratha, on the coast of Libya 40&nbsp;km, to the west of modern Tripoli, was founded by Phoenician settlers in the sixth or fifth century B.C. and grew to be a prosperous town during much of the Roman period, though it did not long survive the coming of the Arabs in the seventh century A.D. Today it ranks alongside [[Leptis Magna]] as one of the major classical sites of the region. The' modern exploration of the ruins was begun in 1926<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal|last=Kenrick|first=Philip|title=Excavations at Sabratha 1948-1951: A Report on the Excavations Conducted by Dame Kathleen Kenyon and John Ward-Perkins|url=https://archaeologydataservice.ac.uk/library/browse/issue.xhtml?recordId=1161456&recordType=MonographSeries|journal=Journal of Roman Studies Monographs|volume=2|pages=416}}</ref> during the Italian colonial period and in the ensuing years much of the heart of the town was laid bare. During the period of the British Military Administration immediately after the end of the Second World War, three seasons of work were carried out by a British expedition.<ref name=":0" />
