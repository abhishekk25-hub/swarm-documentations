COUNTY CONNECT TRANSIT AUTHORITY
ADOPTED TITLE VI MAJOR SERVICE CHANGE, DISPARATE IMPACT AND DISPROPORTIONATE BURDEN POLICY
(Board Resolution 2021-14, as amended; applies to all fixed-route service changes)

1. Purpose and federal basis
This policy implements the service-equity requirements that apply to the Authority as a
recipient of Federal Transit Administration funds. It establishes the local thresholds the
Authority is required to adopt for identifying a major service change, a disparate impact on
minority populations, and a disproportionate burden on low-income populations, and the method
the Authority uses to measure those effects. The governing federal framework, the definitions
of "minority population" and "low-income population", and the requirement that the Board
consider the equity analysis before adopting a major service change are set by FTA Circular
4702.1B, Chapter IV. Staff must confirm and cite the current Circular and the FTA Title VI
program requirements when preparing each analysis.

2. Major service change threshold
A service change is a MAJOR service change when it changes, on the affected day type, either
the daily revenue miles or the number of daily trips on a route by twenty-five percent
(25%) or more. The screen is applied to each route on its own.

3. Concurrent changes are evaluated in the aggregate
When two or more service changes are proposed to take effect at the same time as a single
package, they constitute a major service change if, taken together, they meet the major
service change threshold, and the Authority evaluates the equity effects of the whole package
in the aggregate. Every route in the concurrently proposed package is included in the equity
analysis -- including routes whose individual change is below the 25% screen -- because riders
experience the combined effect of the package. A route is removed from the aggregate analysis
only for the data-quality reason in section 6.

4. Measurement method: people-trips
The Authority measures service-equity effects in people-trips. For each route:
   annual trips        = daily trips on the affected day type x service days per year for that
                         day type (see the service calendar).
   people-trips        = walkshed population (1/4-mile) x annual trips.
   minority people-trips    = walkshed population x minority population share x annual trips.
   low-income people-trips  = walkshed population x low-income population share x annual trips.
People-trips are computed for the before condition and the after condition; the route's
people-trip change is (after - before). For a realignment, the before and after walkshed
populations differ and both must be used. For a new route, the before condition is zero.

5. System rollup and the disparate-impact / disproportionate-burden tests
Sum the per-route people-trip changes across all included routes to obtain the package totals
D_overall, D_minority, and D_low_income. Then:
   minority share of the change   = D_minority / D_overall
   low-income share of the change = D_low_income / D_overall
Compare each share to the corresponding service-area population share:
   service-area minority share    = 0.49
   service-area low-income share  = 0.27
A DISPARATE IMPACT on minority populations is found when
   (minority share of the change) / (service-area minority share) >= 1.2
A DISPROPORTIONATE BURDEN on low-income populations is found when
   (low-income share of the change) / (service-area low-income share) >= 1.2
i.e. when the group bears at least twenty percent more of the change than its share of the
service-area population. The two tests are independent; one may be triggered while the other
is not. Low-income status is defined as household income at or below 150% of the federal
poverty level.

6. Data-quality exclusion
A route is excluded from the people-trips rollup only when the ACS block-group overlap of its
walkshed is below fifty percent (50%), because below that overlap the demographic shares are
not reliable. Excluded routes are listed and the exclusion is disclosed; they are not used in
D_overall, D_minority, or D_low_income.

7. Source-of-record and conflict resolution
The adopted FY2026 service-change package (the proposal of record) governs the planned service
levels. Where the raw GTFS schedule export disagrees with the adopted proposal on a route's
post-change service level, the adopted proposal governs and the discrepancy is logged.

8. Required disposition
If a disparate impact or a disproportionate burden is found, the analysis must state it
plainly, quantify it, and the Board must either avoid, minimize or mitigate the effect or
make the substantial-legitimate-justification finding required by the Circular before adoption.

9. Regulatory confirmation and citation
The legal footing of every analysis must be confirmed against the live/official authorities
listed in sources_to_confirm.md, not paraphrased from memory. For each authority and data
source the analysis records the requirement or fact it supplies, the official URL actually
retrieved, and the retrieval date, so the citations are frozen and defensible.

10. Transit-dependent populations and public comment
Beyond the minority and low-income tests, the analysis describes the effect on transit-dependent
populations -- zero-vehicle households, limited-English-proficiency residents, and older adults --
using the corresponding ACS sources for context (these are descriptive and do not change the two
numeric findings). Every public comment received during the hearing process is addressed in the
analysis with a disposition (for example incorporated, mitigated, noted, or declined with a
reason), including comments that fall outside the scope of the service-equity decision.
