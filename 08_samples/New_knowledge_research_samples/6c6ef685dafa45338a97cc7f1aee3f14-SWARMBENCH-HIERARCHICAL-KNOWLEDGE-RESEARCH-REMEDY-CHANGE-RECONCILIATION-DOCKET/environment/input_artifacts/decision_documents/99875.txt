             EXPLANATION OF SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCES
                Caldwell Trucking Superfund Site
                 Fairfield Township, New Jersey
In September 1986, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
issued a Record of Decision (ROD) that described EPA's remedial
actions for the cleanup of the Caldwell Trucking Company
Superfund Site in Fairfield Township, New Jersey. These
included:
• Restoration of water quality to Municipal Well No. 7, through a
  treatment process known as "air-stripping";
• Provision of an alternative water supply for residents with
  private wells potentially affected by groundwater contamination
  from the site; and
• Excavation and treatment of approximately 28,000 cubic yards of
  contaminated soils, and their placement in a secure landfill to
  be constructed on-site.
Section 117 (c) of the Comprehensive Environmental Response,
Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA) , and Section
300. 435 (c) (2) (i) of the National Oil and Hazardous Substances
Pollution Contingency Plan, contain provisions for addressing and
documenting changes to a remedy that occur after the ROD is
signed. An Explanation of Significant Differences (ESD) is
issued to document these changes, in accordance with CERCLA.
Municipal Well No. 7, located 3300 feet south of the site, was
found contaminated with volatile organic compounds (VOCs) during
EPA's comprehensive investigation of the site. A treatment
technology known as "air-stripping" was selected in the ROD to
remove VOCs in the groundwater, thereby restoring water from
Municipal Well No. 7 to drinking water quality. As originally
envisioned, emissions from the air-stripping system would be
vented to the atmosphere. Subsequent sampling from Municipal
Well No. 7 indicated increased levels of VOCs in the groundwater.
With the higher levels of contamination, calculated air emissions
from an air-stripping system would exceed State and Federal air
quality standards. Therefore, an "activated carbon" system would
have to be added to control emissions from the air-stripping
operations to meet the air quality standards.
Under Federal law, after the first year of operation, EPA would
not be responsible for operation and maintenance costs of
treatment systems for public water supplies. According to a
Township sponsored study, water extracted from Municipal Well
No. 7, and processed through the air-stripping/carbon filter
system would be twice as expensive as the Township's existing
contracts to purchase water. Consequently, the Fairfield Town
Council passed a resolution (Resolution #90-146) on October 10,
1990, declining the treatment system for Municipal Well No. 7.

The Township decided to continue purchasing water from the
Passaic Valley Water Commission, and forgo the use of Municipal
Well No. 7 as a source of potable water.
Therefore, EPA will not install the water treatment system for
Municipal Well No. 7, as originally set forth in the ROD. The
New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection concurs with
this decision.
This Explanation of Significant Differences (ESD) is being issued
to document the change to the ROD, and will become part of the
Administrative Record File. Supporting documentation for the ESD
will be available at EPA, 26 Federal Plaza, New York,
