                 EXPLANATION OF SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCES

 SOUTH JERSEY CLOTHING COMPANY/GARDEN STATE CLEANERS
           SUPERFUND SITES OPERABLE UNIT TWO


Site Name and Location

South Jersey Clothing Company/Garden State Cleaners Superfund Sites Operable Unit 2 (OU2)
Buena Borough, Atlantic County, New Jersey

Introduction
The purpose of this Explanation of Significant Differences (ESD) is to explain a change made by
the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) to the 1991 remedy selected for the Garden
State Cleaners (GSC) and South Jersey Clothing Company (SJCC) Superfund Sites (Sites),
located in the Borough of Buena, Atlantic County, New Jersey (Figure 1).

Under Section 117 (c) of the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and
Liability Act of 1980, as amended (CERCLA or Superfund), EPA is required to publish an ESD
when, after issuance of a Record of Decision (ROD), subsequent enforcement or remedial
actions differ in significant, but not fundamental, respects from the selected Site remedy.
Sections 300.435(c)(2)(i) and 300.825(a)(2) of the National Oil and Hazardous Substances
Contingency Plan (NCP) set forth the criteria for issuing an ESD and require that an ESD be
published if the remedy is modified in a way that differs significantly with respect to scope,
performance, or cost from the remedy selected in the RODs for the Sites.

EPA is modifying the scope of the remedy selected in the 1991 ROD (amended in 2010) by
incorporating the groundwater Classification Exception Area/Well Restriction Area (CEA/WRA)
established by the New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection (NJDEP) in 1997 into
the remedy. The CEA/WRA is an institutional control1 that will maintain the protectiveness of
the selected remedy by providing notice that the constituent standards for the aquifer are not or
will not be met in a localized area due to natural water quality or anthropogenic influences, and
that designated aquifer uses are suspended in the affected area for the term of the CEA/WRA,
until groundwater is restored.

Although a CEA/WRA was not called for in the 1991 ROD, a CEA/WRA was established for
the Sites in 1997 by NJDEP to ensure that the remedy would be protective in the short term,
while groundwater remediation was being implemented. More recently, EPA has determined
that the existing CEA/WRA needs to be expanded to fully encompass the extent of contaminated
groundwater at the Sites as the remedy continues to make progress in restoring the aquifer. In
March 2021, EPA requested that NJDEP expand the existing CEA/WRA and maintain it until
the cleanup goals established in the 1991 ROD and 2010 ROD amendment are met.


1 Institutional controls are non-engineered controls, such as property or groundwater use restrictions imposed by a
property owner by recorded instrument or by a governmental body by law or regulatory activity for the purpose of
reducing or eliminating the potential for human exposure to contamination and/or to protect the integrity of a
remedy.

The remedy, as modified by this ESD, remains protective of human health and the environment.

The ESD and documents that provide the basis of the ESD will be incorporated into the
Administrative Record for the Sites in accordance with Section 300.825(a)(2) of the NCP. The
Administrative Record is available for review during business hours on a limited basis and by

Site History, Contamination, and Selected Remedy
The SJCC Site is a 1.2-acre property located on the northwest corner of Central and Atlantic
Avenues in Minotola, Buena Borough, New Jersey, approximately 30 miles southeast of
Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. The GSC Site is located on Summer Road, approximately 500 feet
south of the SJCC Site. The land hydrologically downgradient of the Sites is occupied primarily
by residences and small businesses. A recreational area lies adjacent to the Cleary Junior High
School (Cleary School), located approximately 2,000 feet south of the Sites. The land
surrounding Buena Borough is primarily agricultural. Buena Borough is one of 56 municipalities
within the New Jersey Pinelands National Reserve.

SJCC was a dry cleaner that operated from the 1940s to the 1980s and is now defunct. A line of
the Central Railroad of New Jersey runs adjacent to the northwest property boundary of the
SJCC Site. GSC operated a dry-cleaning business on the property from 1966 until 2011. The
GSC building was demolished in 2011 and the property is vacant. The GSC property occupies
an area of approximately 3,000 square feet.

Remedial investigation (RI) fieldwork was conducted in two phases: from December 1989
through February 1990, and from January 1991 through April 1991. Phase I of the RI included
primarily surface and subsurface soil sampling, shallow and intermediate monitoring well
installation, and groundwater sampling. Phase II included shallow soil sampling, intermediate
and deep monitoring well installation, and groundwater sampling activities. In August 1991, a
feasibility study was completed for the Sites that proposed soil vapor extraction (SVE) for
remediation of the contaminated soil and a groundwater extraction and treatment system
(GWETS) for treatment of the contaminated groundwater.

Accordingly, a ROD was issued in 1991 that selected an SVE system for the contaminated soils
and a GWETS for the contaminated groundwater plume, located on the former SJCC property.
The SVE soil remedy for the Sites represented the first cleanup phase and was designated as
OU1 and the groundwater remedy was designated as OU2.

The 1991 ROD was amended by a ROD Amendment issued in 2010 to include acquisition of the
GSC property, demolition of the GSC building, and excavation of additional contaminated soil at
both Sites. The 2010 ROD amendment also included in-situ treatment of soil that could not be
practicably excavated due to depth and proximity to the groundwater treatment plant at SJCC.

The SVE system for the GSC Site was designed first and construction was completed in the
spring of 1994. The system operated from June 1994 through March 1995. In 1995, EPA
collected soil samples from previously contaminated areas and determined that the soil cleanup
goals for the SVE remedy at the GSC Site had been met. Completion of the OU1 remedy at the
GSC Site was documented with the approval of a Remedial Action Report on September 13,
1995.

Also, since the groundwater underlying both the GSC and SJCC Sites contained similar
contaminants, it was determined that one GWETS located on the SJCC property, would address
both plumes. The design for the GWETS was completed in August 1995. In April 1997, the
SJCC building was demolished to allow for the construction of the GWETS and SVE system,
which was completed in January 1999. The GWETS consists of 15 extraction wells in the
shallow and intermediate aquifer zones, pumping at a rate of 510 gallons per minute (gpm), and
12 injection wells. The SVE system at the SJCC Site operated from February 1999 through
February 2001. In 2000, EPA collected soil samples and determined that the soil cleanup goals
for the SVE remedy at the SJCC Site had been met through the SVE treatment system.

Pursuant to the 2010 ROD amendment, demolition of the building on the GSC property occurred
in August 2011, and soil excavation at both Sites was completed in September 2011. Completion
of the GSC building demolition and GSC soil excavation is documented in Remedial Action
Reports approved on September 29 and 30, 2011 respectively. The RD and Performance Work
Statement for in-situ soil treatment of the remaining soil contamination at the SJCC Site were
completed in October 2012. Thermal treatment was selected as the preferred in-situ treatment to
be implemented. This work was completed during 2016-2017 and resulted in about a 95%
reduction of vadose zone contamination. In 2013, an additional extraction well, EW-21, was
installed to enhance capture of the intermediate depth plume in the vicinity of the Cleary School.

In 2019, EPA conducted a five-year review (FYR) to evaluate the protectiveness of the
implemented remedies at the Sites. The FYR recommended that the downgradient extent of the
plume be determined to ensure that the plume is within the capture zone of the treatment system.
EPA installed three wells in August 2020 and sampled them in September 2020. Results of the
sampling clarified the boundary of the contaminated plume. Since these new wells were outside
of the current CEA/WRA boundaries, EPA submitted a proposed revision to the existing
CEA/WRA to NJDEP to expand the extent of the CEA/WRA established in 1997.

In September 2020, EPA transferred the responsibility for operation and maintenance of the OU2
remedy to NJDEP.

Description of the Significant Differences and the Basis for those Differences
The OU2 remedy at the Sites currently protects human health and the environment because the
groundwater extraction and treatment system is capturing and controlling contamination
emanating from the SJCC Site. Residential wells in the vicinity of the Sites with PCE
concentrations that exceed state drinking water standards (Maximum Contaminant Levels or
MCLs) are no longer used for potable purposes or have had systems installed for treatment of
PCE. Residences not connected to the municipal water supply and in the vicinity of the plume

are monitored semiannually. The most recent sampling event occurred in the summer of 2020.
During the fourth FYR for the Site in 2019, it was determined that groundwater capture at the
downgradient leading edge of the plume needed to be confirmed. EPA installed and sampled
three wells downgradient of the groundwater plume to confirm the edge of the plume. Sampling
results for the three new wells indicated 3.66 micrograms per liter (μg/l) of PCE, slightly above
the MCL (1 μg/l), in one well and no contamination in the other two wells. However, since the
new wells were outside the boundaries of the 1997 CEA/WRA, EPA submitted the respective
sample results to NJDEP and requested that the extent of the CEA/WRA be expanded to include
the newly installed wells, as they are needed to monitor and ensure full capture of the
contaminant plume.

On September 21, 2021, the NJDEP revised the 1997 CEA/WRA to more accurately reflect
current contamination levels onsite, as well as the horizontal boundary of the CEA/WRA (Figure

The GWETS has significantly diminished the groundwater contamination but has not yet met the
remedial goals of the 1991 ROD. EPA requested the expansion of the existing CEA/WRA until
the cleanup goals established in the 1991 ROD and 2010 ROD amendment are met.
Establishment of a CEA/WRA was not a component of the 1991 ROD, or the 2010 ROD
amendment, therefore, EPA is documenting the inclusion and expansion of the CEA/WRA as
part of the OU2 remedy for the Sites by issuing this ESD. The inclusion and expansion of the
CEA/WRA does not fundamentally alter the scope, performance or cost of the OU2 remedy.

Support Agency Comments
The NJDEP supports EPA’s revision to the remedy and decision to issue this ESD. The
NJDEP’s concurrence letter on the ESD was sent to EPA on September 24, 2021.

Affirmation of Statutory Determinations
EPA is issuing this ESD after consultation with NJDEP. NJDEP concurs with the approach
presented in this ESD. The remedy, as modified by this ESD, will continue to be protective of
human health and the environment and will comply with federal and state requirements that are
legally applicable or relevant and appropriate to the remedial actions.

The remedy for OU2 is technically feasible, cost-effective, and satisfies the statutory
requirements of CERCLA by providing for remedial actions that permanently and significantly
reduce the toxicity, mobility and volume of hazardous substances at the Sites.

Public Participation Activities
In accordance with the NCP, a formal public comment period is not required when issuing an
ESD. However, EPA will announce the availability of the ESD in a local newspaper of general
circulation. The ESD will be placed in the Administrative Record for the Sites and the
information repository at the Borough of Buena Municipal Building located at 616 Central
Avenue, Minotola, New Jersey. The ESD will also be placed on EPA’s SJCC and GSC Site
pages located at https://www.epa.gov/superfund/south-jersey-clothing and
https://www.epa.gov/superfund/garden-state-cleaners.
