Situation Overview and Humanitarian Needs
In the week to 17 May 2015, there were six confirmed Ebola cases, a considerable increase comparing to the previous week. Five out of the six new cases came from known contacts. Only one case come from an unknown source (Western Urban). In the last 21 days (to 17 May 2015), 19 confirmed cases have been recorded nationwide: six in Kambia District, which sits on the border with Guinea, ten in Western Area Urban, and three in Port Loko. Liberia was declared free of Ebola transmission by WHO on 9 May 2015 after 42 days since the safe burial of a confirmed infected person.

There have been a total of 8,605 people who have contracted EVD in Sierra Leone, of whom 3,541 have lost their lives. 19,030 children have been directly affected by the Ebola crisis, nearly half of whom have lost one or both parents.

Summary Analysis of Programme Response

Health
In preparation for the June 2015 integrated measles/polio and routine immunization defaulter tracing campaign, UNICEF has provided technical support in the development of training manual and data collection materials, the development and production of social mobilization materials (banners, flyers, jingles), the procurement of spares and technical support for the repair of faulty cold chain equipment in all 13 districts. The final dates for the campaign will be agreed upon this week.

UNICEF is supporting the MoHS to implement the Community Health Workers (CHW) program in eleven districts in the country. During the reporting period, UNICEF has conducted field missions in Bo, Moyamba and Koinadugu, three focus districts which do not have implementing NGOs, to build their implementation capacity. Refresher training on the key aspects of the CHWs program was organized for 195 Peripheral Health Unit (PHU).

With the aim of creating a sustainable CHW program, UNICEF supported the national CHW Technical Working Group (TWG) to conduct a Strength Weaknesses Opportunities and Threats (SWOT) analysis of the program based on the eight thematic areas of the integrated Community Care Management (iCCM)/CHWs Benchmark Framework. The results of the analysis will be presented in the next TWG meeting in the week of 24 May 2015. It will then be shared with all stakeholders and act as a base for further discussions and recommendation for next steps in the program.

UNICEF undertook a joint field visit with DFID supported consultants and other partners to Bombali and Tonkolili to assess DFID funded activities like Maternal and Child Health (MCH), Maternal and Newborn Health (MNH), service delivery by community health workers, family planning, youth friendly service delivery and in-service trainings including training on appropriate management of MNH in the context of Ebola. Preliminary reports from the consultants showed that despite the Ebola outbreak, the Integrated Reproductive Maternal and Newborn Health (IRMNH) programme was resilient and good progress had been made in all output indicators.

In a bid to contribute to the restoration of child health services, supportive supervision and coaching of Community Health Center (CHC) staff, UNICEF is providing technical and financial support to an average of four staff per PHU from 173 CHCs in 12 districts on Integrated Maternal and Newborn Care Initiative (IMNCI) and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC). The purpose of this supervision and coaching exercise is to assess the gaps and equip staff with the knowledge and skills to appropriately manage childhood illnesses and maintaining good infection control measures.

Nutrition
In the last week, as part of the Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM), a total of 21,992 children under 5 were screened at the community level in 77 out of 149 chiefdoms (466 communities). A total of 348 (1.58 per cent) severe acute malnourished (SAM) children were identified, of which 328 (89.1 percent) were admitted for treatment at the Out Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP), while 139 children3 who had SAM with medical complications were referred to an In-

So far the number of EVD centres that UNICEF is providing with nutrition supplies4 include: 13 Ebola Treatment Units (ETUs), 23 Ebola Holding Centres (EHCs), one ETU/EHCs, 11 interim care centres (ICCs) and six observational interim care centres (OICCs) as well as 20 Community Care Centres (CCCs)5.

Nutrition supplies have been prepositioned at District Medical Stores (DMS) for replenishment to the operational ETUs, EHCs, CCCs, ICCs and OICCs countrywide. Ready-to-Use Infant Formula (RUIF) is supplied regularly to all infants below six months of age at quarantined households who have been infected or affected by EVD as well as separated/children who have lost one or both parents. There are 106 infants 0-5 months of EVD affected mothers and infant orphans (0-5 months) of non-EVD affected mothers being supplied with RUIF on bi weekly basis in communities in Port Loko, Kambia, Tonkolili, Koinadugu, Western Area, Moyamba, Kenema, Pujehun, Bonthe, Bo and Kono.

UNICEF continues to support the Food and Nutrition Directorate to ensure a harmonized nutrition response for the EVD outbreak through twice-a-month Nutrition in Emergency Coordination meetings.

WASH
As the lead agency for WASH, UNICEF is coordinating the WASH sector in the Ebola response, helping to identify and fill gaps, in addition to monitoring the functionality of WASH facilities in Ebola Care Centers (ECCs). Moreover, UNICEF is coordinating the decommissioning of Community Care Centres (CCCs) and disposal of infectious waste, following established standard operating procedures (SOP) and supporting capacity building of implementing partners in WASH information management. UNICEF continues to support 17 CCCs, as well as 28 ETUs and EHCs in eight districts with essential WASH packages (sanitation facilities, waste management and water supply for drinking, personal hygiene and disinfection). UNICEF WASH is working with the Government's waste management stakeholders, including the office of the President, in findings urgent solutions for improving the current liquid and solid waste disposal sites.

During the past week, the Ministry of Water Resources (MoWR), with the support of UNICEF, delivered about 850,000 litres of water to Ebola affected communities including ECCs. Decommissioning of the second phase of 14 CCCs is complete, including relocation of two CCCs to new sites away from schools. Improvement of the physical structure of the remaining CCCs to mitigate the anticipated impact of the rains is almost complete save for minor improvements.

Decommissioning of select CCCs In line with preparations for the safe reopening of schools, the first phase of closure of select Ebola Care Centres (ECC), including CCCs on or near school premises, was completed under the leadership of the National Ebola Response Centre (NERC), with the Ministry of Health and Sanitation (MoHS) and partners. Of the initial 46 CCCs, 15 were decontaminated and closed during the first phase of decommissioning. In response to declining EVD case incidence, UNICEF is supporting the NERC, DERC, DHMT and partners in moving forward with the second phase of CCC decommissioning. During the second phase, 14 CCCs were safely decommissioned. There are now UNICEF-17 CCCs currently operational across four districts, with 136 total bed capacity.

Communication for Development (C4D) and Social Mobilization (SM)6
UNICEF continues to provide technical support to Social Mobilization (SM) Pillars across all 14 districts with special focus on Kambia, Western Area and Port Loko. Following the new cases in Kambia and Port Loko, the rapid response teams have been activated in hotspot chiefdoms in Port Loko along with hotspot busters for continued community engagement and social mobilization. UNICEF is engaging communities and working in close collaboration with the District Ebola Response Center (DERC), the District Health Management Team (DHMT), community stakeholders and paramount chiefs.

The Kambia Community Action Plan (KCAP) launched last week continues to engage communities through influential local descendants, elected representatives and women leaders. In Port Loko, a joint visit to Cotton Tree Island was conducted by UNICEF and WHO to reinforce the messages on Ebola and trace outsiders and travelers coming from hotspot areas. Still in Cotton Tree Island, UNICEF held a community engagement meeting focusing on safe and dignified burials and the importance of calling 117 for any EVD symptoms. The `Chiefdom stewardship plan' in the district has also been initiated aiming at assigning one lead NGO in each Chiefdom to coordinate all social mobilization activities.

Reactivation of Village Development Committees (VDC) During this reporting period, 190 VDCs meetings were conducted. Key community stakeholders, including section chiefs, traditional healers, Primary Health Unit (PHU) staff members, imams/pastors, women and youth networks, participated in these meetings. Discussions were held on Back-to-School, the by-laws related to outsiders and travelers coming from hotspot areas indicating that they are to be reported to the district authorities and the importance of calling 117 or a district hotlines. All the meetings were chaired by the section chiefs.
CCC Decommissioning Social mobilizers from UNICEF's implementing partners World Hope International, Marie Stopes International, Partners in Health, and Action Aid, visited 978 villages and reached out to 6,324 households in Bombali, Kambia, Kono, and Tonkolilli for active case finding, community surveillance, and community engagement. 657 women, youth networks and 231 traditional and religious leaders were engaged in community dialogues focussing on key messages like "Ebola is not gone yet'', safe and dignified burials, sustaining preventive behaviours in relation to Ebola and preventing complacency among the communities. 721 sick people were referred to the CCCs, the majority being from Kambia and Kono.
Community engagement interventions have been continuing in all districts for this second phase of decommissioning. Key messages focussing on the CCC areas being safe to use by communities, with an emphasis on the use of PHUs, as well as lifesaving messages on Ebola and relocation have been relayed to the communities. 2,305 households in 184 villages have been engaged on the decommissioning and relocation. 312 community dialogues have also been held, mobilising the communities to be engaged in the decision making during decommissioning.
Back-to-School SM pillar partners continue to support the Back-to-School social mobilisation activities, reaching 1,184 communities and 24,484 households in Tonkolili, Kambia, Koinadugu, Port Loko, Kenema, Moyamba, Bombali and Western Area this week. 638 community meetings were also held on Back-to-School.
Child Protection7
This week, the Case Management and Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS) taskforce has finalized a road map for rolling out the case management system and the CPIMS.
Registration: No new data has been reported since 14 April 2015. 19,030 (9,422 boys and 9,608 girls) have been documented by MSWGCA through child protection service providers, of whom 8,619 (4,311 boys and 4,308 girls) have lost one or both parents. 742 (349 boys and 393 girls) were reported as separated and/or unaccompanied. As reported earlier, an independent body was commissioned to conduct a data verification exercise to determine the accurate caseload of EVD-affected children. The results are expected to be finalized and reviewed by the MSWGCA in the coming weeks, and during this time MSWGCA has made the decision to not report any new data in order to avoid confusion.
Psychosocial support 220 children out of 243 (90.5 percent) in need of psychosocial services (PSS) received one-on-one counselling and/or group counselling support during this reporting period. Counselling support is provided as part of the follow up on children during and after quarantine, when reunified or placed in alternative care.
Observational Interim Care Centres (OICCs) There are currently 13 OICCs that remain on stand-by to provide care for asymptomatic contact children with no caregivers.
As of the 18 May 2015, four contact children were in quarantine in the OICC in Western Urban district and a cumulative total of 551 contact children have been receiving care and support in OICC's all over the country: 21 in Bo, 24 in Moyamba, eight in Tonkolili Magburaka, 91 in Bombali, 54 in Kenema, 23 in Kono, 100 in Port Loko Town, 65 in Port Loko Lunsar, 90 in Kailahun, 58 in Western Urban and 17 in Kambia. Interim Care Centres (ICCs) There are currently eight Interim Care Centres (ICCs) receiving children, out of twelve that are ready to receive children upon referral. The ICCs provide care and support for Ebola-affected (non-contact) children with no caregivers as last resort. On 18 May 2015, 106 children were counted in these eight ICCs: one in Bo, three in Kenema, 68 in Western Rural, 23 in Western Urban, two in Tonkolili, one in Kailahun and eight in Bombali. During the week 11 May 2015, seven children were newly admitted to the ICC's and six children were reunified with their families. All children in the ICC's receive psychosocial support.
From the total of 106 children, 81 children have been in the centre longer than six weeks. One ICC in Western rural is accounting for 56 of these 81 children. Most cases of children staying longer than six weeks in ICCs are due to complex family tracing and reunification processes.
Child Protection district coordination 81 referrals were made to the protection desks (38 boys and 43 girls) between 11 and 17 May 2015. 57 of the cases (70 percent) were immediately referred to appropriate partners for assessment and follow up.129 units of services were provided to children as a result of the referral from the protection desk. This includes two children placed in ICCs and 36 placed in other forms of alternative care. 71 children benefited from psycho-social services and 20 from other services (such as food, water and other non-relief items). Currently all 13 desks are operational, staffed by the MSWGCA and supported by UNICEF.
Education
In order to effectively plan for the transition and early recovery phase, the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MEST) and partners have conducted a two-day strategic planning workshop (12-13 May 2015) aimed at identifying priority activities and costing. Three broad priority areas have been identified: maintaining zero cases in schools, getting all children back to schools, and accelerating learning and ensuring quality. UNICEF participated in five of the subcommittees at the workshop (teachers training, WASH, social mobilisation, supply and logistics and special needs) and provided technical input into the prioritisation and costing of activities.
UNICEF also continues to monitor the school reopening. Field presence for education staff is maintained in high risk districts of Western Area Urban and Rural, Kambia and Port Loko to provide rapid response and coordinate interventions among partners and the MEST.
This week, UNICEF, the offices of the Deputy Directors for Education and partners in Western Area and Port Loko provided radios and learning materials to 49 children (38 boys and 11 girls) in quarantine homes. The focus of this radio education program broadcasting is to provide quality `catch up` learning for these children until they can return to school. This education program is broadcasted daily for 1-2 hours for each group (Primary, Junior Secondary and Secondary). Plans are being made to shift these education broadcasts in the afternoon so that children can also benefit after school. Content will also be changed to cover the core content curriculum put together for the compressed academic calendar.

Media & External Communication
With the distribution of hygiene and sanitation kits to schools all over Sierra Leone, local media interest and coverage were generated. A UNICEF's External Relations and Advocacy colleague gave interviews to the main national television and radio stations regarding the distribution of the kits. Local media Awoko reported on this week distribution of over USD 1.6 million worth of drugs and other medical supplies for the Free Health Care initiative, funded by the European Union. UNICEF, the EU and the Government of Sierra Leone also issued a joint press release that was widely distributed and used in the local media.
UNICEF facilitated a CCTV's crew and a freelance Spanish journalist to do a coverage on children who have lost one or both parents due to Ebola and UNICEF's response. The Chief of Education gave an interview to Politico, one of the main local newspapers, on back-to-school activities. UNICEF's role in the back-to-school activities was also highlighted in Science 151. Finally, UNICEF WASH specialist's published a note on hand washing in the context of Ebola on the UNICEF's blog site.
Last week, UNICEF's Facebook page witnessed an increase in likes by 36 making it a total of 7,377 total likes. Our Twitter account followers increased by 42 making it a total of 3,279 followers. It also received 86 mentions, 114 retweets and 11,950 posts reached.
Funding
In line with the UN inter-agency response strategy for the Ebola Outbreak, UNICEF humanitarian action for children (HAC) is appealing for USD 178 million for Sierra Leone to support the needs of children and communities affected by the Ebola crisis until the end of June 2015. As of 12 May 2015, USD 118.9 million has been received, 67 percent of the current appeal.
UNICEF greatly appreciates the contributions that have been received to date including from DFID/UKAID, OFDA/USAID, the World Bank, the African Development Bank, the Islamic Development Bank, SIDA, the Governments of Canada, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, Norway, Switzerland, and the United Arab Emirates, ECHO, OCHA, and the Danish, Japan, Swedish, Swiss and UK Committees for UNICEF, as well as the US Fund for UNICEF, and the private sector.
