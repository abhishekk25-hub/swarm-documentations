Situation Overview & Humanitarian Needs
The weekly caseload continued to decline with the country recording the lowest number of confirmed cases (107) for at least eleven weeks. The previous week had seen 184 cases, with 276 the week before that. Following recent trends, more than half of national cases were recorded in the Western Area (rural and urban) in and around the capital Freetown. In the south, the district of Pujehun, on the border with Liberia, has now gone more than 42 days without a case. Hotspots persist in the Western Area (Urban and Rural), Port Loko, Kambia and Kono.
A total of 7,923 people have contracted the virus of whom 2,788 have died. 15, 623 children are directly affected by Ebola of which 8019 children have lost one or both parents and 565 are unaccompanied or separated.
Summary Analysis of Programme Response

As of 19 January

Health
In order to ensure that health personnel has confidence and skills to deliver routine health and EPI services to under-five children in the context of Ebola, UNICEF continued to support the Ministry of Health and Sanitation (MoHS) and District Health Management Teams (DHMTs) to conduct the training of health personnel. During this week, 1016 staff working in 508 PHUs were trained in 5 districts (48% of the targeted PHU staff).
With the view to recover and strengthen the health system, a review of draft plans developed by the Health Sector Steering Group (HSSG) was convened by the MoHS. All partners committed to support the Government in the recovery process and emphasised the need for a multi sectorial approach.
The roll out of the `No Touch' Policy Guidelines for protecting CHWs continued and additional 778 CHWs from Tonkolili district were trained. This brings the cumulative number of CHWs trained to 3429 which is 57% of the targeted number. The Revised `Guidelines/Protocol for Delivering Maternal and Newborn Health Services at Health Facilities in the Context of Ebola', developed by the Reproductive and Child Health Directorate with technical and financial support from UNICEF, were presented to and approved by the National Ebola Response Centre.
Nutrition
In the last week, as part of the Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM), a total of 10,901 children under 5 were screened at the community level in 59 out of 149 chiefdoms (260 communities). A total of 154 severe acute malnourished (SAM) children were identified and admitted for treatment at the OTPs. 35 children who had SAM with medical complications were referred to an inpatient facility (IPF) to receive pediatric and nutrition care. A total of 214 boxes of RUTF were consumed. UNICEF is providing twenty four ETUs, ten ETU/EHCs, 56 EHCs, 21 interim care centres (ICCs)[2] and 19 observational interim care centres (OICCs) as well as 46 Community Care Centres (CCCs) directly with nutrition supplies1. The ETUs supported by GOAL in Port Loko district; Save the Children International and Aspen Medical in Western Area currently utilizes F75, F100 and RUTF to feed Ebola patients.
HIV
More concerns and anecdotal possible cases of EVD transmission to spouses through sexual intercourse arise from the districts. Although condoms are part of the discharge package there is a need for more education and counseling to the survivors and eventually their spouses on the risks occurred and the way to avoid it. UNICEF is working closely with case management pillar, Ministry of social welfare and C4D to address the issue.

WASH
As lead agency for WASH, UNICEF is coordinating the WASH sector Ebola response. UNICEF continues to support 25 ETUs and EHCs in eight districts with an essential WASH package (sanitation facilities, waste management and water supply for drinking, personal hygiene and disinfection), with a total capacity of 476 beds. UNICEF has also ensured that a total of 46 CCCs with more than 400 beds in five districts continue to have adequate access to essential WASH services. To date, approximately 39,953 people in quarantined households and communities have benefited from WASH Supplies distributed through WFP packages. A total of three OICCs in Port Loko and Western areas continued to have access to safe WASH services benefiting more than 83 Children through support provided by UNICEF WASH partners.

Regular functionality and water quality monitoring is being conducted to ensure that Ebola Centers have access to safe water supply through support provided to UNICEF partners. Since December 2014, a total of 121 centers in the country has been monitored and remedial actions taken to maintain quality supply. Since the last reporting period, an additional 13,935 persons including 3,923 children have been reached with Ebola prevention messages including the importance of hand washing with soap. Since June 2014, UNICEF's WASH partners operating in seven districts have reached more than 602,590 people with essential Ebola prevention messages.

All 5 UNICEF implementing partners for CCCs reported on social mobilization activities in their respective districts reaching out to 275 communities covering 25,592 households on utilization of CCCs, safe burials and Ebola rumours. As part of ongoing monitoring of community engagement for the CCCs, 6 Focus Group Discussions and 2 triads were conducted in Bombali, Tonkolilli and Kambia on perceptions of the communities on CCCs and their utilization. The key findings reflect improved perceptions around health seeking behaviors and community engagement process of setting up CCCs.

The Social Mobilization RapidPro Survey is now generating daily data at District, Chiefdom & Ward level on the following 6 issues: (i) House to House visits; (ii) Miking activities; (iii) Community Meetings; (iv) Resistance experienced by contact tracers or burial teams; (v) heard of any secret burials; and (vi) heard of any bodies exhumed after burial. This information will be integrated into the district responses.

Community Care Centres
Since 17 November, UNICEF-supported CCCs have triaged 1,541 patients and admitted 237 patients across 5 districts. The number of patients triaged at UNICEF-supported CCCs has increased by more than 50% since early December emphasizing the important role played by these facilities in building community trust and ensuring sick individuals seek professional assessment. Furthermore, the most recent data demonstrates a 37% increase in the number of patients seeking care less than 48 hours after the onset of symptoms.

Development of Social Welfare Plans: A draft monitoring and evaluation framework was developed with UNICEF technical support for CP/PSS/Gender pillar district plans to ensure district level quality assurance and consistency in the achievement of results.

Family Tracing and Reunification: Cumulatively 1,036 of the children requiring family tracing and reunification have been placed with families since the beginning of the outbreak. A daily reporting FTR register was endorsed by FTR partners for scale up.

Psychosocial support: During the reporting period (13 ­ 19 January) a total of 211 out of 222 (95%) in need of psychosocial support services received one-on-one counselling and group counselling support within their communities. Counselling is provided as part of the follow up on children when they are reunified with families or placed in alternative care. Cumulatively 9,314 children have been provided with psychosocial support since the outbreak out of 11,194 (83%) identified to need PSS.
Relief items: In the course of the reporting period 93 FTR kits were distributed. Since the beginning if the outbreak 6,966 relief items (including FTR and Survivor Discharge kits) out of 10241 (68%) identified in need were provided since the beginning of the outbreak. An additional 850 were prepositioned for FTR partners in the previous week.
Observation and Interim Care Centres: There are currently 9 functioning OICCs provided care for 106 contact children for the week 11-18 January. These include 9 children in Western Area Urban; 33 in Port Loko district; 17 in Kono; 17 in Bombali, 9 in Moyamba; 1 in Kambia; 14 in Bo; and 6 in Kenema. Cumulatively 299 children have been admitted in OICCs. OICCs provide care for asymptomatic, contact children with no caregivers.
8 of the 12 functioning Interim Care Centres (ICCs = for unaccompanied `non-contact' children) are currently accommodating 176 children in the reporting period. These include 3 in Bo, 5 in Kenema, 41 in Port Loko, 1 in Tonkolili, 1 in Kailahun, 98 in Western Area Rural, 27 in Western Area Urban. ICCS provide care for surviving and affected children without caregivers
Thirty-three OICC facility and MSWGCA staff from 6 districts were trained on the Standard Operating Procedures, including standard admission, operating and monitoring procedures for health, nutrition, child protection and psychosocial support at the OICCs. The three day workshop will be replicated in 6 different districts for facility and MSWGCA staff.

Protection desks: Seven protection desks are fully functioning, and 4 partially functioning in 13 districts. In the week 13 ­ 19 January 43 referrals were received by protection desks in 9 districts, 42 were responded to and cases closed. 39 of these referrals were to OICCs, and 13 also included for FTR.

Education
An assessment of the listenership of the Emergency Radio Education Programme (EREP) has been conducted. This week 2801 households covering 10 districts were visited by UNICEF implementing partners to determine whether children are following the radio programme. 51.3% of households had children of primary school age listening to the education broadcasts. UNICEF, with Ministry of Education and other implementing partners, held a workshop to finalize training materials for teachers' training on EVD prevention, social mobilization and psychosocial Support.

Supply and Logistics
One full charter arrived this week delivering 37 MT of PPE, medicines and cold chain equipment for the Ebola response. To date, 28 UNICEF supported charters have delivered 1,540 MT of essential supplies in response to the Ebola crisis in Sierra Leone. The seventh round of distribution of Ebola supplies to ETU/EHUs/District Medical Stores has been completed. Also this week, 2,100 Family Tracing and Reunification kits were delivered to all 13 Districts. Nutrition supplies have been prepositioned at district medical stores (DMSs) for replenishment to all ETUs, EHCs, CCCs, ICCs and OICCs as well as to support quarantined households countrywide.

Human Resources
As at 19 January 2015, the total number of IP staff in the country office is 55; 10 of these have been deployed in the field. Out of 147 national staff members, 47 are based in the field. The surge and stand-by-partner staff is 17; 14 in Freetown and 3 in the field. These staff members, including the 942 outsourced third party and government staff continuing to support the CCCs.

Media & External Communication
External Relations work with the BBC's Global Health correspondent, Tulip Mazumdar, resulted in a series of reports raising the profile of children in the Ebola crisis, including reports on Radio 4's Today programme, the BBC News website, and on BBC television. We responded to queries from Bloomberg and the Daily Beast on Ebola and FGM. The issue was also raised by the Huffington Post,

A video feature with a survivor in Kenema filmed by the ERA team was published this week on UNICEF's global YouTube site. The UNICEF website also published a story on UNICEF's mass distribution of malaria medicine. The South Africa-based Channel Africa radio interviewed a member of the Health team for more information on the distribution. On national radio, a member of the country office also took part in a morning discussion on the distribution on the Good Morning Salone show.

Social media postings continued on a daily basis on Facebook and Twitter. During the reporting period on Facebook there were 31 new page likes, 268 post likes, 5 post comments, 34 shares and 4,700 posts views on the site. On Twitter we gained 34 new followers and received 497 mentions with 17 retweets and 23 favourites.

Funding
In line with the UN inter-agency response strategy for the Ebola Outbreak, UNICEF humanitarian action for children (HAC) is appealing for USD 178 million for Sierra Leone to support the needs of women and children affected by the Ebola crisis until end of June 2015. To date, USD 115,5 million has been received, 65 percent of the current appeal.

UNICEF greatly appreciates the contributions that have been received to date including from the Governments of Canada, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Japan, The Netherlands and United Arab Emirates, DfID, ECHO, OFDA/USAID, SIDA, OCHA, the World Bank, and the Swedish, UK and US Fund for UNICEF, as well as the private sector.
