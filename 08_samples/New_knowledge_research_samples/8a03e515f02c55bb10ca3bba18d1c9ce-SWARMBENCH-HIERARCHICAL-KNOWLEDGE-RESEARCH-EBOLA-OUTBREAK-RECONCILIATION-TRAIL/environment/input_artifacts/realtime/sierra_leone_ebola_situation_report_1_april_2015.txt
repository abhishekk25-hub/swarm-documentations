Situation Overview & Humanitarian Needs
In the week to 31 March 2015, Sierra Leone recorded 25 new confirmed cases, down from 33 cases the previous week, and the lowest levels seen since June 2014. This continues a downward trend over the past four weeks. In the last two weeks, all confirmed cases have appeared in five districts ­ Western Area Urban and Rural, Port Loko, Kambia and Bombali.
A three-day Stay-at-Home campaign was held on 27 to 29 March 2015 to re-energizing Sierra Leoneans in the fight against Ebola and encourage personal commitments to ending the outbreak. The key objectives were to (i) Inform communities about the behaviours that continue to drive the EVD transmission in their own communities; and (ii) Encourage behaviours which can prevent transmissions such as: regular hand washing with soap and water; and find, isolate and treat Ebola cases through targeted door-to-door surveillance and contact tracing. The campaign saw more than 25,000 social mobilizers go house to house across the country distributing soap, and sharing Ebola safety messages. As a result of the campaign there was a 63 percent increase in calls to the 117 Ebola hotline to report sick and death persons compared to the previous Friday to Sunday period (1,627 calls vs 1,108). The number of suspect cases being tested1 increased only slightly compared with previous weeks, from 630 to 728 (9 percent). Overall, 10 cases were discovered during the campaign.
Source: UNMEER
The Stay-at-Home is part of several weeks of efforts to push for zero Ebola cases, something that started on 26 March 2015 with an initiative by women's groups for the wearing of yellow on Thursdays to keep people engaged in the Ebola campaign.
In Sierra Leone, a total of 8,547 people have contracted EVD, of whom 3,444 have lost their lives. 18,956 children have been directly affected by the Ebola crisis, nearly half of whom have lost one or both parents to the virus.
Summary Analysis of Programme Response
Health
UNICEF continued to support the Ministry of Health and Sanitation (MoHS) to roll out the training of staff in Peripheral Health Units (PHUs) and maternity hospitals in the districts on the revised guidelines on maternal and newborn health care in the context of Ebola. During this reporting period, an additional 117 PHU staff from Kailahun district were trained. This brings the cumulative number of health personnel trained to 2,127. The total number of districts which have completed their trainings is now 11 out of the targeted 14. The remaining districts are Bonthe, Pujehun and Moyamba where trainings will start in the second week of April 2015.
UNICEF continues to support the MoHS in preparing for the health campaigns that will deliver the package of essential health interventions to prevent diseases among children and mothers. A meeting held on 28 March 2015, between MoHS, CDC and UNICEF, proposed the interventions to be delivered in three campaigns organized one month apart. Currently the plan is that the first integrated campaign will be organized at the end of April 2015 and will provide Vitamin A, Albendazole, Middle-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening, and routine immunization. The second round will be organized in the last week of May 2015 and will deliver measles, polio and routine immunization intensification. The third round will be implemented at the end of June 2015 and will include: a second dose of polio, routine immunization, antenatal care, prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) and family planning. In all three rounds, there will be Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) prevention messages. The plan of action, scheduling, and budget for the different interventions will be finalized in the coming days to be approved by the National Task Force on Maternal and Child Health Week, and the Chief Medical Officer.

Nutrition
In the last week, as part of the Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM), a total of 21,003 children under 5 were screened at the community level in 61 out of 149 chiefdoms (798 communities). The screening, using no-touch technique2 designed to be done by Mother Support Groups (MSGs), is in progress in various communities in the districts and is contributing to an increase in the number of children being screened. A total of 224 severe acute malnourished (SAM) children were identified, of whom 1673 were admitted for treatment at the Out Patient Therapeutic Programmes (OTP)4, while 29 children who had SAM with medical complications were referred to an In-Patient Facility (IPF) to receive paediatric and nutrition care. The total number of children in the programme was 1,081, while 239 children were discharged as cured by end of the week. 175 boxes of Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) were consumed.

So far, the number of EVD centres that UNICEF is providing with nutrition supplies 5 include: Ebola Treatment Units (ETUs), Ebola Holding Centres (EHCs), 12 interim care centres (ICCs) and 14 observational interim care centres (OICCs) as well as 31 Community Care Centres (CCCs). Nutrition supplies have been prepositioned at district medical stores (DMSs) for replenishment to all ETUs, EHCs, CCCs, ICCs and OICCs as well as to support quarantined households and orphaned or separated infants under six months of age countrywide.

Ready-to-Use Infant Formula (RUIF) is supplied regularly to infants who are below six months and have been infected or affected by EVD in all the districts.

UNICEF continues to support the Food and Nutrition Directorate to ensure a harmonized nutrition response for the EVD outbreak through twice-a-month Nutrition in Emergency Coordination meetings on Wednesdays.

WASH
As the lead agency for WASH, UNICEF is coordinating the WASH sector in the Ebola response, helping to identify and fill gaps, in addition to monitoring the functionality of WASH facilities in Ebola Care Centers (ECCs) and supporting capacity building of Implementing Partners in WASH information management. UNICEF continues to support 31 CCCs; and 28 ETUs and EHCs in eight districts with an essential WASH package (sanitation facilities, waste management and water supply for drinking, personal hygiene and disinfection).

With the support of UNICEF, the Ministry of Water Resources (MoWR) provided during the three day `Stay at home' campaign water on a regular basis to high density neighbourhoods through a network of water bowsers. Overall, during the past week, more than 3.3 million litres of water were delivered to affected communities as well as in ETU/EHUs.

Decommissioning of selected CCCs: In line with preparations for the safe reopening of schools, the closure of select Ebola Care Centres (ECC), including CCCs, on or near school premises has commenced under the leadership of the National Ebola Response Centre (NERC), with the Ministry of Health and Sanitation (MoHS) and partners. Of the 46 CCCs, 15 have been decommissioned leaving 31 CCCs consisting of 264 beds currently operational. In each district, the District Health Management Team (DHMT), in collaboration with the District Ebola Response Centre (DERC) Social Mobilization pillar, is continuing discussions with the community during this transition, in order to ensure that communities are fully engaged in this process, just as they were when the CCCs were first constructed.

Following successful completion of Phase I of CCC decommissioning, community engagement plan has been finalised for the CCCs that will be decommissioned in Phase II. The key messages on decommissioning remain the same with an additional set of messages for relocation of CCCs (in a few instances). A set of key Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) is designed to enable effective community dialogues and participatory discussions around the CCCs. Key messages for the WASH implementing partners to use to mitigate any resistance in the communities while decommissioning and to respond effectively to community queries around supplies/materials for the CCCs has also been finalised. But, the fight to end Ebola is far from over, and the remaining CCCs will continue to operate and support affected communities.

This week, a joint meeting of the three Ebola affected countries was held in Freetown, Sierra Leone. Child Protection7
For the three-day Stay-at-Home campaign, protection desks across the country participated in the training of volunteers to orient them on criteria for identifying protection concerns and where to report such cases. Protection desks remained on alert during the campaign and received and referred cases that were identified by volunteers. In Western Area, 55 social workers joined the teams to go house to house to address protection concerns for vulnerable groups including children. Hundreds of children living on the street were housed and fed by child protection partners across the country. The Observational Interim Care Centre (OICC) in Kambia was made operational in preparation for the three days campaign and will remain open beyond.

Registration: 248 additional children (107 boys and 141 girls) were documented by the Ministry of Social Welfare, Gender and Children's Affairs (MSWGCA) / UNICEF and partners during the period 23 to 29 March 2015 as being affected by the Ebola Virus disease (EVD). Of this number, 247 children (106 boys and 141 girls) are in quarantine as a result of exposure to an infected person. 58 children (25 boys and 33 girls) were identified as single or double orphans. No children were documented as being unaccompanied in this period. The cumulative number of children documented through the child protection service providers is 18,956 (9,387 boys and 9,569 girls) of which 8,617 (4,311 boys and 4,306 girls) have lost one or both parents. 742 (349 boys and 393 girls) have been reported as separated and/or unaccompanied.

Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR): In the period 23 to 29 March 2015, six children were reunified by family tracing and reunification partners. The cumulative number of children registered as in need of FTR is 2,472, of which 1,875 (76 percent) were reunified with their families.

Psychosocial Support: The Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) strategy and Minimum Standards were revised by the MHPSS sub-working group and are awaiting approval by the MSWGCA. 100 children out of 100 (100 percent) in need of psychosocial services (PSS) received one-on-one counselling and group counselling support within their communities during this reporting period. Observational Interim Care Centres (OICCs): Currently there are 14 OICCs covering eleven districts with a total capacity of 275 beds. The OICCs provide care for asymptomatic contact children with no caregivers. Of these 14 OICCs, six were holding children during the past week. On 30 March 2015, a total of 22 contact children were in quarantine in the six centres: six in Bombali, six in Kenema, three in Port Loko (Port Loko Town), four in Western Urban, two in Kambia, one in Moyamba. Between the 23 and 29 March 2015, ten children were newly admitted to the centres. Nineteen children were reunified with their families. Eleven children were referred to an Interim Care Centre (ICC) for further family tracing and reunification. Two children were referred to a holding or treatment centre. OICC status data are reported on a daily basis through the RapidPro SMS service. As of 30 March 2015, a cumulative total of 532 contact children have received care and support in OICCs across the country.

Interim Care Centres (ICCs): There are currently eight Interim Care Centres (ICCs) receiving children, out of twelve that are functional at present. The ICCs provide care and support for Ebola-affected (non-contact) children with no caregivers. Between 23 and 29 March 2015, 167 children were present in ICCs: one in Bo, 12 in Bombali, three in Kenema, 45 in Port Loko ­ Lungi, 74 in Western Rural (56+18) and 31 in Western Urban. Fourteen children were reunified and these fourteen children all received Family Tracing and Reunification packages. All children in ICCs received psychosocial support.

Child Protection District Coordination: 461 referrals were made to the protection desks (206 boys and 250 girls) between 22 and 29 March 2015. 341 of the cases (73 percent) were immediately referred to appropriate partners for assessment and follow up. 200 units of services were provided to children as a result of the referral from the protection desk, including referral to OICCs/ICCs, psychosocial services, and the provision of food and non-food items.

Education
While schools remain closed, the Emergency Radio Education Programme (EREP) continues for children in Sierra Leone. Monitoring this week was done in two districts (Western Rural and Western Urban) covering 969 households with children at primary school age. Of these households, 494 had children of primary school age listening to the education programmes (51 percent).

UNICEF is supporting the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology (MEST) in the nationwide rollout of the C4D Back-To-School (BTS) strategy and the communication down to a community level. Three radio jingles in six local languages focusing on parents, pupils and teachers were launched during the `Stay-at-Home' campaign across 41 radio stations in the country. Based on the results of the Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey which emphasised the needs of reaching women, their engagement in the BTS is being emphasized from the beginning. At the national level, 32 market women associations have been oriented on BTS messages. The SM Pillar partners at national and district level have been oriented on the key BTS messages. The second round of teacher training aimed at reaching out to 2,000 teachers was completed on 26 March 2015. The number of teachers trained is not included in this report as the training reports are still being compiled.

The preliminary findings of the School Needs Assessment, conducted in March 2015, suggests that the number of Ebola related deaths of learners and teachers known to school authorities is high. In total, the assessment reported 945 deaths of learners and 181 deaths of teachers due to Ebola from all assessed schools. Additionally, 609 learners and 597 teachers were reported to have contracted and survived Ebola. 1.7 million school children were registered in the previous academic year.

1,020 Junior Secondary Schools opened for more than 275,234 learners to take their Basic Education Certificate Examinations (BECE), which started on 30 March 2015. School monitoring visits by UNICEF to selected schools in the Western Area showed that although schools had received all the essential hygiene materials for hand washing and thermometers for temperature monitoring, attendance by children was initially poor, though it picked up in the ensuing days. Teachers reported that the low turn-out of children for revision classes was partly linked to the reluctance by parents to send their children to school.

Supply and Logistics
The distribution of hygiene kits and cleaning materials for 8,000 schools is ongoing in preparation for the government's planned general reopening of schools on 14 April 2015. 1,230 hand washing stations together with 333,600 bars of soap, jerry cans and water purification tablets are being distributed to partners for hygiene promotion activities in the remaining eight districts.

Human Resources
As of 1 April 2015, the total number of international staff in the country office is 75, of whom 26 are deployed in the field. Out of 155 national staff members, 74 are based in the field. The total number of staff on surge and stand-by-partner staff is 30, with 20 in Freetown and 10 in the field. These staff members, including the 674 outsourced third party and government staff, continue to support the CCCs.
