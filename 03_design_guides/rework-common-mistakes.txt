I S S U E 0 1
The task reads as LLM generated
A reviewer reads instruction.md as something a human trainer wrote. Anything that
reads as model output is a finding, and this is the cheapest one on the list to avoid.
Em dashes throughout the instruction body.
Markdown spec-sheet formatting: ### headings, bolded field tables, numbered requirement lists.
Opening the instruction with a # heading so it reads as a specification, not a request.

I S S U E 0 2
Not commenting on the Quality Gate document
The QG LLM judge is not a one-shot report. It is a loop, and the loop only closes if you
write your side of it down.
01
Run the QG LLM judge on your
package. 	→
02
Read the feedback and fix the issues.
→
03
Once you have confirmed the fixes, re-
run the QG LLM judge. 	→
04
If it comes back with new issues you
do not fully agree with, comment on
the document: how you resolved it, or
why it is invalid.
T H E I S S U E
A few trainers are not adding comments on the document at all. Without that, nobody can tell a fixed finding from an ignored one, and a disputed finding just gets raised
again on the next round. Every finding needs one line from you, either how it was resolved or why you believe it does not apply.

I S S U E 0 3
Metadata does not match the execution logs
Declared depth, declared width and estimated_sub_agents all have to match what the trajectory
actually shows. A common one is a declared dag_depth of 3 or more where the run only ever
materialized a flat fan-out.
F I X
Re-measure depth, width and spawn count from the multi trajectory after the run, then write task.toml from that. Not from the plan.
If the realized shape is a flat fan-out and depth 2 or more is required, the decomposition needs a genuine intermediate tier that the orchestrator is forced through, not a
relabeled one.

I S S U E 0 5
The source URL was never checked
The URL mentioned in the instruction results in a 403 error, so the agent cannot perform the task on it. The multi tries to fabricate and achieves it.
It is the trainer's responsibility to check that the given source or URL is valid and reachable from the internet before proceeding with task
creation.
This is the worst class on the list, because it produces a passing run built on invented data. A failure
is easy to spot. A fabricated pass is not.
F I X
Verify every URL in the instruction and in every input artifact from a clean environment, and record the date in the RCA.
Check for rate limiting, not just reachability. A source that answers once and throttles under real load is the same defect.
Do not let an external rate limit be your difficulty.
Prefer a pre-staged or cached corpus so fetching is never the bottleneck.

I S S U E 0 6
Weights, gates, caps and floors in the verifier
The rubric has to be additive. Anything that shapes the reward beyond counting passed
checks is a finding.
N OT A L L OW E D 	
Ad-hoc weights outside the agreed point values
Multipliers, for example completion * rubric
Score caps and score floors
A binary gate that zeroes the score before the judge runs
A fallback reward written when the verifier fails
R E Q U I R E D
Additive points only, 1 for structure, 2 for reward-hacking, 3 for partial oracle,
nothing higher
Reward is points earned over points available
No multipliers, no caps, no floors, no gates
An infrastructure-failure flag, so an outage is distinguishable from a bad
submission rather than becoming a fabricated score

I S S U E 0 7
Do not mandate tool use
Ask for the deliverable, not the method. Naming a specific library, or fixing the order in which the
work has to be done, is an implementation constraint, and the verifier only ever sees the final
deliverable, never the agent's trace. So there is no way to enforce it and no reason to ask for it.
F I X
State the required outputs and the evidence quality bar, and leave the method open. The model can use any tool it wants, only the result gets graded.

T H E R C A D O C U M E N T
It is being written the night before standup
W H AT I S H A P P E N I N G 	W H AT I T I S F O R
T H E R U L E
Keep the RCA open beside the task from Phase 1. Write the phase entry when you finish the phase, not when someone asks for the document. Every task gets one,
submitted, whether or not the task shipped.
The RCA gets opened when the task is already blocked, or the evening before
standup when it is asked for.
Phase timings are reconstructed from memory, so nobody can see where the
handle time actually went.
Half the phases come back empty, and it is exactly the half that would have
explained the delay.
Several are simply never submitted.
It is the only place the decision trail lives: what you predicted, what you ran, what
you changed, and what that did.
Filled in as you go it costs minutes per phase. Reconstructed at the end it costs
hours and is less accurate.

G A P N O T A C H I E V E D
The single agent starts from a high floor
If roughly half the reward is reachable by a submission that is complete, schema-valid and lexically
derived from the source, then an agent that reads nothing already banks 0.5 and there is no room
above it for a 0.20 gap.
Averaging a saturated structural half with a floored judged half lands every competent submission
near 0.5. The scoring architecture, not the task, is what fails to express the gap.

-- 13 of 19 --

G A P N O T A C H I E V E D
An agent that did nothing should not score half
While every check is worth the same, a handful of file-existence checks is a large share of the
reward. That is how a submission with no real work still lands near 0.5.
file A exists
file B exists
file C exists
output.json parses
Four checks, four equal points, awarded for producing empty shells. On a ten-check rubric that is 40 percent before any work happens.
G U I D A N C E F R O M M A N A G E M E N T : P O I N T VA L U E B Y C H E C K T Y P E
1 pt
Structure check
Files exist, parse, match the schema, columns and enums
are valid.
2 pts
Reward-hacking check
Catches gaming: templating, near-duplicates, fabricated
evidence, uniform filler, degenerate submissions.
3 pts
Partial oracle check
Is the answer actually right: accuracy and recall against
real ground truth, grounded evidence, judged depth.
W H Y T H I S WO R KS
Nothing is worth more than 3 points, and reward is passed points over total possible points. The structural layer's share of the total drops on its own, without removing any
checks, so completeness alone can no longer carry a submission and the real work is where the points are.

P O I N T W E I G H T S
What it does to the score
Take five checks in each category, and assume the structural ones mostly pass, because
they usually do.
C AT E G O R Y 	W E I G H T 	PA S S E D T E S T S
Structure 	1 	4 of 5
Reward hacking 	2 	1 of 5
Partial oracle 	3 	1 of 5
score = sum(passed tests × weight) / sum(total tests × weight)
W I T H N O W E I G H T S
passed = 1 + 1 + 4 	= 6
total = 5 + 5 + 5 	= 15
score = 6 / 15 	= 40%
Structure contributes roughly 27 percent of the final score, 4 of the 15 possible points.
W I T H W E I G H T E D P O I N T S
passed = 1×2 + 1×3 + 4×1 = 9
total = 5×2 + 5×3 + 5×1 = 30
score = 9 / 30 	= 30%
Structure contributes roughly 13 percent, 4 of the 30 possible weighted points.
S A M E S U B M I S S I O N , S A M E C H E C KS
The score drops from 40 to 30 percent and the structural share halves, purely because the structural pass is no longer carrying the result. Nothing was removed and
nothing was made stricter.

D O N ' T C H A S E T H E G A P
What chasing looks like, and why it fails
C H A S I N G 	W H AT AC T U A L LY H A P P E N E D
I N O N E L I N E
A check that costs both arms equally does not move the gap. Never spend an iteration on a check whose effect is smaller than normal run-to-run variance.
Harden the one judge that is scoring too generously.
Reweight reward off the oracle and onto the judged checks.
Add a check to catch the specific thing SA did last run.
Add more sub-agent roles and hope MA climbs.
A judge was hardened from 0.875 to 0.000 on SA exactly as designed. SA's total
went up 0.057, because run-to-run variance on other axes was larger than the
check removed.
Reward was moved off the oracle onto judged narrative depth. Both arms dropped
by the same amount. Gap unchanged.

D O T H I S I N S T E A D
Same model on both sides
The single agent and the multi agent run the same model, so the same intelligence is on both sides
of the comparison. Adding more tests does not make the single agent fail, it just lowers both scores
by the same amount.
D O E S N O T C R E AT E A G A P
More checks. Stricter checks. A harsher judge. Anything that makes the rubric tougher
is paid for equally by both arms, so the difference between them stays exactly where it
was.
C R E AT E S A G A P
A genuinely more complex task, one that a single context cannot hold at quality, plus a
decomposition that lets the swarm actually split it. The decomposition is where the
multi agent's benefit comes from, so that is where the effort belongs.
S O
If the gap is short, the answer is a harder task and a better decomposition.yaml, not a stricter verifier.
