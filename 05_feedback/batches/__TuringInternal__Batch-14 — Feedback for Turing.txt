What we're asking for, and where it applies. Two of the asks below are delivery-wide and neither needs any sample regenerated: (1) add a realistic high-level task description as an extra file per sample, and (2) report the content and structural scores separately alongside the overall score. ───────────────────────────────────────
How to read this document (key terms)


* SA and MA — each task is run two ways: SA = single agent, MA = multi-agent (a coordinated swarm). Same task, same inputs, same budget; the intended difference is one agent vs many. The gap = MA score − SA score. Scores run 0 to 1.
* verify.py — the grader shipped with each sample. verifier_type is the config label declaring how it works: deterministic/executable (pure code), llm-judge (an AI model rates the output), or hybrid (both).
* Structural check vs content check — a structural check asks "is the output the right shape?" (file exists, valid schema, right row count, an id copied from an input file). A content check asks "is the work actually right?" — either an AI judge rating quality (rubric) or a comparison against ground truth (GT). Throughout this document content = rubric + GT.
* Freebie floor — the score a well-formed but hollow submission banks from structural checks alone, before doing any real work. Lower is better.
* Completion cliff — an arm scored near zero because it wrote nothing, not because its work was poor.
* decomposition.yaml — the pre-written orchestration plan shipped with each sample: a list of sub-tasks, each naming a role, its input files and its output path.

Cross-cutting feedback


A. The single-agent runs


A1. The 6 single-agent failures are not one uniform "completion cliff" — we traced each one through its execution log.


* 3 genuine capability cliffs (#1 MULTIWEAPON, #2 PATENT, #3 HAZMAT): SA tried to emit all deliverables in a single write and hit the 32K per-response output cap, producing 0 files. Choosing to one-shot a write that large is a real single-agent failure mode, and MA avoids it because each subagent writes its own smaller file. Worth knowing how the zero arises, though: OpenCode ends the run on the first finish_reason=length, so the truncation ends the episode rather than costing partial credit. PATENT stopped ~6.5 minutes in and HAZMAT at ~8% of budget, both with budget to spare, and PATENT's instruction explicitly rewards partial writes. The failure is genuine, but the 0.00 is harsher than the work was — so the PATENT and HAZMAT gaps overstate the multi-agent advantage by that margin. (MULTIWEAPON runs the other way: its SA score is spuriously inflated by a grading bug, so its gap is understated — see A2.) Nothing for you to action here; we're flagging it so the numbers get read correctly.
* 1 harness/infra artifact (#4 OHIO): SA was killed by an opencode auto-compaction followed by exit 1 (NonZeroAgentExitCodeError) after 5 steps and ~0.5% of its budget, with zero write attempts. It never got a fair run. Please re-run this sample — as delivered it is not a valid SA↔MA pair.
* 1 degenerate loop (#5 MOJ-OSS): SA looped on repo 1 of 70 and quit at ~15% of budget. A genuine robustness failure.
* 1 failed shortcut (#9 FEDERAL): SA attempted a template generator that failed, then exhausted ~96% of its timeout with no truncation. A genuine capability limit on a ~1.9M-token corpus.
A2. #1 MULTIWEAPON's two arms were graded by different verifier versions, and the SA score is an artefact. The SA arm was scored on 2026-07-22 against 250 checks; the MA arm on 2026-07-29 against the 426 checks that ship with this delivery. The two numbers are therefore not on the same scale.


SA's 0.204 is also not a partial result — it is entirely spurious. SA produced no output files at all (the verifier's own notes record balance_simulation_model.json, simulation_results.csv and output.json as missing), yet exactly 51 of its 250 checks scored 1.0, and every one of them is a ranking check (dossier_rank_correct_* ×50 plus dossiers_rank_correct_eq_all). No other check scored above zero, and 51 ÷ 250 = 0.204 — the whole score. Ranking can only be derived from the model file that was never written, so the older verifier passed those checks by default on absent input.


Please re-run both arms against the same shipped verifier before this pair is used. On the current verifier SA would score at or near 0, so the true gap is wider than the reported 0.61.


B. How the score is composed, and how hard the tasks are


A framing note first: we do not read any of this as the model gaming the grader. In all 14 samples the agent is doing what the instruction asks. Where the instruction and rubric only specify the shape of the output, a correctly-behaving agent produces the right shape and stops. The fix is on the specification side — weight substance, not shape.

One clarification on that 40%. It means rubric and ground truth together. A rule of "≥40% must be LLM-rubric" would wrongly penalise your best graders: PATENT is 0% rubric / 95% GT, SEC-EDGAR 2% / 74%, CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL 0% / 73% — deterministic ground truth is exactly what a strong grader looks like. Rubric and GT should be counted together as content, with GT preferred wherever it is feasible.


Note also that a structural check is not automatically a freebie. CA-DROUGHT is 58% structural but has a ~19% floor, because 12 of its 18 structural checks are gated on the row carrying a verbatim quote found in the real plan. That gating is the good pattern — see the two floor-killers below.


Reweight targets in this batch, in priority order: MULTIWEAPON (88% structural, no held-out ground truth at all), TEA-STALL (~60–65% floor), FEDERAL (~55%), TRIAL-REGISTRY (~50% — from volume thresholds, not from the oracle), MOJ-OSS (~42%), OHIO (~40%), PHANTOM (~40%).


Seven of the 14 samples sit at a ≥40% floor — considerably more than Batch-12, which had two. Two patterns already used in this batch drive the floor to ~0%, and we'd like to see them standardised:


1. An _eligible authorship gate — every per-record check is gated behind a deterministic authorship test (copy-density, word-order, source vocabulary) plus judge verdicts on whether the record is composed/specific/deep. A hollow or copied record then earns nothing on any check. Used well here in USGS and CIT.
2. Ground truth denominated over the whole corpus — per-item shares over the fixed corpus, requiring exact matches against frozen truth. Used well in PATENT (383 of 404 checks) and SEC-EDGAR (28 of 43).
B4. Where full ground truth is infeasible, hand-verify a subset and give it real weight.


Some graders credit values that are already in the agent's input files, so the check confirms the agent read a provided file rather than that it did the work: MULTIWEAPON (no held-out key at all — everything checked is in the weapon catalog the agent was handed), FEDERAL (every input is agent-visible; no hidden oracle at all), MOJ-OSS (4-repo oracle, URLs derivable), OHIO (raw filing JSON provided).


We understand a complete answer key is sometimes prohibitively expensive. We are not asking for full coverage — a small, hand-verified, held-out slice (a handful of rows or entities that are not in the inputs), carrying meaningful weight, is worth far more than a large key the agent was handed. MULTIWEAPON and FEDERAL are the priorities — neither has any held-out truth today.


B5. Task calibration — please leave headroom at the top.


7 of the 14 multi-agent runs scored ≥0.80: TEA-STALL 1.00, HAZMAT 0.98, CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL 0.91, FEDERAL 0.91, PATENT 0.87, TRIAL-REGISTRY 0.81, MULTIWEAPON 0.81. We'd like the strongest MA run on a task to land nearer 0.70–0.80, so the task still discriminates between a good run and an excellent one. TEA-STALL scoring a perfect 1.00 leaves nothing to measure at all.


To be clear: this is about the MA ceiling, not the gap. A large SA→MA gap is fine and is what we want to see — the concern is a task on which the multi-agent arm is already at 90–100%.


Two different causes, two different fixes:


* Score composition — MULTIWEAPON (floor ~85%), TEA-STALL (~60–65%), FEDERAL (~55%), TRIAL-REGISTRY (~50%): the reweight in B2/B3 will bring these down on its own. MULTIWEAPON's 0.81 is the least meaningful MA score in the batch — see that sample.
* Task difficulty — PATENT (0.87), HAZMAT (0.98) and CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL (0.91) sit on tight, low-floor deterministic graders, so those scores are legitimately earned. Please do not reweight those graders — they are the exemplars. There the fix is to make the task harder: more items, more traps and decoys, tighter tolerances.
C. Labels and provenance


C1. verifier_type — one missing, ten imprecise, three correct. We checked each declared label against whether verify.py actually calls a model:


* Missing (1): FEDERAL declares no verifier_type at all, though its grader does call a judge. Please add it.
* Imprecise (10): these declare llm-judge and do call a judge, so the label isn't false — but in most the deterministic half carries the majority of the score (MULTIWEAPON is ~88% deterministic, SEC-EDGAR 42 of 43 checks). hybrid would describe them better. Low priority.
* Correct (3): PATENT, HAZMAT and CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL declare executable and contain no model call anywhere — no change needed.
None of this changes a score; the label is what tooling and the team use to catalog tasks and to know which ones need a judge available at grade/train time.


C2. Please record the model used by each arm — 9 of 14 pairs ran on different models. SA is generally kimi-k2p6 or kimi-k2p7-code; MA is often the personal deployment accounts/bhanu-nalamadgu-7pl5/deployments/dghe4tfw (also ut1aj6qu; #13 TEA-STALL is k2p6 → k2p7-code). Different models across arms is acceptable to us as a recorded note — but it needs to be recorded in the delivered metadata, and we need to know what the personal deployments map to.


C3. Sub-bar notes (context, not defects). MA below 70% (#5 MOJ-OSS, #6 CA-DROUGHT, #10 USGS, #11 CIT, #12 PHANTOM) or a gap under 30pp (#6, #7, #13, #14) — these are genuine gaps on hard tasks, no action needed.
